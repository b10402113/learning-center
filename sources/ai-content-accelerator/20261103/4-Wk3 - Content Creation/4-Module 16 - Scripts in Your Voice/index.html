Module 16 - Scripts in Your Voice

AI slop is writing that sounds like every other AI, and people spot it and scroll on.

So teach your agent how you sound with /tone, a skill it reads every time it writes for you.

_

What You'll Learn

  • ✍️ Build /tone from your chats, or model a creator you admire, like Two Minute Papers

  • 🧹 Refine /tone with a humanizer: the AI tells it finds become things you never say

  • 🎤 Two skills, /hooks and /tone, turn an idea into a script you read out loud

    Prompts to Send Your Agent

    Build your /tone skill: your agent already knows how you talk from your chats, so send this:

    """Based on everything you know about me, create a tone of voice skill based on how I talk to you.

    1. First, read what you already have of me: our chats, your memory of me, my notes and files, and anything I've posted. Tell me in 2 or 3 lines what you found.

    2. Then ask me a few questions, one at a time, and wait for each answer: what I make content about, who it's for, and a line or two I've said or written that feels most like me. If we haven't talked much yet, interview me about how I normally speak instead, and build a first version we can refine as we go.

    3. Write down how I sound: the words I use a lot, how long my sentences are, how I open and close, my jokes and quirks, and the things I never say.

    4. Write 3 short sample lines in my voice and ask which ones sound like me. Fix the rules until I say yes.

    5. Save it as a skill called /tone (or a file you read first, if you can't make skills), and use it every time you write anything for me."""

    Model a creator you admire: your agent studies their transcripts, and tells you if one fails.

    """I like the content of [a creator I admire, for example Two Minute Papers, or two creators I'd like to mix]. Help me write in a tone inspired by theirs, then make it my own.

    1. Find their channel and get the transcripts of [their last 3] videos. If you can run commands, use yt-dlp to download the auto subtitles. If you can't get a transcript, tell me plainly which ones failed and why, and I'll paste it from the Show transcript button on YouTube.

    2. Study how they talk: how they open, how they explain hard things, the phrases they repeat, how long their sentences are, and how they close.

    3. Show me what you found in a few lines, with a short quote for each point.

    4. Ask me, one at a time, what I want to keep, what doesn't fit me, and what I make content about.

    5. Add it to my /tone skill as a style to lean on, not lines to copy. Never use their catchphrases or sentences as mine."""

    Refine /tone with the humanizer: it finds the AI tells, and you choose which ones go into /tone.

    """Install the humanizer skill from github.com/blader/humanizer and use it to refine my /tone skill. Read its README first and pick the install that fits our setup.

    1. Tell me in 3 or 4 plain lines what it checks for.

    2. Run it over a few things you've written for me lately (or my last few scripts), and list the AI tells it finds.

    3. Ask me about them one at a time: would I ever say this? Wait for each answer.

    4. Add the ones I'd never say to /tone as things I never say, so you stop writing them in the first place.

    5. Whenever a draft still sounds like AI later, run the humanizer again and update /tone the same way."""

    Write a script for an Instagram short: take an idea from your Week 2 list, then send this:

    """Write a script for an Instagram short, in my voice.

    1. Ask me these one at a time and wait for each answer: which of my ideas this is (in one sentence), who it's for, and what real result or example I have (never make one up).

    2. Use my /hooks skill to pick a hook shape and write 3 hooks in it. Tell me which one you'd pick and why, and let me choose.

    3. Write the script with my /tone skill, short enough for a reel, in short lines I can say out loud.

    4. Read it back as if I'm saying it out loud and cut any word I'd stumble on. If a line still sounds like AI, tell me, and add that pattern to /tone as something I never say.

    5. Show me the script."""

    Make your skills better every time: these are your baseline skills, so at the end of a session, send this:

    """Update my skills based on the feedback I gave you in this session.

    1. Look back over this session and list every change I asked for in my hooks and scripts, in a few short lines.

    2. For each one, tell me which skill it belongs in (/hooks or /tone) and the rule you'd add, in plain words.

    3. Ask me which ones to keep, and wait for my answer.

    4. Add the ones I keep to those skills, and show me what changed."""
