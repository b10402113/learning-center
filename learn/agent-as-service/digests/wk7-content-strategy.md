# L1 overview

- Jay's four levers of what actually works in content: hooks, consistency/volume, email capture, and the value exchange (selfless content).
- Hooks are the one thing to master: a great hook on mediocre content beats great content with a weak hook; the "God prompt" video with an orange-on-a-stick visual hook got 4.6M views via curiosity gap.
- Consistency beats polish — publish as much as possible, improve 1% each time; volume is key and no platform limits posting (Instant Bollywood posts 100×/day for 9.3B impressions).
- Collect emails early because social followers are rented space; Substack takes five minutes to set up; framework: post 2–3 times per day, batch on one day per week.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L36` — Module scope: what content actually works — hooks, consistency, collecting emails, and how much to post to build momentum; if you master just one thing in the content game, make it hooks.
- `#L37-L72` — A hook is the very first sentence, intro, or visual shown; that's what the algorithm cares about because a strong hook keeps people watching; beginners should not worry about polish or production quality.
- `#L73-L136` — Case study from a 2024 viral RoboNuggets video: Jay found a prompt going viral on Reddit (credited the Reddit user), tried it and found it revealing — powerful prompts were not prevalent yet, so he made a quick video sharing his experience.
- `#L137-L216` — Hook packaging lesson: instead of "I found a good prompt," he branded it "the God prompt for ChatGPT" to create curiosity; the verbal hook was "Did I just find the God prompt for ChatGPT? So I was rousing Reddit…"; the headline needs to be interesting and arresting.
- `#L217-L272` — Visual hook: the orange on a stick makes people curious about what it is and which pointer he uses, driving comments and watch-through; that curiosity gap carried the video to 4.6 million views.
- `#L273-L360` — Hook-vs-content truth: a great hook with poor content still outperforms great content with a weak hook, so hooks are the top skill — but aim for the combination of great hook AND great content, since content is what people follow you for over time (the shared prompt was genuinely useful, which is why virality stuck).
- `#L361-L448` — Strategy 2, consistency: don't worry about polish or cameras early; focus on publishing and talking about AI as much as possible per day/week; the space looks crowded but your specific context is unique (AI for real estate, creatives, or fitness influencers); ship, improve 1% each time — you start at level zero and level up.
- `#L449-L540` — Strategy 3, build your email list: platform followers live on rented space — three TikTok strikes can permanently ban the account and remove all followers; an email list is yours; a Google Sheets form can work, but the easiest free recommendation is Substack, which gives a frictionless landing page and takes about five minutes to set up.
- `#L541-L680` — How much to post: as soon as content gets steam, add an email capture device; volume is key and no platform limits posting as long as you aren't classified as a bot; warm up new accounts (tutorial linked) or start from an existing personal account, posting organically from native apps (use the TikTok app rather than going straight to Blotato auto-posting).
- `#L681-L796` — Volume examples: if you can post 10 organic videos per day, do that; RoboNuggets TikTok ran 2–3 posts/day via screen sharing; Instant Bollywood posts 100 times per day for 9.3 billion impressions and Instagram said it's fine — a drop in the ocean; community member Sabrina still posts 2–3 times per day and batches content on Saturdays as her content day, scheduling everything by batch.
- `#L797-L896` — The formula: more content equals more chances to be discovered, so don't hold back; framework: post 2–3 times per day as an intention, batch one day per week; combine great hooks + consistency in volume + always a value exchange — make content selfless, not selfish, thinking about the person on the other side of the screen.
- `#L897-L937` — Challenge: after watching, record something — point your phone at your screen, talk about one AI thing learned today, and post to TikTok or Instagram; every unpolished post teaches something; next module: productizing your offer to make it easy to sell.
