# L1 overview

- Jay walks the practical steps of making the practice real: pick a lane (operational vs creative, or "AI solutions practice" if unsure), choose a name, check domain and social-handle availability on Namechk, and register on Namecheap — budget roughly 30 minutes, rebrand later if needed.
- Domain guidance: owning the TLD (.com, .ai, .io, .co, .so, and the cheap, underused .solutions at ~$8/yr) elevates professionalism; if not ready to buy, host free on Netlify or Vercel subdomains (example: robolabsai.netlify.app). Namecheap's Beast Mode supports keyword combos, price filters, domain hacks, and pluralizing.
- Logo without a designer: generate on Looka (name → industry → style → colors → font; continue as guest, screenshot the result), then ask ChatGPT to remove the background and output a high-resolution transparent PNG; or use the AI logo prompt template with any image model (Jay suggests nano banana in Google Gemini). Key message: done beats perfect — a decent logo that exists beats the perfect logo you never create.
- Closes with the week-one milestone: share it to the community for accountability, feedback, and celebration; next week goes live with the website, business email, and first public announcement.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L101` — Module 3 intro and lane choice: by the end you'll have a branded identity ready to sell and talk about — "where it starts feeling real." It doesn't have to be perfect; you can always rebrand; the goal is "real enough to move forward." Words have meaning: what you call yourself and the agency shapes how clients see you and is the first thing they hear. First, have a semblance of your lane: operational (automating business processes), creative (automating content production), or both.
- `#L102-L261` — Name availability and Namechk: if unsure, "AI solutions practice" is broad enough to cover whatever you end up doing; rebrand once you find your niche. Start a list of names or use the prompt below, but you can't just pick any name — perception matters. Key consideration: top-level domain availability (is neocor.com free? are the LinkedIn/Instagram handles taken?), because owning the TLD (.com, .net, .io) elevates credentials and professionalism. Tool: namecheck/Namechk.com — type a name, search, see which TLDs are available (example: Neocor.com taken), and click "check user names" to instantly see which social platforms the username is still on. Advice: spend ~30 minutes, pick a name quickly and run with it — you can always change it later.
- `#L262-L441` — Modifiers, registrars, and free hosting: add modifiers when names are taken (e.g., "Bright AI"); professional alternative TLDs include .ai, .io, .co. Recommended registrar: Namecheap (simplest, what they use; search recommends available options like neocore.to). Namecheap's Beast Mode tab: type multiple keywords, filter by price range, apply domain hacks (hireme.com → hired.at/me, dropping the last vowel like Flickr; tool.com → tools.com), generate iterations across trusted TLDs (.ai, .io, .app, .so — example pick neocor.so at ~AUD 60/year ≈ 60 USD; .so is well established, e.g., Notion uses not.so). If expense is a blocker: free hosting with subdomains on Vercel and Netlify — example site robolabsai.netlify.app is fully built and interactive with client testimonials and a contact form; Netlify publishes your website to the web free; Vercel is only slightly more complex (e.g., neocor.vercel.app).
- `#L442-L573` — The .solutions trick and naming wrap-up: for a 4–5 letter practice name, the underrated TLD is .solutions — neocor.solutions likely available at ~12 AUD ("about $8 in freedom dollars"); pick a random four-letter brandable word (example: "Kiva", made up on the spot) and grab kiva.solutions — it already looks legitimate. Repeat the discipline: don't spend too much time; choose a domain, identify availability, secure it on Namecheap, or go free with Netlify/Vercel.
- `#L574-L824` — Logo: in the old world you'd hire a designer; instead generate something professional enough to go live fast. Recommended tool: Looka (looka.com) — type the practice name (kiva.solutions), pick industry (technology consulting), choose inspiring styles (minimalist), optional color palette (blue and gray scale), optional slogan (defer it), pick a font type, continue as guest; you get several neat logo ideas — all you need is minimalist and professional. Brand design kits are a premium feature; for just the logo, take a screenshot, send it to ChatGPT with "remove the background of this logo and output it as a high resolution PNG" — ChatGPT's image model can output transparent-background PNGs, downloadable as an asset for website and brand materials. Alternative: the template below the module gives an image-model prompt (Jay recommends nano banana in Google Gemini) for a fully AI-generated logo you can tweak. Key message: "done will always be perfect" — it doesn't have to be perfect now, it just needs to exist; a decent logo that exists beats the perfect logo you never create; upgrade later when revenue comes in; professional enough for a website and LinkedIn profile.
- `#L825-L885` — Milestone and next week: you've finished the first set of modules; complete the items on the week-one milestone page. The milestone is designed to be shared to the community — it boosts accountability, gets feedback from a larger audience in a no-risk manner, and lets you celebrate the first step. Recap: you understand the opportunity, downloaded the principles that carry you through the course, and have a real business identity. Next week goes live: set up your website, business email, and first public announcement to get the word out on your agency practice.
