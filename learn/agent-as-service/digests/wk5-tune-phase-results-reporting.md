# L1 overview

- Jay teaches the third delivery phase — the Tune: ongoing optimization after launch (adjust prompts/configurations against real-world performance, handle edge cases that only appear with real data, evaluate new models as they release faster and faster).
- Most engagements include a warranty/hypercare/tuning period of 2–4 weeks of free support; tuning is where retainers come from — systems break, models update, edge cases appear, and the builder is the best person to maintain it. Builds on the recurring-revenue and delivery-model framing from earlier modules.
- Two delivery models to stay clear about: systems-based (you hand the system over; involvement ends or becomes optional maintenance, e.g. a monthly check-in for X dollars, good for clients with technical capacity) vs. output-based (you own the system and deliver results like 30 videos/month or 50 qualified leads/week; they pay for results, you handle how).
- Results reporting is "not optional" and multiplies impact tenfold. Match the metric to the value type: time saved (hours × hourly rate), leads (count, quality, acquisition cost), content produced (assets, plus engagement metrics), revenue impact (attributable sales). Always put a dollar figure on it, even with estimates/industry averages. Three reasons to report: justifies cost, opens upsell conversations ("we automated X, maybe we can automate Y"), and creates referral ammunition (a specific one-liner like "$1,500 saved in one week"). Automate the reporting itself: a template that pulls tracked metrics, or a 2-minute Loom walkthrough as an under-promise/over-deliver move.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L96` — Module intro: tune = ongoing optimization + results reporting, "what gets you retainers, referrals and renewals." Post-launch work: adjust prompts/configurations on real-world performance, handle real-data edge cases, evaluate newly released models (release cycles keep shrinking). Depends on the initial agreement; most engagements have a warranty also called hypercare or tuning period — 2–4 weeks of help while the client uses the solution.
- `#L97-L144` — Why tuning matters commercially: this is where retainers come in — systems break, models update, edge cases appear, someone must maintain it, and since you built it you're the best candidate. It answers the recurring-revenue question: build and integrate are where the money is charged, but trust turns it into month-on-month always-on support. Recap of the two delivery models from earlier modules and why you must be clear which one you're under.
- `#L145-L276` — The two models in detail. Systems-based delivery: you build the automation/agent, hand it over, they own it; your involvement ends or becomes optional maintenance — offer a monthly check-in for X dollars; great for clients who want ownership and have some technical capacity but may need expertise support. Output-based: you own the system on your side and deliver the outputs (e.g. 30 videos per month, 50 qualified leads per week); they pay for results, you handle how it gets done; easier because the system stays with you and you keep improving output over time. One client can have multiple streams mixing both models; not knowing which model you're in makes expectation-setting much harder.
- `#L277-L364` — Results reporting: never skip it — handing over a "clean and done" solution wastes value; proving the value multiplies the build's impact tenfold. Always show the return on their investment. Package it in the tuning cadence: weekly or monthly meetings with a 1–2 slide results outline; weekly is common for high-touch clients (ads, videos, creatives) needing constant optimization; sometimes no meeting is needed — a one-pager or single slide is completely viable. Examples from previous engagements are in the resources below.
- `#L365-L560` — The "match metric to value type" framework with worked numbers. Automation → time saved: hours saved per week/month × average hourly rate of whoever did the job (e.g. 20 hours saved this month at $50/hour = $1,000 in value). Leads/customers → count, lead quality, and cost to acquire (45 leads this week at ~$20 each = $900 for the week). Content produced → number of assets (videos, posts, ads); if you also launch the material, engagement metrics (views, aggregate clicks) belong in the bucket. Revenue impact → attribute sales to the system (an automated follow-up sequence that closed five deals is quantifiable per deal). Style rule: this month the AI agent handled 200 customer inquiries which previously took ~50 hours of staff time; even industry-average estimates are fine (50 hours × $30/hour industry average ≈ $1,500 saved) — always attach a final absolute number so ROI is direct (paid $500 → 3x ROI).
- `#L561-L680` — Three reasons never to skip reporting: (1) justifies the cost — they see what they got for their money; (2) the reporting conversation opens upsells ("Oh, we automated X. Maybe we can also automate Y, which would save you even more"); (3) referral ammunition — when peers ask about you, the client shares a concrete one-liner ("saved us $1,500 in just one week"). Positioning: this separates commodity vendors from trusted partners. Automate it: if you track metrics, build a template that pulls the data automatically; even without a requested meeting, record a 2-minute Loom walkthrough of how the system is doing — an under-promise/over-deliver move that gives them the feedback loop unprompted.
- `#L681-L693` — Summary of the tuning phase: optimize on an ongoing basis, charge for maintenance if you can, and always report on the results. Sign-off to the next module.
