# L1 overview

- Jay's walkthrough of getting an AI practice's website live and a business email set up. Core mindset: the site doesn't need to be perfect, it just needs to exist — the goal is a shareable URL.
- Recommends Carrd.co (free, simplest one-pager) or Framer.com (professional templates, ~$15 USD/month for a custom domain), and narrows the site down to three components: tagline, what you do, and a way to reach you.
- Business email via Google Workspace: a domain email elevates the practice and what you can charge; he recommends setting up two addresses (personal + team@) so the practice looks like it has a team behind it.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L85` — Intro and mindset: name, domain, and logo are already done from earlier modules; now "make it real." The site "doesn't need to be perfect, it just needs to exist" because you can always iterate later; the goal is a shareable URL to point people at. Simple is fine — you're not trying to win design awards. A separate lesson exists for building a custom site yourself, but most people should pick a template-based platform.
- `#L86-L272` — Platform recommendations with a live demo: Carrd.co is "literally the simplest way" to build a one-page site, with free templates you can launch the same day. For more professionalism, Framer.com (used by the instructor, has a monthly fee) has prebuilt templates per service type — he demos typing "tech agency," filtering free templates, previewing, clicking "use for free," editing fields (headline example: "AI solutions for every business"), hiding unneeded sections via right-click, checking tablet/mobile previews, and hitting publish to get a live URL you can send to your phone.
- `#L273-L352` — Domain options on Framer: a free framer.website subdomain works but "is not as professional" as a proper TLD; connecting an existing domain requires a paid plan — Framer Basic is 20 AUD, roughly 15 USD per month — which he calls a good option given how professional and easily editable the result is.
- `#L353-L528` — The three components to focus on (since templates come prebuilt): (1) the title/tagline — your hook and the first thing visitors read; examples of sharpening: "AI solutions for content businesses" or "helping companies automate what slows them down"; put a specific industry in the headline if you have one. (2) What you do — frame it as outcome/benefit-based; the RoboLabs example copy is "building AI agents that let you save time, reach more people, and create more," which he explicitly says you can borrow. Start simple and get more specific as you learn what resonates. (3) A way to reach you — contact form, email, or calendar link, made very visible ("let's talk" button at top plus in-page buttons linked to a contact form tied to the business email). That's enough for now; testimonials, case studies, and deeper detail can come later.
- `#L529-L765` — Business email: starting with Gmail "looks amateur," while name@yourbrand.com elevates the practice and the amount you can charge; it builds trust, separates work from personal accounts, and signals you're serious. Recommends Google Workspace: one business email for $4 the first month, ~$8.40/month after; you get the Google ecosystem (Gmail, Calendar, Drive) on your own domain; it scales — easy to add users when you expand the team and to set up emails for AI agents that act like employees. Recommended setup is two emails: a personal one (e.g. J@yourdomain.com) and a team email (team@yourdomain.com) used as the website contact, which gives visitors the sense the practice has a team behind it even if it's just you plus AI agents. Closes with the module's two deliverables: a live website where people can contact you and a professional email, and a bridge to the next module on telling people about it.
