# L1 overview

- Course finale on leverage: building systems where outputs exceed inputs, so an hour of work today produces more than an hour of value over time — the reason some people get richer isn't working harder but building leverage (Archimedes: "give me a lever long enough and I shall move the world").
- The three C's of leverage: Capital (money — oldest and most traditional, hires people and buys assets, but least accessible), Code (software — build once, distribute forever, near-zero marginal cost per customer, and now permissioned by AI since you only need to direct agents rather than know Python or C++), and Content (media — build once, distribute forever; permissionless, so the lever Jay recommends starting with; often even more important than code or capital because code without distribution fails).
- Path from services to scale: selling skills as a service is the right start — it teaches what people actually pay for, puts you in the arena, and builds credentials, relationships, testimonials, and case studies; then share what you learn to attract inbound via content, and package your knowledge into productized offers. Getting to 10K MRR is a matter of time; getting to 100K MRR requires leverage and the three C's — a different game.
- Recommended reading list (six books with one-line theses): The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (leverage and wealth framework), Alex Hormozi's $100M Series ($100M Offers/Leads/Money Models — scaling tactics), 4,000 Weeks by Oliver Burkeman (life is ~4,000 weeks; at 30 you've used ~1,500), Noah Kagan's Million Dollar Weekend (get first customers in a weekend to validate), The Lean Startup, and Company of One (small teams, even one person, building wealth with AI). Closing message: you have the frameworks, templates, and proven path — the rest is execution and reps; the classroom is great but the community is the university; go build something.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L172` — Final module scope and the definition of leverage: deeply covering leverage, the three C's in context, and the path from services to scaling. Leverage = building systems where your outputs exceed your inputs — an hour of work today leads to more than an hour of value over time; this is why some people get richer than others (not working harder, building leverage). Archimedes quote: "give me a lever long enough and I shall move the world"; creating more leverage in your work and environment should be a primary goal.
- `#L174-L272` — The first C, capital: the traditional and oldest form of leverage — money lets you hire people, purchase machines, and buy assets; during the industrial revolution money meant you could set up factories. But it's the least accessible to most people; the good news is it used to be the only lever.
- `#L274-L312` — The second C, code: writing software — build it once and distribute it forever; every technology you use is mostly just code, written text. This is why AI is so powerful: you no longer need to understand Python or C++ — you just need to direct agents properly to write and build software you create once, test, distribute forever, and keep improving. The marginal cost of one new customer is literally zero, so there's infinite scale. But code alone fails without distribution: if you can't advertise or market it, no one knows the tool exists and you get no leverage.
- `#L314-L396` — The third C, content: often even more important than code or capital, and the lever Jay recommends starting with because it is permissionless. Create content once, distribute forever — a video made today can generate value for years. The combination of capital, code, and content is how you scale beyond trading time for money and build leverage.
- `#L398-L500` — Your journey from services: whether you trade time building solutions for clients or for your employer, services are the right place to start — selling skills as a service teaches what people actually pay for, puts you in the arena instead of theorizing, and builds credentials, relationships, testimonials, and case studies that grow your network. Over time, layer leverage on top: offer agents as a service, learn what works, get paid; share what you've learned to attract inbound via content and build a following; once that flywheel turns, package your knowledge into something scalable and productized so you serve multiple people consistently without much variation. If this course is about the first 10K MRR, following the frameworks makes it a matter of time — but 100K MRR is completely different: you need leverage and the three C's, a different game entirely.
- `#L502-L568` — Recommended books, "a lot of these questions have already been figured out": The Almanack of Naval Ravikant — the framework for achieving leverage and wealth plus how to live your life; Alex Hormozi's $100M series — Offers, Leads, and now the Money Models book — tactics applicable to any niche; 4,000 Weeks — average human life is about 4,000 weeks, so at 30 you've already used roughly 1,500, which spurs action; Noah Kagan's Million Dollar Weekend — always think about getting your first street customers in a weekend to validate your idea, the most important thing when launching products; adjacent to that, The Lean Startup and Company of One — increasingly important as AI lets people build small teams, even one person, and build wealth for themselves. Method: screenshot the list, have your AI summarize the books first, then grab an ebook or audiobook for the ones that resonate.
- `#L570-L629` — Course close: you've completed the course — you have frameworks, templates, a proven path, and you know how to find clients, close deals, deliver results, and grow; the rest is execution and reps, and the more you do, the better you get. Hitting your first 10K MRR is just the start; what comes after is up to you. Share wins in the community, ask questions, help others — the structured classroom is great but the community is like university: lessons add value, but you multiply the value by interacting with others. Key message: go build something and I'll see you on the other side.
