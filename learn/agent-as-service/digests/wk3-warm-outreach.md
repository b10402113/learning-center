# L1 overview

- Jay's warm outreach lesson: contacting people who already know you is the fastest (not fanciest or most scalable) path to the first client, because existing trust halves the battle.
- Who counts as warm: friends/family, former colleagues, LinkedIn loose connections, old clients, vendors — the bar is simply "anyone who would recognize your name."
- Start in the industry you already know (his first customers were Unilever marketing peers): you get network plus innate domain knowledge, and your Week-2 website is what vouches for you when referrals check you out.
- Workflow: export contacts from phone/Google/LinkedIn into CSVs, send manual personalized DMs first (no full automation yet), keep messages short with a "know anyone who might benefit?" ask, follow up after 3–5 days, cap at two follow-ups, and move referrals toward a 10-minute discovery call.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L61` — Definition and why it works: warm outreach = reaching people who already know you; fastest path because existing trust means no need to prove you're real; speed matters most right now for the first win.
- `#L62-L140` — Who counts as warm: friends and family (even if they don't need it, they know people who do), former colleagues who saw how you deliver, LinkedIn loose connections from events, previous clients, vendors and partners; the bar is "anyone who would recognize your name," and it's lower than you think.
- `#L141-L284` — Start where you are: your first client will likely come from your existing world, not a funnel or cold campaign; example — his first customers were marketing peers from Unilever because he had both the relationship and domain knowledge in marketing, not dentists or lawyers; apply this to real estate, finance, etc.; AI agents cut across every vertical; having the Week-2 website ready is key because referrals will check it, and a polished site cements legitimacy and sells for you.
- `#L285-L483` — Building the list and the manual-first rule: step one is making a list of everyone you know — export phone contacts to a spreadsheet, plus LinkedIn and email as the next sources; even though warm outreach can be partly automated, start with manual sendouts for the top 10–20 people to learn what works; automate message ideas but not the whole flow; two questions for the module: how to get contacts in bulk, and what to send.
- `#L484-L669` — Channel-by-channel export how-tos: get started today by messaging the top 5–10 people who come to mind; iPhone "export contact" app and Android equivalent for CSV backup; contacts.google.com lists and exports your Gmail contacts (he has 1,500+ from his career); LinkedIn export via Settings & Privacy → Data Privacy → download your data archive; the result is CSV/Excel lists of potential contacts.
- `#L670-L817` — The message and follow-ups: short direct messages work best — long messages feel salesy; say you've started an AI solutions practice and ask "do you know anyone who might benefit?" so it reads as consulting, not pitching (asking them directly triggers the being-sold-to feeling); most non-replies are busyness, so follow up after 3–5 days with a "bumping this up" note — most results come from follow-ups; cap at two follow-ups; when someone offers an intro, make it easy (email intro or you initiate); if they want it themselves, get curious, don't sell, and set a 10-minute discovery call — deals close in conversations, not DMs.
- `#L818-L893` — Closing action: warm outreach is free, fast, and has one of the highest conversion rates you can imagine; make the list and send at least 10 messages this week to start conversations and surface opportunities already in your network.
