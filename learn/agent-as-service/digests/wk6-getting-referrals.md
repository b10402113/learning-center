# L1 overview

- Week 6 module 2: why referrals are your most valuable source of new clients, backed by data (9 out of 10 B2B buyers say purchasing decisions are influenced by word of mouth; 82% of sales leaders say referrals give their highest-quality leads).
- Explains the mechanics — trust transfer from a happy client to their industry peers — and why referrals compound: one happy client leads to two or three more in the same industry until demand is no longer your problem.
- Three principles for getting referrals: ask directly, time it right after a win, and make it easy — with usable scripts for each, tying back to Week 5's results reports as forwardable proof.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L80` — Intro and the data case: the instructor has been in the business over a decade and referrals are the best way to expand "bar none." 9/10 B2B buyers say word of mouth influences purchasing decisions; 82% of sales leaders say referrals produce their highest-quality leads. For big-ticket, operationally significant systems, a polished website or optimized cold outreach isn't enough — you need to capture trust, and the fastest way is being referred by a party the buyer already trusts: if you did good work for business A, its peer business B will likely trust you more simply because you already work with their peer.
- `#L81-L160` — Why the numbers look that way: industries are connected. Within a vertical or niche (e.g. real estate), local businesses have huge networks — they know each other, attend the same events, and face the same problems, so performing well for one gets you referred. All big gains come from compounding: one happy client leads to two or three more in the same industry, and it compounds as you expand — soon you're constrained on supply, not demand, "which is always a better problem to have."
- `#L161-L245` — Tie-back to Week 5: the results report from the delivery module is where referrals start. Clear data points — e.g. saving the client 20 hours per week, or tripling their lead response — are talking points; a proper slide pack illustrating those points is a key artifact the client can forward to peers or share with a wider group.
- `#L246-L324` — Human nature: people like sharing their own successes, so a happy client will mention you at industry events. Resource reports are easy to share, so when a peer asks "do you know anyone who does AI stuff," the client has something specific to say — e.g. "this person helped us save 20 hours a week on follow-ups, so we didn't need to hire for that role." A report means the client knows exactly what to say about you.
- `#L325-L427` — Three principles. (1) Ask directly — don't wait for referrals to happen; best time is after a win, e.g. "I'm glad this is working well. Do you know anyone else who might benefit from something similar?" — asked when perceived value is highest; make it part of the agenda after delivering or launching an agentic automation, and seed the idea casually: "We're actually looking for ways to offer this to more people because it's quite transformative" — even with nobody top of mind, the idea is planted for later. (2) Time it right — usually right after a win.
- `#L428-L569` — (3) Make it easy — don't make the client do the work; referring you should be the easiest thing they do all week: "If anyone comes to mind, feel free to intro us via email and I'll take it from there." The resource report and slide pack make forwarding frictionless. Referrals work because you arrive pre-sold — the person trusts you before you've even spoken. The formula: deliver results first, give talking points (specific numbers, forwardable artifacts), ask at the moment of highest win, and let the client just hand over a contact. Next module: proof and presence — testimonials, showing up, building credibility to get entrenched in your space.
