# L1 overview

- Week 6 module 3: a collection of tips that quietly compound your credibility — systematizing testimonials and case studies, and being present in your network (events, meetups, conferences) so you attract more clients in your field.
- Core testimonial advice: never leave them to chance; build the ask into your delivery process, ideally capturing feedback during the delivery call itself and clipping it.
- Core presence advice: the people who get opportunities are the ones who are present and remembered; speaking at even small stages builds expert credibility, and the bar is lower than you think.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L80` — Intro: tips for building credibility, systematizing testimonials, and being present in your network. First piece: testimonials and case studies — the key advice is to always systematize them, not leave them to chance. Build the ask into the process: when the project is done and delivered, ask for feedback; depending on the relationship, ask for a quick message or short video to use as a testimonial.
- `#L81-L160` — Capture testimonials then and there: keep the delivery meeting recording on and ask the client questions in that same meeting — whether they think the solution will help and how — then clip their reply as the testimonial. You can also ask afterward for permission to post it on your website or demo reels; the key is making it frictionless so you get the testimonial while still mid-delivery.
- `#L161-L260` — Ask at the point of highest value (callback to the referrals module): when the project goes live and the client sees agents automating work that used to take hours, that's the moment to ask both for referrals and for their thoughts on how it helps their day-to-day. Keep the ask simple: "would you mind sharing a few sentences about what we worked on and how it helped."
- `#L261-L340` — Publish testimonials on your website — builds credibility and works for you 24/7. Display format: client photo, their verbatim quotes, name and designation. Using tools like Antigravity or Claude Code, you can feed captured testimonial videos to an agent that summarizes them into quotes for the website. The habit to build: every project, one testimonial request — systematize it because it gives leverage down the line.
- `#L341-L420` — Second piece: show up. Be present in your network — attend industry events, meetups, and conferences. The people who get opportunities aren't always the best; they're the ones who are present and remembered. AI's rise means more in-person meetups and events are likely. Use the prompt in the resources section with your LLM or agent to find events in your area and industry — there are more than you think. Example: the instructor had OpenClaw (his assigned agent) scout the web for events in his niche and produce a table tracking upcoming events, locations (partial to Sydney), relevance, and next steps; take a screenshot and use the prompt to find your equivalent.
- `#L421-L500` — You don't need to speak at these events — just being there is valuable: meet people, have conversations, be known. To level up, apply to speak: speaking positions you as an expert, and even small stages build credibility. The bar is lower than you think — many speakers at local events are less advanced than you; they just applied. Example: TechHouse in Sydney this March — some speakers are legitimately successful (Jeremy, founder of finder.com, a multi-million-dollar Australian brand), but others are in their early twenties.
- `#L501-L645` — Example continued: Ishita, founder of "steady self," which is only a waitlist for an app likely built on a vibe-coding platform, yet she books speaking engagements that add credential to her knowledge even before launch. Side benefit: get photos of yourself speaking (or have a friend take them) for your LinkedIn profile photo and website — instant legitimacy, because of the inherent perception that being on stage means more credibility; you can do this even without a successful company or website yet. At minimum: a photo at a conference on stage or networking adds legitimacy — and in a field where perception and status play a role in business dealings, that boosts confidence in your practice. Summary: credibility compounds — systematize testimonials, show up in your network, get on stages when confident; it stacks over time.
