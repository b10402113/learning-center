# L1 overview

- Week 6 module 1: vertical expansion — once you have your first client, go deeper with the clients you already serve before chasing new ones. Covers upsells, add-ons, and retainers.
- Three reasons it works: existing clients already trust you, you already understand their business, and the sales cycle shrinks or disappears (no cold outreach, sometimes not even a pitch deck).
- Teaches the "adjacent problems" pattern for finding upsells, defines three retainer types (maintenance, growth, advisory), and ends with the key message: go vertical first before expanding horizontally.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L130` — Intro to vertical expansion: upsells, add-ons, retainers. The instinct after landing client X is to find clients Y and Z, but the smarter move is going deeper with existing clients. Three reasons: (1) they already trust you — you've delivered work and proven you're legitimate; (2) you already understand their business and have a working relationship with all the undefinable qualities that make collaboration smoother; (3) most importantly, there's no sales cycle — the DOTS framework (discovery, offer, tailoring, seal the deal) compresses dramatically; sometimes not even a pitch deck, just a conversation about what else you can offer.
- `#L131-L284` — Concrete expansion example: the instructor's Sydney agency built digital apps including PAM, a wayfinding app for places (mall maps). Beyond developing digital products, they noticed PAM wanted leads to sell its technology to, so they expanded their offering to include creative assets — easy because trust and business knowledge already existed. Adjacent-problem examples for agent builders: if you built an agent that finds and contacts leads, ask to also automate its follow-up; if your content automation works for YouTube Shorts, upsell to TikTok and Instagram; while streamlining operations you inevitably spot other wasted time, which is an upsell opportunity.
- `#L285-L394` — The pattern: look for adjacent problems you can solve. Current tools make this cheap — open Claude Code or Antigravity (or any agent platform), provide a brief and well-constructed background of what you want to help the client with, and it often signals the right solution. Transition to retainers: charging an ongoing amount in exchange for a service, with three forms depending on the client model.
- `#L395-L500` — Three retainer types: (1) maintenance retainer — monthly fee to maintain what you built (updates, fixes, monitoring); (2) growth retainer — monthly budget for ongoing improvements and new features, e.g. expanding YouTube content automation into TikTok and Instagram; (3) advisory retainer — regular calls advising on AI strategy, especially valuable because business owners lack the time to become experts on the latest AI tools; some pay a monthly fee just for expert presentations on which tools and opportunities fit their business, common in corporate environments.
- `#L501-L677` — Advisory value scaled up: McKinsey/BCG get paid millions essentially for PowerPoint presentations seeding AI ideas to top leadership; a high-status agency delivering ideas is worth it if one meeting gives a 5% incremental to a billion-dollar business. Scale that down: translating your AI expertise and technical skill-building into language business owners understand is chargeable if it saves them time or makes money. Key message: go vertical first before expanding horizontally — become a better steward for existing clients before chasing new ones.
