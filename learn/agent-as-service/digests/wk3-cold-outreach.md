# L1 overview

- Jay's cold outreach lesson: messaging strangers is harder than warm outreach with lower response rates, but it scales beyond your network — top earners in services run a cold machine that produces new conversations weekly.
- Same two questions as warm outreach: where to find leads (Apollo.io's 275M+ contacts with a free tier; Hunter.io for per-domain email finding) and what/how to send (never your personal Gmail — use Instantly.ai with warmed-up or purchasable pre-warmed accounts).
- Message principles: 3–5 sentences max, lead with value (free SEO report, homepage mockup, content audit — the Amplify Sydney 9-page SEMrush audit example), expect 1–5% response rates and treat it as math (2% × 200 sends = 4 conversations).
- Follow-ups drive 60–70% of positive replies; wait 3–5 days, cap at 2–3, don't sell or flatter in the first message; the formula is specific target + short message + upfront value + follow-up = conversations.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L121` — Definition and why cold matters: cold = complete strangers who never heard of you; harder with lower response rates but essential because your warm list runs out while the whole market opens up; the highest earners don't rely on warm networks alone; content is a future-week topic.
- `#L122-L220` — Specificity wins: "reach out to businesses" is not a plan — start with an industry you understand (real estate example: you know their pain points, language, workflows); identify the decision maker (owner in small business, department head in enterprise) because emailing the intern wastes time.
- `#L221-L388` — Finding leads: Apollo.io, the market leader with 275M+ contacts searchable by title, industry, company size, location, with a free tier; demo — searching "marketing directors at companies with 5–20 employees in the US" returns names, titles, companies, emails, phone numbers in seconds; Hunter.io as the alternative — an email finder/verifier for a target domain like acme.com, useful for specific companies but second choice since first-client hunting favors volume.
- `#L389-L534` — Sending infrastructure: never send cold email from personal Gmail or your company email — blasting 50 unreplied emails/day tanks your sender reputation and can land even personal mail in spam; use a cold email tool (Lemlist, Smartly mentioned) — Instantly.ai recommended for ease plus reliability, with a good free trial; it warms up your email address over a week and even sells pre-warmed accounts; demo of adding email accounts (connect Google Workspace, let Instantly set up emails, or buy pre-warmed ones like the reframeworkx.org example giving 5–6 ready accounts, optionally redirecting to your real website).
- `#L535-L765` — Campaign setup and agent automation: create campaigns under the campaign tab by uploading a lead CSV and writing sequences; instead of filling it manually, use the skill kit below and give it to an AI agent (Claude Code, Antigravity) — Instantly's API lets the agent write subject lines, copy, and dynamic values and run campaigns on your behalf.
- `#L766-L929` — Message principles 1 and 2: keep it short — nobody reads long cold messages, 3–5 sentences max, first sentence states why you're writing (templates below); always lead with value — "I do AI automation, interested?" gets ignored, while "I found your website could be better — here's the revised design" gets attention; make it about them, not your story or qualifications.
- `#L930-L1142` — Value-first playbook and volume math: concrete value leads — SEO report, homepage mockup ("built a quick mockup of how your homepage could convert better, want to see it?"), Google Maps listing photo audit, lead-capture form optimization; case study — Amplify, a digital partner in Sydney, got a free 9-page SEO audit (organic search, backlinks, site usability) generated via SEMrush, offered with a 15-minute walkthrough call, repeated across hundreds of websites; the pattern is doing a little work upfront (or automating it) so strangers get something useful; expect 1–5% response rates — people who say cold email doesn't work sent 15 messages and quit; at 2%, 50 messages = 1 conversation, so send 200 and get 4, which is why Instantly automates hundreds of emails weekly.
- `#L1143-L1389` — Follow-ups and closing formula: most responses come from follow-ups, not the first message — 60–70% of positive replies come from a follow-up; wait 3–5 days (next-day feels desperate), keep it short, cap at 2–3 follow-ups; don't pitch hard — you're starting a conversation, and the moment someone feels sold to their guard goes up; no fake flattery ("love your company") — people see through it instantly, so be honest about why you're reaching out; final takeaway formula: specific target + short message + value upfront + follow-up = conversations, no tricks, just a consistent process; this week's action — identify at least 20 potential clients, send cold messages, track results; "don't aim for perfect messages, aim for sent messages."
