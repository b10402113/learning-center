# L1 overview

- The shift from custom work to productized packages — offers that are easier to sell, price, deliver, and scale — following the leverage journey: services → content → products.
- Case studies: Alex from Ozzy (Gym Launch blueprint) and Pieter Levels (audience first, then products like PhotoAI.com); exceptions like Higgs Field and Arcads bought attention with influencer payouts and ads instead.
- Custom work asks "what do you need?"; productized says "here's what I offer" — named, templated, set-price packages (e.g. Nexus Labs Lead Follow-up System at $3,000 with 5-minute lead-response email sequences).
- Repeatable path: mine past projects (client or employment) for patterns, name the package, price value-based with high anchoring, and use agent prompts to package workspaces into clean reusable templates.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L52` — Module goal: shift from custom work to productized packages that are easier to sell, deliver, and scale; the usual journey starts as a professional selling services (employed and specializing, or working with clients as in previous weeks), sharpening one skill.
- `#L53-L112` — To expand beyond services, Jay recommends creating content first: it builds leverage, gets you known, captures an audience, and builds trust; RoboNuggets followed this exact framework; the third component, when ready, is productizing the offer.
- `#L113-L160` — When to productize: you've served multiple clients, the solution is fixed with only a few iterations, and today's tools make creating an application/service/product easy; the pattern repeats — Jay calls him "Alex from Ozzy" (Alex Hormozi): Gym Launch grew from selling services to gyms into a step-by-step blueprint with templates and sample ads.
- `#L161-L228` — Second example, Peter Levels (transcribed "Peter Levels"): built an audience by showcasing and writing about his work on Twitter, then went from web development services → content → products across verticals like PhotoAI.com (improves photos) and interior-design ideation sites, likely powered by Nano Banana 2 / Nano Banana Pro under the hood; none would have that customer base without his following — that is the power of content.
- `#L229-L288` — Chart your journey: start with services to sharpen skills, but post content meanwhile so that when the product is ready you already have a captured market that listens; exceptions exist (companies without creator followings) but they invested heavily in marketing.
- `#L289-L344` — Exceptions in the AI space: Higgs Field and Arcads grew without content followings by paying AI influencers and running ads; for an individual or small business that usually isn't an option; the core is leverage — an hour of input should yield outsized returns, ideally commensurate to hundreds of hours of output.
- `#L345-L424` — Custom work vs productized packages: custom means every project differs — scoped from scratch, coded individually, built unique — which is important for the first few clients to spot emerging patterns; a productized offer is a defined solution at a set price, the same deliverable and templated approach for different clients.
- `#L425-L472` — Core difference phrasing: custom asks "what do you need?"; productized says "here's what I offer"; packages are specific, concrete, and clear — give the package a name, e.g. the Nexus Labs Lead Follow-up System at $3,000 including automated email sequences that respond to leads within five minutes.
- `#L473-L532` — Packaging rule: always give it a name and template it; the classroom's lesson names (Creative Cloner AI agent, Cinematic Adverse agent) each embody a productized package; the RoboNuggets community itself is a productized offer — a small team serving thousands automatically, still doing custom solutions but increasingly building agents at a set price.
- `#L533-L608` — Think about your niche: what common patterns does your industry keep asking for? Package it, name it, and give it clear instructions so the next sale is ready-made; three reasons productizing works — easier to sell (prospects immediately understand what they get), easier to price (you know exactly what you deliver; pricing is consistent), easier to deliver (you've done it before, have a process, and get faster each time).
- `#L609-L656` — Productizing also positions you as a specialist instead of a generalist; to find your offer, look at past projects — what you built and what got results; employment experience counts as a rich source too.
- `#L657-L692` — Find a pattern: something you've done multiple times or could repeat, where a set of actions produces a specific deliverable — the more specific the better; then choose a price based on the value provided and your time; start somewhere and adjust (a pricing guide prompt is linked below the video).
- `#L693-L744` — First final point: reuse components — for every client build ask "can this be used for another customer?"; get into the mindset of building templates, frameworks, and processes that apply again; each project makes you faster so you charge the same price in less time, and eventually you may deliver in half the time at the same price.
- `#L745-L828` — Concrete packaging workflow: take an automation like the lead-capture/outreach system built earlier, add Antigravity or Cloud Code to package it into a template — give the agent a command so it asks clarifying questions and locks the spec before building; the detailed one-shot prompt (shown on screen, transcribed in the course page) handles packaging and templating, keeps keys private, and cleans everything so you can share with confidence; this is how RoboNuggets packages templates for hundreds of members daily.
- `#L829-L941` — Pricing recap: price value-based — think what it's worth to them, not what it costs you in time and effort; anchor high and adjust down based on the client's spending capacity rather than starting low and raising prices; whatever price you pick, optimize as you sell more; summary: for more leverage, go from custom work to productized packages — easier to sell, price, and deliver; the final week brings everything together and covers what comes next after establishing the AI agents practice.
