# L1 overview

- Jay's Wk4 module 1: the internal DOTS framework (Discovery, Offer, Tailor, Seal) for turning interested conversations into paying clients — no salesiness required, just understanding the problem and showing you can solve it.
- Discovery: a 15–30 minute call where your only role is to ask questions and listen (not pitch), using the 5WH framework (what, who, where, when, why, how); relationships, not technical competence or model choice, are what get you far.
- Offer: sent asynchronously as a slide pack/PDF — firm intro (because decks get forwarded), an AI-agents explainer, then the offer itself; two packaging modes: systems-based engagements use the BIT framework (Build, Integrate, Tune), deliverable-based engagements quote output directly.
- Tailor: adjust the proposal to client feedback (sometimes just a scope-clarifying email); Seal: written email confirmation is usually legally binding, but DocuSign/PandaDoc make signing easy — never start work without a contract or you invite scope creep and non-payment.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L56` — Week 4 module 1 framing: you've done outreach and have interested parties; this module is turning conversations into clients without being salesy — "understanding their problem and showing that you can solve it"; the internal DOTS framework is the general process to follow per client.
- `#L57-L152` — DOTS defined letter by letter: D = Discovery (learn the client's situation, problem, needs); O = Offer (the proposal: exactly what you'll do, deliverables, how long, how much); T = Tailor (adjust the offer based on feedback toward a mutually beneficial contract); S = Seal (final yes, contract, collect payment); "discovery, offer, tailor and seal."
- `#L153-L460` — Discovery phase in depth: a discovery call runs 15–30 minutes (even 15 is fine; an hour only for big corporates); your role is to ask questions and listen, not pitch; use the meeting document template and AI note-takers (Fathom, Fireflies — also reinforces your AI-practitioner credential); for larger teams/enterprise bring a 1–2 slide intro deck prepared from previous weeks; confidence is key, keep it simple; use the 5WH framework — what is their goal (more customers, leads, an operational automation), who do they impact (their ICP), where are they now, when do they want it achieved, why are they doing it, and any how ideas; applies to creative agent work and ops automation alike; let them talk, take notes, build the relationship — client relationships matter more than technical competence or the latest AI models for business clients; close the call with a quick summary and say you'll send something offline.
- `#L461-L1180` — Offer phase: proposals go out asynchronously after the call as a slide pack, document, or clean PDF by email; recommended structure — 1–2 slides introducing your firm because the deck gets forwarded inside the client's team and to referred prospects, so you're "properly introduced every time"; example: the RoboLabs company snapshot PDF with a who-we-are note, a slide explaining what AI agents are (still a new concept to most), and a benefits slide translating jargon into the three core benefits — save time, reach more people, create more output — plus example clients; the meat is the offer: a slide framing the problem from discovery and a slide on your process; two packaging options — systems-based engagements (selling the system itself, key question "how do I make this AI agent workflow part of their process?", hand over the build and you're left with tuning/support) vs deliverable-based engagements (common in creative work like UGC or clips, you sell the output itself, no client integration needed); for custom builds present the BIT framework: Build (create the agent in a staging environment — your own laptop if solo — ending in milestone 1 where you demo it; communicate that phase 1 is real work that affects pricing), Integrate (connect the agent to their systems — Notion content calendar, Postgres SQL — usually via logins/credentials, plus robust testing, with one month of free tuning support included), Tune (ongoing retainer; usually ~80% of what you charge); for deliverable work just quote output, e.g. a real quote declaring the number of unique Facebook/Instagram posts over a two-week trial plus adjacent work (boosting, brand engagement, launching ads); summary: put discovery learnings in a standard framework, send in a formal email, offer a walkthrough call or email Q&A.
- `#L1181-L1320` — Tailor phase: expect feedback after they read the proposal; it's a back-and-forth — listen to what they need changed and adjust if it makes sense; keep it tight, you don't need to redo the whole proposal (often a quick email clarifying scope or adjusting a line item); if no changes are needed, move straight to the contract (covered in module 3); the clothing analogy: a standard cut like BIT is the template, but the client should feel it's made for them — crucial for the relationship.
- `#L1321-L1525` — Seal phase: get the final yes, sign, collect payment; a written email confirmation is usually more than enough to be legally binding depending on location; DocuSign, PandaDoc, or a simple PDF signature make it official — make it easy for them to sign; warning for beginners: starting work without a written contract is a key mistake — undefined scope means you may not get paid, and you get scope creep (client demands more and more until you burn out); locking scope protects both sides, especially what's included and what's not; after signing, collect the first installment before any work; recap of the whole DOTS flow with the "10 videos for a thousand bucks" deliverable example vs a BIT-illustrated systems build; next module: pricing.
