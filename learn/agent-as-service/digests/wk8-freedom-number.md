# L1 overview

- Final module framing: week 8 is about defining what freedom actually means, calculating the exact monthly income needed to live the way you want, and building a learning system so the path forward — services, content, or products — is clear through stacking leverage that compounds over time.
- Most people have a vague target ("more money"); the fix is pinpointing an exact number and back-calculating the inputs, which shifts focus from output KPIs (e.g., 10K MRR, hard to control month-on-month) to input KPIs (client count, outreach emails, LinkedIn DMs, videos posted — directly controllable).
- The freedom number ("enough number") is concrete and specific: roughly your corporate salary, or precisely, all monthly expenses plus a 20–30% buffer; a rough number is enough — don't overcomplicate it. The course pegs 10K MRR as the example because it replaces most salaries worldwide.
- Worked reverse-engineering example: 10K/month = 4 clients at 2.5K retainers; if clients come from content, the most actionable input KPI is videos posted per week on TikTok, cross-posted to Instagram — everything else is tactics.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L137` — Welcome to week 8, the final module: not just hitting a revenue number but defining what freedom means and setting goals to expand beyond the freedom number; the week builds a sustainable learning system to stay sharp about AI, and clarifies the path forward (stay in services, build content, or create products) by stacking leverage that compounds over time.
- `#L139-L249` — Why the target must precede scaling: most people have a vague idea ("more money" or "just enough to quit my job") without doing the math; pinpointing the exact number and back-calculating inputs creates focus. Defines output vs. input KPIs: 10K per month MRR (monthly recurring revenue) is an output KPI — Key Performance Indicators are hard to set as exact month-on-month targets because too many factors intervene; input KPIs are what you control more directly.
- `#L251-L332` — Defining the freedom number (some call it the "enough number"): the monthly income needed to live like you want, which depends on location, living situation, and family dependents; it must be concrete and specific — not a number without a ceiling, but the actual number that would make you feel free. For most people it's roughly what they make at their corporate job (a perfectly good target); the course example is 10K MRR because that replaces most salaries in most locations. Exact method: tally all monthly expenditures — housing, food, transport, insurance, subscriptions, everything — then add a 20–30% buffer; the total is your baseline freedom number, and a rough number is enough to aim at.
- `#L334-L414` — Output KPIs can't be hit on a straight line: targeting 1K MRR this month, 2K next month, and so on to 10K in 10 months likely fails because an output KPI is simply the output of your inputs, the ones you actually control. Reverse-engineering chain: clients served this month → clients talked to (some won't pull through) → cold outreach emails sent or LinkedIn DMs sent. Early on, people start by offering services at a lower price and need more clients; as credentials and reputation grow, they charge higher and work with a few select clients, expanding vertically within those clients.
- `#L416-L553` — The number is probably lower than you think: when you actually add up expenses, it's often lower than the vague "I need to make a lot of money" feeling, because most people never set the target — they overestimate. Homework: calculate your freedom number and reverse-engineer it as far as possible to the most controllable input KPI. Worked example: 10K/month via four clients at 2.5K retainers, gathered through content → the directly actionable input KPI is the number of videos posted per week on TikTok (cross-posted to Instagram); once you have that target, everything else is tactics. Next module: how to stay current in a fast-moving space and get the skill of learning how to learn.
