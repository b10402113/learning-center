# L1 overview

- **Ch4 §4.1–§4.9 — Inductance and PDN Design (first half)** — PDN design targets an impedance profile; the loop inductance of capacitors, planes, and vias dominates impedance at high frequency, while VRM inductance interacting with bulk capacitance sets the low-frequency parallel resonance and real capacitor parasitics create mid-band peaks. Inductance is defined as flux per amp, L = ψ/I (Webers/Amp = Henrys), i.e. the *efficiency* of a conductor loop at creating magnetic field-line rings per amp — not the ring count, not the current, not B, and not a dielectric property. Inductance depends only on geometry: (1) shorter length, (2) larger cross-section so current spreads, (3) closer signal/return spacing all lower L. Six field principles: lines are closed rings; current creates concentric rings; dielectrics don't interact; ring count ∝ current; changing ring count induces voltage (Faraday); energy lives in the field. Z_L = jωL, so impedance rises with frequency while L itself is nearly constant (slight decrease from current redistribution). Quasi-static validity: structure < λ/20, delay < period/20, phase < 18°, giving Len·f < 6 in·GHz in air (≈0.6 GHz at 1 inch, scaling inversely with length); in FR4 speed halves so 12 in / 30 cm boards go full-wave above ≈30 MHz — the origin of power/ground cavity resonances, whereas a discrete capacitor stays quasi-static lumped RLC past 1 GHz. Measured check: 10 cm (12 in circumference) 18-gauge loop matches ideal L to 10% to 40 MHz and 20% to 60 MHz (Figs. 4.6–4.7). Units: 1 Weber = 10^8 Maxwells; B in Wb/m² = Tesla = 10^4 Gauss; µ0 = 4π×10⁻⁷ H/m = 1.257 nH/mm = 32 nH/inch = 32 pH/mil. Energy density e = B²/2µ0, total U = ½LI², so inductance density dL/dv = B²/(2µ0I²) — reduce B and confine it near the loop. Loop inductance is the B integral over the closed loop area, subject to ∇·B = 0 and ∇×B = µ0J; Biot-Savart makes it a double integral, so use closed-form approximations or 2D/3D quasi-static/full-wave solvers. Total L = external (outside conductor, geometry-only, frequency-independent, equals high-frequency L) + internal (inside conductor, frequency-dependent, material-sensitive); DC internal L of a round wire is diameter-independent, ∝ µr·Len, and µr = 1 for Cu, Ag, Al, Sn, Pb — only Fe, Ni, Co (and alloy 42, 42% Ni) amplify it.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L51` — §4.1 why inductance matters: PDN goal is a target impedance profile; interconnects modeled as transmission lines reducible to L/C at low frequency; VRM inductance × bulk C sets the low-frequency parallel resonance peak, capacitor parasitics set mid-band peaks, loop inductance of capacitors/planes/vias dominates at high frequency. Lower inductance raises no cost when designed into the structure; inductance is the most important and most misunderstood PDN property.
- `#L52-L141` — §4.2 capacitance review as a contrast: C = Q/V, a measure of charge-storage efficiency per volt, set only by overlap area, spacing, and dielectric constant — not by the applied voltage. Time-domain capacitor impedance depends on the voltage waveform; frequency domain gives the simple extrinsic Z = 1/(jωC) for sinusoidal V and I.
- `#L142-L191` — §4.3 definition of loop inductance: L = ψ/I with ψ the flux through the loop and B(x,y) the field density; the surface integral obscures intuition. Working definition: inductance is the efficiency of a conductor loop at producing field-line rings at the cost of current. Introduces the six essential principles of magnetic fields (closed rings; concentric rings around current; no dielectric interaction; ring count ∝ current; changing ring count induces voltage; energy stored in the field).
- `#L192-L287` — Counting rings: rings are countable, measured in Webers (SI) or Maxwells (cgs); a long straight wire carries concentric rings along its whole length (Fig. 4.1) with circulation given by the right-hand rule (Fig. 4.2). Because every line is a closed ring, the ring count equals the flux crossing a plane from the conductor out to infinity (Fig. 4.3); flux of field lines = number of rings surrounding the conductor.
- `#L288-L397` — L = ψ/I in Webers/Amp = Henrys, practical units nH/pH (abHenry in EMU cgs noted only for completeness). Inductance is intrinsic geometry, unchanged whether current is 1 µA or 1 MA or zero. Dielectrics are transparent to magnetic field lines (diamagnetic/paramagnetic effect < 0.01%), except ferrites (iron oxide plus Zn/Ni/Mn/Sr), which raise L when they fully enclose the current and are used as common-mode chokes and discrete inductors; only ferromagnetic Fe/Ni/Co affect internal self-inductance (see §4.9). Three geometry levers: shorter length, larger cross-section (current spreading), closer signal–return spacing.
- `#L398-L498` — §4.4 impedance of an inductor: Faraday's Law (a Maxwell equation) gives ΔV = dψ/dt, hence V = L dI/dt — either a changing current generates a back-emf, or an applied voltage drives a changing current (the useful view for chip-attach inductance, Ch9). Time-domain Z = V/I depends on waveform; frequency domain gives Z = jωL. L is roughly constant with frequency (slight decrease from current redistribution); the impedance, not the inductance, rises with ω — the reason minimizing L is central to PDN design.
- `#L499-L587` — §4.5 quasi-static approximation: propagation at the speed of light makes full Maxwell treatment expensive; if the phase is constant across the problem area, propagation can be ignored (quasi-static) versus full-wave. Three equivalent criteria: phase variation < 1/20 cycle; size < 1/20 wavelength; delay < 1/20 period (18°). Equation Len < v/(20f); in air c ≈ 12 in/ns so Len·f < 6 in·GHz, i.e. f_max ≈ 0.6 GHz for a 1-inch structure, scaling inversely with length. If unsure between 1/10 and 1/20, use full-wave.
- `#L588-L653` — Consequences and validation (Figs. 4.5–4.7): at 1 GHz structures up to ~1 cm remain valid lumped elements, so discrete capacitor RLC models hold above 1 GHz; in FR4 the speed halves and the limit halves, so a 12-inch/30 cm board needs full-wave above ~30 MHz — the origin of plane-cavity resonances. Measured 10 cm diameter, 18-gauge (49 mil OD) copper loop, 0.3 m circumference, predicted full-wave onset ≈50 MHz; VNA two-port extraction matches ideal inductance within 10% to 40 MHz and 20% to 60 MHz.
- `#L654-L775` — §4.6 magnetic field density B: B = dψ/da, the flux per unit area (Wb/m² = Tesla; Maxwells/cm² = Gauss; 1 T = 10⁴ G). Calling B "the magnetic field" is misleading — it is a density, not a line count, and high B alone does not imply high inductance. Earth ≈0.5 G = 50 µT; sunspots up to 5,000 G; CME fields ~1 G can cancel Earth's field and drive auroras/grid disruption. Table 4.1 summarizes units; ψ = ∫B·da over the half-plane from the conductor to infinity, and L = ψ/I — integrating from the conductor's outer edge gives external inductance (Fig. 4.8).
- `#L776-L918` — §4.7 energy in the magnetic field: energy density e = B²/(2µ0) J/m³ with µ0 = 4π×10⁻⁷ H/m = 1.257 nH/mm = 32 nH/inch = 32 pH/mil; U = ∫(B²/2µ0)dv = ½LI². Energy is stored in how tightly lines are packed (a "pressure"), not in individual lines, so inductance is the efficiency of storing energy per amp². Inductance density dL/dv = B²/(2µ0I²) is distributed in space; integrate it for total L. Design implications: widen conductors to cut B, shorten them to cut the integration volume, and bring signal/return close so B falls to near zero far from the loop.
- `#L919-L1039` — §4.8 Maxwell's equations and loop inductance: a closed current loop is the unambiguous case — every ring links the enclosed area (Fig. 4.9), so L_loop = (1/I)∫B·da over that area (Fig. 4.10). B must obey ∇·B = 0 (no magnetic sources/sinks, closed rings) and ∇×B = µ0J (currents generate B, J in A/m², µ0 = 32 nH/inch). Biot-Savart dB = (µ0 I dl × r)/(4πr³) gives B from each current element (Fig. 4.11); total L then needs a double integral (all currents, then all area). Practical alternatives: closed-form approximations, 2D quasi-static solver for uniform transmission lines, 3D quasi-static solver (DC/HF/frequency-dependent), or a full-wave solver (always frequency-dependent). Do one integral by hand once, then never again.
- `#L1040-L1124` — §4.9 internal vs external inductance (section continues past this chunk): current distribution shifts with frequency, changing B inside the conductor, so L becomes frequency-dependent even within quasi-static limits. External field lines lie outside the conductor, are geometry-only, composition-independent, frequency-independent, and equal the high-frequency inductance; internal lines lie inside and depend on material. At DC current is uniform; internal L of a round wire of length Len is independent of diameter (larger diameter lowers current density and ring density proportionally) and scales with relative permeability µr. µr = 1 for Cu, Ag, Al, Sn, Pb, so internal L is composition-independent; Ni plating can reach µr ≈ 1–40 (falling toward 1 above ~1 GHz), and alloy 42 (42% Ni, balance Fe, CTE-matched to glass, stiffer than Cu) is the noted ferromagnetic exception.
