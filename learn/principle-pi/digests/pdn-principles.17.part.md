# L1 overview

- **Ch10 opening — the practical design flow** — Robust/cost-effective PDN design means die voltage within spec at lowest cost. "Fully robust" holds under all code/conditions and costs the most (parts, layers, materials, tools, time); "reasonably (marginally) robust" accepts rare worst-case failure. The general strategy is three escalating passes: (1) judgement, rules of thumb, simple approximations to pick a first-pass starting value for each important parameter; (2) a linearized spreadsheet to iterate tradeoffs; (3) field solvers plus time/frequency circuit simulation to verify and optimize. Overdesign is explicitly framed as buying insurance against unknown inputs.

- **Ch10 §10.2 — the principle summary (target impedance, peaks, q)** — Frequency-domain impedance seen from the die is the primary view, valid because the PDN is linear, time invariant, passive; two exceptions break LTI and need transient simulation: the SMPS (topology and output impedance change with load) and on-die circuits (capacitance changes with switching). These are linearized anyway: SMPS as a few elements behind a constant source; switching load as an averaged linear damping resistance. Target impedance comes from max time-averaged current and tolerable noise; meeting it everywhere gives a fully robust PDN, while a marginally robust one only requires every peak's Z0 below target. Peaks (never dips) cause violations; each peak is a parallel RLC described by f_res, Z0 = √(L/C), and q, with Z_peak = Z0·q (series-loss parallel resonance) or Z_peak = Z0/q (parallel-loss). Series RLC makes a valley at the ESR; parallel RLC makes a peak whose height the loss sets. Step droop = Z0 × I_step, so measuring the initial droop with known Z0 is the best way to back out transient current. A square wave at f_res sustained ≥ q cycles gives V_pk-pk ≈ (2/π)·q·I_transient·Z0, valid for q > 2; with one dominant peak, peak impedance must stay below ≈ π/2 = 1.6× target.

- **Ch10 §10.2 — Bandini Mountain and loop inductance** — The dominant peak is normally the Bandini Mountain from on-die capacitance resonating with package lead inductance, with on-die metal and package conductor resistance as R plus a parallel conductance from nonlinear CMOS switching (extra damping/margin). Reduce it by more on-die C, less package L, more damping; on-package decoupling (OPD) splits it into two peaks (low one via large C, high one only via mounted inductance and ESR), and board MLCCs otherwise add to the package inductance. Optimized board capacitor values can lower the peak but are sensitive; the robust alternative is a spread of values giving a flat, resistive profile up to the Bandini frequency — which damps the peak provided Z0(Bandini) < target and the flat board profile sits just below target. Peaks are fundamentally limited by loop inductance: short conductors, power/ground close, wide conductors; practically via-in-pad, thin power-ground dielectric, planes near the component surface, and many parallel paths.

- **Ch10 §10.3 — the PRC spreadsheet** — The PDN Resonant Calculator (PRC, Table 10.1) is a linearized sizing/planning/optimization and model-to-hardware correlation tool, not a verifier and not tied to artwork. It models three peaks roughly a decade apart: (a) on-die C + package lead L + MLCC L, (b) MLCCs + bulk-cap loop L, (c) bulk caps + VRM. Six sections: rail/current/target impedance; 0th-dip clock-edge noise; on-die properties; peak analysis ×3; frequency/time-domain FOMs; vertical and horizontal loop-inductance extraction. It predicts impulse, step, and resonant responses from load current and clock frequency, and identifies which parameters are insignificant.

- **Ch10 §10.4 — PRC lines 1–12 (rail, current, target Z)** — Line 2 Vdd = 1 V; line 3 max current = 10 A (leakage + clock + switching, time-averaged over microcode); line 4 min current with clock = 2 A; line 5 leakage without clock = 1 A (exponential in Vdd, often ∝ Vdd³ by tunneling/subthreshold). Line 6 max dynamic = 10 − 1 = 9 A; line 7 min dynamic = 2 − 1 = 1 A; line 8 AC tolerance = 5% (single-sided amplitude, ±5%, not pk-pk); line 9 transient current = 9 − 1 = 8 A; line 10 AC target = 5%·1 V / 8 A = 6.3 mΩ; line 11 DC tolerance = 1%; line 12 DC target = 1%·1 V / 10 A = 1 mΩ (leakage included because it flows through the DC path).

- **Ch10 §10.5 — PRC lines 13–24 (0th droop and on-die RLC)** — Clock-edge (0th droop) noise comes from charging load capacitance each rising edge; 1st/2nd/3rd droops belong to the impedance peaks. Line 14 clock = 2,000 MHz; line 15 charge = 9 A × 0.5 ns = 4.5 nC/cycle; line 16 switched load C = Q/V = 4.5 nF; line 17 switch factor = 4.5 nF / 100 nF = 4.5%; line 19 on-die C = 100 nF (measurable by VNA if no OPD/power gating); lines 20–21 split the on-die ESR into R_die1 (metallization, DC IR) and R_die2 (FET transconductance + signal wire to the next gate) at 60%/40%, with RC ≈ 250 ps independent of die area; line 22 leakage as a shunt resistance; line 23 coefficient a (=1 here for 1 A at 1 V); line 24 dynamic leakage resistance = 330 mΩ shunt across the on-die capacitance.

- **Ch10 §10.6 — extracting vertical and horizontal parasitics** — Vertical structures (vias, balls, bumps) use the twin-rod approximation: L per length in pH/mm from ln(D/R) with µ0 = 4π×10⁻⁴ H/mm, current assumed on the outer surface; DC loop R per length = 2/(σπ(R²−r²)) for the two series paths; skin depth δ = √(1/(πfµσ)) with f in MHz, copper δ = 6.6 µm at 100 MHz, and R scales as √f from that reference. Multiple pairs in parallel divide inductance only without mutual coupling; clumped Vdd or Vss raises it above 1× the isolated value, while a checkerboard array of interleaved Vdd/Vss pins reduces it via the checkerboard reduction factor, which saturates: 1×2 (1 pair), 2×2 (2), 3×3 (4.5), 4×4 (8), 5×5 (12.5), 6×6 (18), asymptote 0.7 when pin diameter is 25% of pitch, lower as pins get closer (Figures 10.4–10.6). Horizontal structures use sheet inductance: L_loop = L_sq × n with L_sq = µ0·h (nH/square, dielectric thickness only) and n = length/width; sheet resistance R_sq = 1/(σt) with two traces in series, evaluated at the resonant frequency's skin-limited resistance.

- **Ch10 §10.7 — worked default geometry** — Default extraction: C4 30 µm diameter on 200 µm pitch, BGA 0.5 mm on 1 mm pitch, board vias 0.5 mm deep. Bottom-side capacitor mounting ≈ 1.9 nH; top-side via loop ≈ 0.37 nH per capacitor; package vertical path ≈ 29 pH; 18 BGA via pairs in parallel ≈ 19 pH; with a 76 µm board cavity the sheet inductance is 95 pH/square, one square gives 95 pH, so PCB path ≈ 111 pH and package ≈ 30 pH, total 141 pH. Tables 10.4/10.5 are the extraction and per-peak RLC summary tabs.

- **Ch10 §10.8 — the three PRC loops** — Three peaks exist when significant inductance separates the capacitive branches, higher L and larger C farther from the die giving lower f_res. The Bandini Mountain (on-die C / package L, with MLCC mounting L) is the highest-frequency, highest-Z, hardest peak and the PRC's main value. Next is MLCC-to-bulk-cap loop interaction, lowest is VRM effective inductance with bulk capacitance. Peaks may be analyzed independently only if separated by ≥ a decade; otherwise they interact, PRC accuracy drops for the low peaks, and circuit simulation is required (Bandini numbers stay usable if its loop L includes both high-frequency and bulk-cap loops). Line 26 package series L; lines 27–28 package R at DC and at f_res (larger value used for damping); line 29 board via + spreading L (plus VRM output L for the lowest peak); lines 30–31 board R at DC/f_res; line 32 capacitor count; line 34 a single capacitor value (multiple values need simulation); line 35 ESR from a 0402 MLCC 1/C fit with C in nF; line 36 capacitor body partial self-inductance; line 38 via series R; line 39 surface-pad L (near zero with via-in-pad); line 40 via loop L; lines 42–44 the resulting series-RLC values combined in parallel.

- **Ch10 §10.9 — five frequency FOMs and three time-domain FOMs** — Per peak: line 55 f_res, line 56 peak impedance, line 57 PDN ratio = Z_peak/Z_target, line 48 Z0, line 49 q. Time domain: line 58 clock-edge droop from one impulse = charge/total on-die C (4.5 nC/100 nF ≈ 45 mV); line 59 step droop = Z0 × I_transient; line 60 resonant pk-pk = (2/π)·q·Z0·I_transient after ≥ q cycles at f_res, the usual worst case. Three loops give three droops (first = highest-frequency peak, second = middle, third = lowest), requiring strong package inductance and an inductive VRM-to-MLCC path. The fix later in the chapter is adding capacitive branches to break up the separating inductances — FDTIM, flattening the profile until peaks blur and a circuit simulator replaces the PRC.

- **Ch10 §10.10 — four damping mechanisms and q bookkeeping** — Z_peak = Z0·q, so lower q is always the goal; damping is the hardest part of model-to-hardware correlation. Four sources (Figure 10.9): leakage, load loss, on-die capacitor ESR, bump-loop resistance; extra series R can be added deliberately. For the Bandini Mountain the loop is a series current loop (electric energy in C ↔ magnetic in L), so die ESR and bump/board copper (with skin effect) are series losses; leakage and load are shunts. q = R_parallel/Z0 for shunt loss and q = Z0/R_series for series loss; leakage uses the dynamic resistance (I ∝ V³) at the operating voltage; q3 = Z0/R_ODC-ESR, q4 = Z0/R_loop-ESR. Individual qs combine like parallel resistors, 1/q_total = Σ 1/q_n, so the smallest q dominates. In the default case load loss is smallest, leakage second, and both are commonly omitted from simulations.

- **Ch10 §10.10 — correlation across three load conditions** — With no load damping the ADS peak is 0.163 Ω at 44.26 MHz versus PRC 165 mΩ at 44.10 MHz (q = 4.5), provided high-frequency and bulk cap paths are paralleled. A steady 9 A load is a 0.111 Ω (1 V/9 A) shunt: q_load = 3.0, q_total = 1.8, 66 mΩ predicted vs 67 mΩ simulated. The realistic case is 8 A transient plus 1 A leakage at 50% duty, so damping uses the time-averaged 5 A: R = 0.200 Ω, q_load = 5.5, q_total = 2.5, 91 mΩ from both PRC and simulation. This validates replacing a nonlinear switched load with a time-averaged frequency-domain resistance, but shows the assumed current profile changes damping by more than 2×.

- **Ch10 §10.11 — switched-capacitor load model** — A resistor is a poor load model; the die is dynamic. ADS time-domain model (Figures 10.11, 10.12): the die becomes a `switch_cap_load` subcircuit that borrows part of the on-die capacitance to draw charge impulses each cycle, so zero switched C means zero dynamic current and the switch factor sets the fraction. L1 is a fictitious inductor shaping the impulse; L_tau/R_tau are sized so the impulse finishes just before the clock period ends; `VAR_for_switch_cap_load` adapts the shaping to the clock frequency; `VAR_waveform_cntl` holds PWL definitions for step and resonance ramps and arbitrary ramp rates; `I_tran` makes c_load time-varying (simulators warn but comply). Leakage is a voltage-controlled current source with V³ dependence for correct damping.

- **Ch10 §10.12 — impulse and step correlation** — Default damping is the 50%-duty 8 A case, the worst realistic condition. Impulse: the die draws a 4.5 nC (line 15) impulse 15 ns in, peaking 25 A and finishing in 0.5 ns; the bump branch is filtered by on-die C and bump L to a 2 A peak 5 ns later, dipping to 0.2 A and ringing out; die droop is 45 mV predicted (line 58) vs 47 mV simulated, and the ringing dies in ~4 cycles matching the no-load q = 4.5 (no load current flows after the edge). Step (8 A in 1 ns): the switched-cap load peaks at 12 A with q ≲ 2, while an ideal current source peaks at 15 A and rings with q ≈ 4; PRC predicts 292 mV for the full ideal step and the ideal-source simulation gives 271 mV (leakage damps it). More realistic load modeling therefore yields extra margin over an ideal step, and the switched-cap final current is slightly low because DC IR drop lowers die voltage and thus the charge consumed.
