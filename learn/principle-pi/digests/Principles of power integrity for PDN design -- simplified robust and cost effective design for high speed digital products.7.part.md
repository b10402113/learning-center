# L1 overview

- **4.1 Why inductance matters** — The PDN's job is meeting a target impedance profile, and loop inductance dominates that impedance at high frequency while also inflating every parallel-resonance peak. Because lower-inductance geometry often costs nothing extra, mastering inductance is the cheapest lever for balancing performance and cost.
- **4.2 Capacitance review** — Capacitance is C = Q/V, a geometry-and-dielectric property measuring how efficiently a conductor pair stores charge per volt of stress; it does not depend on how much charge or voltage is present. Its time-domain impedance depends on the voltage waveform, but in the frequency domain it collapses to a simple jω form.
- **4.3 What inductance is** — Loop inductance is the flux of field-line rings per amp, Ψ/I — a measure of how efficiently a conductor loop creates magnetic field lines at the cost of current, not of how many lines exist. Six principles (closed rings, right-hand circulation, dielectric transparency, proportionality to current, Faraday induction, stored energy) plus Webers/Maxwells units build the intuition; only length, cross-section and signal-return spacing change it, and only ferrites/ferromagnetic metals perturb the field.
- **4.4 Impedance of an inductor** — Faraday's law turns changing flux into voltage, giving V = L dI/dt and, in the frequency domain, Z = jωL. The key asymmetry: inductance is roughly constant with frequency while its impedance rises with frequency, which is why high-frequency impedance is inductance-limited.
- **4.5 Quasi-static approximation** — The lumped model is valid when the structure is under 1/20 wavelength, 1/20 period of delay, or 18 degrees of phase; in air that is about f(GHz) ≈ 0.6 divided by length in inches. A 12-inch board needs full-wave treatment above ~30 MHz, and a 10 cm wire loop measures a constant inductance to 40–60 MHz, matching the rule.
- **4.6 Magnetic field density B** — B is flux per unit area (Tesla, or 10⁴ Gauss), a density rather than the number of lines, and high B is not the same as high inductance. Flux is recovered by integrating B over a half-plane from the conductor to infinity, and Table 4.1 collects the magnetic units.
- **4.7 Energy in the magnetic field** — Energy density is e = B²/2µ₀, total energy U = LI²/2, so L = 2U/I²; equivalently an inductance density dL/dv = B²/(µ₀I²) is spread through space. Because energy scales with the square of field density, lowering the field density and shrinking the volume it occupies are the same design moves as before.
- **4.8 Maxwell's equations and loop inductance** — A closed loop makes inductance unambiguous, since every ring that surrounds the conductor also pierces the loop area. ∇·B = 0 (no monopoles) and ∇×B = µ₀J (with Biot–Savart) fix the field, but computing L needs a double integral; in practice one uses closed-form approximations, 2D or 3D quasi-static solvers, or a full-wave solver.
- **4.9 Internal and external inductance, skin depth** — External field lines give geometry-only, frequency-independent inductance, while internal lines give material-sensitive inductance that vanishes as current crowds to the surface. The DC internal inductance of a round wire is µ₀µ_r·Len/(8π), independent of diameter; alloy 42's µ_r ≈ 200 makes DC inductance huge but irrelevant above 10 MHz. Skin depth δ = √(1/πfµσ) is 2.1 µm in copper at 1 GHz and 21 µm at 10 MHz, so a 17 µm trace is all external above ~100 MHz.
- **4.10 Loop versus partial inductance** — Loop inductance belongs to the whole loop and shifts if any feature changes. Partial self-inductance (set by width) and partial mutual inductance (set by separation) let you attribute that loop inductance to segments: L_loop = L_self,s + L_self,r − 2M for identical conductors, which reproduces the shorter/wider/closer design rules.
- **4.11 Uniform round conductors** — Only coax, twin round rods, and a rod over a plane have exact closed-form loop inductance per length. Worked cases: a coax with 5 mm and 2 mm radii; a BGA via pair at 5 mil radius and 40 mil pitch giving ~21 pH/mil, i.e. ~1.3 nH through a 64-mil board; and a 10-mil wire 20 mils over a plane giving ~10 nH/inch.
- **4.12 Round loops** — The high-frequency round-loop approximation L ≈ µ₀·a·ln(8a/W_R) predicts 247 nH for the 3.5-inch measured loop, matching experiment. Inductance scales with a·ln(a), not with area, and for thin wire settles near 25 nH/inch of circumference (1 nH/mm) over a wide range of loop sizes.
- **4.13 Wide conductors close together** — Two broadside plates give the parallel-plate model L = µ₀·Len·h/w, whose per-square form is sheet inductance L_sq = µ₀·h (32 pH/mil × h). A 2.7-mil dielectric yields 86 pH/square, so 10 squares of 1000×100 mil trace is 860 pH; the approximation holds only above about 10:1 width-to-height (30% error at 10:1, 300% at 1:1), and w = h gives roughly 10 nH/inch.
- **4.14 Any uniform transmission line** — For any uniform line, L = Z₀ × TD, so every 50 Ω line in FR4 has ~8.3 nH/inch regardless of cross-section. IPC microstrip and stripline Z₀ approximations feed this estimate; transmission-line formulas fit aspect ratios below ~3 while the parallel-plate form fits above ~3.
- **4.15 A simple rule of thumb** — For a narrow conductor with a distant return, assume 25 nH/inch or 1 nH/mm and multiply by length; the straight-rod partial self-inductance formula confirms this order of magnitude. Use it for a fast answer, then switch to the section 4.14 approximations for wide conductors close together.
- **4.16 Extracting L from 3D-solver S-parameters** — A 3D solver handles arbitrary via/plane geometry but returns S-parameters; shorting port 2 creates a loop, and S11 converts to Z_in and then to L. For the BGA power/ground via pair, twin-rod theory predicts 1.3 nH while Simbeor extracts 1.5 nH (extra non-uniform current and fringing), and the ideal-inductor model holds to ~6 GHz, consistent with the 1/20-wavelength limit.
- **4.17 Bottom line** — Ten takeaways: inductance sets high-frequency impedance and peak heights; it is field-line efficiency per amp and stored-energy efficiency; do not confuse it with B; quasi-static is good below 1/20 wavelength; only three exact geometries exist; shorten, widen, and tighten the loop; all squares of a plane pair share one sheet inductance; parallel plate is valid above ~10:1 aspect; and equal-Z₀ lines share equal inductance per length.
