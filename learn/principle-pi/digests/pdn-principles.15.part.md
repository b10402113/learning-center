# L1 overview

- **Ch9 §9.1 — Why the transient current matters** — Voltage noise = f(transient current waveform, its frequency content and timing) × PDN impedance. A constant die current would reduce PDN design to DC IR drop plus a remote-sense VRM. Three canonical stimuli classify the problem — single clock pulse (impulse), step, and resonance — and any two of {current waveform, impedance profile, voltage response} determine the third. A PDN impedance profile is only flat regions plus peaks (each peak = f_res, Z0, Q).
- **Ch9 §9.2 — Flat impedance and target impedance** — For flat Z, Vnoise = Zflat · Itransient. Tolerance gives Ztarget = Vtolerance / Itransient. Example: 1 V rail, 1 A transient, 5% (50 mV) → Ztarget = 50 mΩ. Transient current = Imax − Imin (Figure 9.1), normally idle→heavy-transition.
- **Ch9 §9.3 — Estimating the transient current** — Vendors rarely supply it. Bounds: idle→full-on gives Itransient = Imax; always-same-code gives ≈0; FPGAs/large-gate-count chips typically Imin ≈ 50% of Imax, so 50% of Imax is the default estimate; mobile chips swing 1%→100% in microseconds, so 100% is right there. Uncertainty → "buy insurance" with a lower Ztarget.
- **Ch9 §9.4 — Actual die current: clock-edge current** — Inverter drives a lumped capacitive load (Figure 9.2/9.3). With load referenced only to Vss, PDN current flows only on the rising edge, once per clock. Charge per edge Qclk_edge = Iclk_edge·T = C·Vdd; dynamic (average) current Iavg = Qclk_edge/T = C·Vdd·f. Example: Ron = 10 Ω, C = 1 pF → 10 ps charging time. Doubling clock frequency keeps each pulse identical but doubles the average current (Figure 9.5). On-die capacitance smooths the spikes so package bumps see the averaged dynamic current.
- **Ch9 §9.5 — Capacitance referenced to both rails** — Usual case: C_Vdd ≈ C_Vss (Figure 9.6). Load capacitance doubles, RC time constant and total integrated charge double, but only half the charge loops through the PDN. Net PDN loop current exists on **both** rising and falling transitions (Figures 9.7/9.8); FET channel currents are 2× the PDN loop current. Rule: **logic gates draw PDN current once per clock cycle; the clock tree draws it twice per cycle** (clock-tree impulses at 2× clock frequency).
- **Ch9 §9.6 — Measurement (embedded controller)** — Arduino-class MCU, single 5 V core+I/O rail, 450 Ω series + 50 Ω coax into scope (10 mA per pin), LeCroy HDO 12-bit. 16 MHz clock (60 ns period, 3 ns edges), I/O low 3 cycles / high 1 cycle. Measured: Vdd collapses ≈20 mV; Vss bounces ≈35 mV positive on **each** clock edge, i.e., at 2× clock frequency. Larger collapse when I/O latches fire; rising/falling asymmetry attributed to differing combinational activity or SSN-style return-path referencing.
- **Ch9 §9.7 — Real origin of PDN noise** — Capacitor: i = C·dv/dt, so i/C is the forcing function for dv/dt; inductor: v = L·di/dt, so v/L forces di/dt (Figure 9.11). Root cause is charge depletion of the on-die capacitance (ODC) by switching gates: ΔV = ΔQ/C comes first, that droop appears across the package lead inductance, and only then does current ramp in from outside at di/dt = v/L. Consequence: "there must be noise to get current"; slope is v/L, so lower loop L is better. ODC also acts as a divider against external noise, so PDN noise is mostly self-aggression. Simulation (Figure 9.12/9.13): 50.7 pH vs 25.3 pH, 1 nsec→100 psec impulse; ODC drops 31 mV instantly (plus 2 mΩ ESR drop); halving L doubles di/dt, raises f_res 25% (∝1/√L) and lowers Q.
- **Ch9 §9.8 — Equations for an impedance peak** — Same RLC seen from the board is series (dip to R); seen from the die with a low-Z board it is parallel (peak) — Figure 9.14/9.15. f_res = 1/(2π√(LC)); Z0 = √(L/C) = reactance at resonance (not a transmission-line Z0); Q = Z0/R; Z_peak ≈ Q·Z0 (accurate only for high Q). Worked design: C = 50 nF, want 100 mΩ peak at 100 MHz → L = 50.7 pH, Z0 = 32 mΩ, Q = 3.15, R = 10.1 mΩ (Table 9.1, Figures 9.16/9.17).
- **Ch9 §9.9 — The three characterizing current waveforms** — (1) **Impulse**: one clock-edge charge, or an anti-impulse (one missing edge). (2) **Step**: Imax−Imin turn-on held high — clock gating, frequency change, cache-miss pipeline restart; rise time can equal a clock edge. (3) **Resonance**: step currents repeating at f_res, modeled as a square wave of amplitude Itransient; low probability, devastating effect. Used both to predict noise from RLC and to reverse-engineer RLC from measurement.
- **Ch9 §9.10 — Impulse response** — Qclk_edge = 1.55 A / 1 GHz = 1.55 nC. ΔV_droop = Q/C = 1.55 nC/50 nF ≈ 31 mV (predicted), 29 mV simulated (some charge entered during the 1 nsec impulse). Rings for >40 nsec; 10 nsec period → 100 MHz; ~3 cycles → Q ≈ 3 (3.15 calculated). Method: droop → C, ring period → f_res, decay → Q, then equation 9.22 gives L/R. **Only ODC reduces clock-edge droop**; loop inductance is irrelevant because the event ends before current ramps.
- **Ch9 §9.11 — Step response** — Parallel-RLC step response = damped sine with initial droop Z0·I0 plus IR offset. With I0 = Itransient: droop ≈ Z0·Itransient, so **Z0 of every peak must be < Ztarget**. Simulated 50 mV (5% of 1 V) for the 32 mΩ / 1.55 A example. Remedies: raise ODC, cut mounted package loop L (Z0 = √(L/C)), or add low-mounting-inductance on-package caps.
- **Ch9 §9.12 — Resonance response** — First harmonic of a square wave: Ipk-pk = (4/π)·Itransient. Vpp = (4/π)·Itransient·Zpeak = (4/π)·Itransient·Q·Z0. Example: 1.55 A p-p, 100 mΩ peak → 198 mV predicted, 201 mV simulated; PDN loop current swings +4 A to −2.5 A for a 1.55 A load (energy builds cycle to cycle; one-sided current, two-sided voltage). Q sets buildup time (~Q cycles). Conservative criterion Vpp = 2·Vtolerance yields **Zpeak < (π/2)·Ztarget ≈ 1.6·Ztarget**. Robustness ladder: flat regions < Ztarget; all peak Z0 < Ztarget; all peak impedances < 1.6·Ztarget. Full resonance stimulation is rare/expensive to achieve but is done deliberately in the lab via clock gating at f_res; aerospace requires all peaks < Ztarget.
- **Ch9 §9.13 — Target impedance re-examined** — Ztarget = Vtolerance/Itransient mixes a single-sided p-p current with a double-sided ±tolerance. It is exact for a resistive/flat PDN and for a recentering VRM, and conservative by ≈1.6× for AC steady state and single-peak PDNs. Recentering mechanisms: VRM regulation loop (unless load line / adaptive voltage positioning holds the droop) and an LC section recovering over several PDN time constants. Consequence: after a current release, the recentered rail overshoots by roughly the original droop magnitude in the opposite direction.

# L2 detail

- `#L17337-L17360` — Chapter 9 opens: PDN noise depends on the specific Vdd–Vss transient current and the PDN impedance; a constant die current would leave only DC IR drop, trivially handled by a VRM remote sense line; the waveform, its frequency components, and their timing plus the impedance set the die-pad noise, so no robust impedance spec exists without the transient current.
- `#L17361-L17400` — Tips: unclear transient current means guessing the PDN; better current knowledge buys smaller margins and lower cost. Time-domain stimulus vs frequency-domain impedance forces linear time-invariant assumptions, which the VRM and dynamic die load violate (damping changes with microcode), yet time-invariant analysis still matches simulation when assumptions are chosen correctly.
- `#L17401-L17446` — Three canonical transient waveforms (single clock pulse/impulse, step, resonance) each probe a different impedance feature; knowing any two of {current, impedance, response} yields the third, which lets knowledge carry from generation to generation. Impedance profiles reduce to flat regions plus peaks (f_res, Z0, Q); flat-region analysis with broadband current comes first.
- `#L17447-L17499` — §9.2: flat (resistive) Z makes Vnoise directly proportional to Zflat; the noise tolerance gives the flat-impedance limit known as target impedance. Numeric example: 1 V, 1 A, 5% = 50 mV → 50 mΩ. Figure 9.1 defines transient current as Imax − Imin (idle/leakage/clock-generation current vs peak draw); the usual worst case is idle → heavy logic transition.
- `#L17500-L17550` — §9.3: vendors rarely give transient current because it depends on microcode. Only Imax is known (it sizes the VRM). Extremes: idle→full-on means Itransient = Imax; static code means ≈0 outside startup. Typical FPGA/large-gate-count Imin ≈ 50% of Imax → use 50% of Imax as the default; older processors idled at 90% power, modern mobile parts go 1%→100% in microseconds → use 100%. Residual uncertainty in Ztarget is covered by "buying insurance" with a lower target.
- `#L17551-L17637` — §9.4 intro: a constant current source with modulation is only a first-order die model; real current depends on each gate's draw, relative timing, and microcode, and transistor-level plus parasitic simulation yields a point solution with no transferability. Practical path is to start from a single gate. Figure 9.2: CMOS inverter as push-pull p/n FET between Vdd and Vss driving a lumped capacitive load (output net metal + next-gate input capacitance).
- `#L17638-L17690` — When the load is referenced only to Vss, charge flows Vdd→p-channel→capacitor on the rising edge and discharges through the n-channel locally; PDN current flows only on the rising edge, once per clock, as a pulse train (Figures 9.3/9.4). Charging profile is set by gate turn-on plus the RC of channel on-resistance and load C (10 Ω × 1 pF = 10 psec). Charge per edge Qclk_edge = Iclk_edge·T = C·Vdd; this "clock-edge current" is triggered by the clock edge and its per-cycle shape is frequency independent.
- `#L17691-L17725` — Average/dynamic current = Qclk_edge/T = C·Vdd·f; doubling f keeps pulses identical but doubles the average (Figure 9.5). Dynamic current depends on how many gates switch, total capacitance, and per-cycle logic changes; on-die decoupling filters the spikes so package bumps see the cycle-averaged waveform. Modulated load capacitance and modulated clock frequency are the two approximations used for the die current profile.
- `#L17726-L17799` — §9.5: interconnect is normally sandwiched between Vdd and Vss metal, so load C splits roughly equally between rails (Figure 9.6). Charging doubles the RC time constant and the integrated charge; PDN loop current magnitude is still set by p-channel Ron; the C·dV/dt displacement current splits half to Vss, half to Vdd. On a low-to-high transition the Vdd-referenced capacitor is "discharged" locally through the p-channel (no PDN current), and only the Vss-referenced half loops through the PDN (Figure 9.7).
- `#L17800-L17848` — On high-to-low, decreasing dV/dt drives displacement current from the Vdd capacitance through the n-channel to Vss, again producing net PDN loop current (Figure 9.8). Hence PDN current on both edges, FET currents 2× the loop current, clock tree at 2× clock frequency; logic, released by latches on the rising edge, draws once per cycle and completes before the next edge. Rail imbalance in C_Vdd vs C_Vss averages out across many wires; the loop current is what develops noise across the PDN impedance.
- `#L17849-L17903` — §9.6 measurement setup: embedded controller with one shared 5 V core/I-O rail; one pin pinned HIGH (Vdd sense), one pinned LOW (Vss sense), each through 450 Ω into 50 Ω coax and 50 Ω scope input, giving 5 V/500 Ω = 10 mA; a third pin toggles as fast as possible (Figure 9.9). Instrument: LeCroy HDO, 12-bit. Clock 16 MHz from a crystal → 60 ns period versus 3 ns driver edges; microcode holds the switching pin low 3 cycles, high 1 cycle.
- `#L17904-L17953` — §9.6 results (Figure 9.10, 100 mV/div, 50 nsec/div, 1 V/div for the switching pin): Vdd collapses ≈20 mV through package lead inductance, Vss rises ≈35 mV from the return current, and the compression repeats at 2× clock frequency from clock-tree activity plus dual-referenced load capacitance, even near idle. More gate activity (I/O latch setup) deepens the collapse; rising/falling asymmetry points to differing combinational activity or SSN return-path referencing. Current flows only at clock edges; dynamic current = averaged clock-edge current over one cycle = f · C · Vdd.
- `#L17954-L18022` — §9.7: capacitor and inductor relations (Figure 9.11) are instantaneous pairings, and cause vs effect is situational. Capacitor current can be square while voltage integrates (i/C is the forcing function); inductor voltage can be square while current integrates (v/L is the forcing function).
- `#L18023-L18107` — Root cause: switching gates deplete ODC charge, so ΔV = ΔQ/C appears first; that droop across the package lead drives di/dt = v/L, and only then does external current arrive. Two capacitors at different voltages joined by a switch is the picture; the impulse flows as fast as FET transconductance and wiring resistance allow. Reducing ΔV means more ODC or less charge consumed; ODC also divides down external noise (package L vs low ODC Z), so PDN noise is mostly self-inflicted. Larger droop → faster current in; smaller L → larger di/dt at the same droop. Some droop tolerance is unavoidable, which is exactly the target-impedance tolerance; lower loop inductance is always better but costs more.
- `#L18108-L18190` — Figure 9.12 test circuit: switched-capacitor "circuits" block injects one clock edge of charge, dropping ODC voltage, which drives inductor di/dt. Figure 9.13: 50.7 pH vs 25.3 pH with a 100 psec-wide impulse; ODC drops 31 mV immediately (ΔQ/C) plus extra from 2 mΩ series R; both cases start with the same droop because no realistic inductor delivers current that fast; halving L doubles the current slope, raises f_res 25% (∝1/√L), and reduces Q/ringing. I/O circuits invert the causality: there di/dt of large off-die switching current plus loop mutual inductance induces noise on quiet loops — "ground bounce" is inductively coupled crosstalk between I/O loop inductances, a separate problem from core Vdd noise.
- `#L18191-L18213` — Large-signal dI (Imax − Imin) is the quantity in the target impedance; the rise time dT decides which PDN element supplies it: fastest edges come from ODC, a few clock cycles from on-package capacitance, tens of cycles from board capacitors, hundreds from the VRM.
- `#L18214-L18327` — §9.8 case study: the same RLC is series from the board (VNA-style dip to R) and parallel from the die when the board PDN is a low-Z short (peak) — Figures 9.14/9.15. Equations: f_res = 1/(2π√(LC)); Z0 = √(L/C), the reactance at resonance where the L and C curves cross (explicitly not a transmission-line Z0); Q = Z0/R for series; Z_peak ≈ Q·Z0, valid for high Q only. Three uses: RLC→properties, measured f_res/Q/droop→RLC, desired properties→RLC. Worked example: C = 50 nF, 100 MHz, 100 mΩ peak → L = 50.7 pH, Z0 = 32 mΩ, Q = 3.15, loop R = 10.1 mΩ (Table 9.1, Figures 9.16/9.17 with R split inside/outside the package); Ztarget deliberately set equal to Z0.
- `#L18328-L18411` — §9.9: three characterizing stimuli — impulse (single clock-edge charge; anti-impulse = one missing pulse), step (abrupt min→max held high; clock start/frequency change/cache-miss restart; rise time as fast as a clock edge), and resonance (steps repeating at f_res, modeled as a square wave of amplitude Itransient). They predict noise from a known RLC and reverse-engineer RLC from measured response (Table 9.2: inputs shaded, outputs from closed-form equations). Setup: 1.55 A steady dynamic current (visible as a bench-supply current increase when the clock turns on), 1 V, ±5% → Ztarget = 32 mΩ = Z0 by construction.
- `#L18412-L18502` — §9.10: at 1 GHz and 1.55 A, charge per edge = 1.55 nC. Impulse depletes ODC: ΔV = Q/C = 31 mV (equation 9.23), the clock-edge droop; then the LC rings at f_res damped by Q. Simulation injects 1.55 nC in 1 nsec (Figure 9.18): 29 mV measured dip versus 31 mV predicted, ringing >40 nsec with a 10 nsec period (100 MHz) and ~3 cycles (Q ≈ 3.15), and current sometimes flows back into the board. Reverse-engineering recipe: droop→C, period→f_res, decay→Q, equation 9.22→remaining values. Only ODC reduces clock-edge droop; cutting loop inductance barely helps because the impulse ends before loop current ramps (the 1 nsec impulse is not negligible against the 10 nsec resonance period).
- `#L18503-L18596` — §9.11: a step I0 produces a damped sine plus IR offset; initial droop = Z0·I0. When the step equals the target-defining transient current, droop = Z0·Itransient ≤ Vtolerance requires **Z0 < Ztarget** — the "Bandini Mountain" characteristic-impedance criterion. Simulation with 1.55 A gives 50 mV (5% of 1 V), matching equation 9.33 (Figure 9.19); step currents arise from clock gating, frequency changes, and cache misses. Fixes: raise ODC or cut mounted package loop inductance since Z0 = √(L/C), or add on-package decoupling with very low mounting inductance. Z0 < Ztarget alone is not sufficient — resonant peak height still matters.
- `#L18597-L18688` — §9.12: square wave at f_res; first harmonic p-p current Ipk-pk = (4/π)·Itransient (Fourier), so Vpp = (4/π)·Itransient·Zpeak with Zpeak = Q·Z0 → 198 mV predicted for 1.55 A p-p and 100 mΩ. Simulation (Figure 9.20) gives 201 mV, a slightly higher actual peak; PDN loop current grows to +4 A/−2.5 A although the load is 1.55 A, because each step lands in phase with existing loop current and magnetic/electric energy accumulates. A one-sided current therefore produces a two-sided voltage, which is why the target-impedance formula pairs single-sided current with ± tolerance. Q ≈ 3 means ~3 cycles to full excursion; Q affects only buildup, not the step droop, which depends solely on Z0.
- `#L18689-L18746` — Derivation: Vpp relates to Zpeak, to Q·Z0, and to Itransient; with the conservative Vpp = 2·Vtolerance and Vtolerance = Ztarget·Itransient, the requirement becomes Zpeak < (π/2)·Ztarget ≈ 1.6·Ztarget — i.e., universal "all peaks below Ztarget" is ~1.6× conservative at resonances. Three-tier requirement: flat regions < Ztarget, peak Z0 < Ztarget, peak impedance < 1.6·Ztarget. Practical note: real microcode almost never launches full current exactly at f_res in the right phase (deliberate clock gating in the lab can); aerospace/mission-critical designs demand all peaks < Ztarget, which is almost always more expensive than a Z0-meets-Ztarget PDN.
- `#L18747-L18800` — §9.13: Ztarget = Vtolerance/Itransient mixes a single-sided p-p current with a double-sided ±tolerance; it is exact for resistive/flat PDNs and for a VRM with a load line or adaptive voltage positioning (die voltage stays between nominal and −5%), and conservative by ≈1.6× for AC steady state and single-peak PDNs. Recentering happens via the VRM regulation loop (depending on feedback point) or via an inductor restoring the rail over several PDN time constants; a subsequent current release then produces an overshoot of roughly the original droop magnitude in the opposite direction, which is why the ± tolerance must cover both.
