# L1 overview

- **Ch5 §5.12 (cont.) — three-term loop inductance of a mounted capacitor** — The mounted loop is the capacitor's intrinsic (partial) inductance plus the mounting structure's partial inductance minus 2× their mutual inductance. Vendor RLC/ESL models are misleading because the measurement fixture geometry is undisclosed; ignoring the mutual is a minor error with long surface traces and thick boards, but a major one for 0201 caps on pads with embedded microvias. Mounting inductance decomposes into (a) the via-pair loop to the cavity and (b) the surface-trace loop to the cavity top (Figure 5.27).

- **Ch5 §5.12 — via-pair estimate, worked numbers** — L = (µ0/π)·Len·ln(s/r) for two round rods (pH; s = center-to-center, r = radius, Len = rod length in mils; valid s ≫ r). For 10 mil vias, r = 5 mil, s = 80 mil the pair contributes 208 pH; moving to 40 mil pitch (vias off the cap edge) lowers it only slightly. Conclusion: bring opposite-current vias closer, but via loop is only part of the mount (Figure 5.27).

- **Ch5 §5.12 — surface-trace leg and capacitor body** — Microstrip approximation L = µ0·Len·h/w (h = trace height above cavity top, w = trace width, Len = length, all mils). h = 10, w = 10, Len = 30 mils → 435 pH per leg, 870 pH for equal legs on both sides. Capacitor body adds 870 pH for an 0603 assuming h = 10 + 20 = 30 mils (5–10 mil solder-fillet standoff plus plate depth; current crowds near the center, so 20 mils effective), w = 30, Len = 60. Total: 208 + 870 + 870 = 1948 pH ≈ **2 nH typical 0603 mount** (Figure 5.28). Package substrates with build-up layers and microvias, or multiple vias at minimum pitch under the terminals, reach **100 pH**.

- **Ch5 §5.12 — backside/BGA mount, reverse aspect ratio, five knobs** — Cap under the BGA on the back side is dominated by via loop through the board: 50 mil ball pitch, 5 mil via radius → 23 pH/mil, so 64 mil board = **1.5 nH**; compare against cavity spreading inductance plus BGA-via inductance for a top-side mount, since thicker boards favor the backside. Reverse aspect ratio caps (terminals on the long side, Figure 5.29) cut loop inductance: 0306 example with htrace 5, wtrace 60, Lentrace 10 mils, hcap 15, wcap 60, Lencap 30, via spacing 40, via diameter 10 gives 300 (traces) + 140 (body) + 100 (vias) = **540 pH**. But squares drop from 2 to 0.5 cuts ESR to **25%**, which can raise the parallel-resonance peak via lower damping/higher Q if loop inductance is not also reduced. Five knobs: short traces, wide traces, cavity close to the surface, reverse aspect ratio, multiple vias (Figures 5.30, 5.31).

- **Ch5 §5.12 — X2Y / interdigitated capacitors** — X2Y ("three-terminal") is an IDC: four capacitors in parallel inside one body with a via pattern allowing through-vias that do not block routing (Figure 5.32; terminals A and B tied to power). Parallel loop inductance: four 2 nH sections → **0.5 nH**. Advantages: parallel caps, parallel via pairs, mutual coupling between vias, and a larger via footprint that lowers spreading inductance; most of the gain comes from the via pattern. Measured (Figure 5.33): via-in-pad X2Y = **200 pH**, adding 20 mil surface traces → **900 pH**, a >4× penalty — small features dominate once mounting inductance is low.

- **Ch5 §5.13 — vendor S-parameter models are fixture models** — Vendor two-port S-parameters come from a series microstrip/VNA fixture with its own lead length and return-path position (Figure 5.34); the inductance is not intrinsic. Users commonly tie one port to the board pads and short the other to ground, then trust the result because "it goes to 3 GHz." Additional hazards: undisclosed "correction," corrupted files still published, and capacitance differing from the label (a 10 nF file extracted as **5.8 nF**; vendors measure at 100/120 Hz on an impedance bridge, irrelevant at MHz). Figures 5.35–5.37.

- **Ch5 §5.14 — extracting a series RLC from measured S-parameters** — Simulate the two-port with port 2 shorted to get one-port S11 (Figure 5.38, Keysight ADS), then Z(f) = Z0·(1 + S11)/(1 − S11). Extract C from the imaginary part at low frequency, ESL from the imaginary part in the inductive region, and ESR as the minimum |Z| at SRF. Worked: 47 nF 0402 → **C = 42.6 nF, ESL = 0.71 nH, ESR = 0.0298 Ω**, excellent fit (Figure 5.39). Use C and ESR in system simulation plus the application's own mounting inductance.

- **Ch5 §5.14 — why the flat-ESR RLC is pessimistic** — Real ESR is frequency dependent: dielectric loss falls with frequency, then skin-depth current redistribution raises it. Extracted Re(Z) (Figure 5.40) is minimum at **1–10 MHz**, roughly where skin effect starts and roughly the fixture SRF; above SRF the effective damping resistance is **>2×** the fitted ESR. Fitted ESR is therefore the minimum the cap ever shows — a figure of merit, not a damping predictor. The same redistribution also lowers inductance above SRF.

- **Ch5 §5.15 — distributed (lossy transmission line) capacitor model** — Visible only when mounting inductance ≪ 1 nH. A 1 µF cap on a low-inductance fixture: at 300 MHz the RLC model predicts **0.9 Ω** but measurement is **0.4 Ω**, implying intrinsic inductance at high frequency is **less than half** its SRF value (Figure 5.41). Physical picture: capacitive impedance falls with frequency so fewer plate pairs are needed, while magnetic field wants current shallow near the return; resistive plates push current deeper — the same equilibrium as skin depth (§4.9). Net effect: L falls, ESR rises with frequency — exactly the damping wanted at parallel resonance. Model is a ladder of ≤10 sections with a dissipation factor (X5R ≈ **0.01**); per-section C = Ccap/n, R = n·ESR (Figure 5.42).

- **Ch5 §5.15 — quarter-wave resonance and the 2.47 factor** — ESL from SRF = Lmount + Lcap, with Lcap = Lbottom (dielectric fill) + Lplates. The cap behaves as an open-ended transmission line: impedance minimum (SRF) at **quarter-wave** resonance, voltage maximum at the open (top) end. A single LC predicts resonance *below* the transmission-line quarter-wave frequency; matching them gives **Ltline = 2.47 × ESL_from_SRF** (Figure 5.43, 5.44). Conversion from RLC needs only Lbottom plus Ccap and ESR; Lmount must already be removed.

- **Ch5 §5.15 — fitting the model to vendor data, and the measurement trap** — Fit five parameters: Df **0.011**, Lmount **0.570 nH**, Lsect **0.03 nH**, Rsect **0.0028 Ω**, C **42.6 nF** (47 nF part, Figure 5.45) — most inductance is mount, so the true intrinsic loop is tiny. Best practice: extract the lossy-line parameters once per part, then re-run with the application's actual mounting inductance. Figure 5.46 (100 µF + 1 µF on low-inductance mounts with plane capacitance): RLC predicts peaks at **~3 MHz** (100 µF ∥ 1 µF) and **~60 MHz** (caps ∥ planes); measured peaks are only **20%** as high, at higher frequency and lower Q, and the lossy-line model tracks them. Neither model changes cavity resonances above 100 MHz. Two-port form suits caps embedded in package/substrate (die bumps to board balls), attenuating high-frequency power transfer. Never probe PDN noise on top of a decoupling cap: from the top the L and C are in **parallel** (resonant amplifier at SRF), from the board they are in **series** (noise sink); dismount the cap and probe the pads.

- **Ch5 §5.16 — the bottom line** — (1) Real caps are ideal only below ~**10 MHz**; mounting inductance changes everything above. (2) A simple RLC still matches real impedance remarkably well. (3) C dominates low frequency, L high frequency, ESR is the minimum at SRF. (4) N identical caps in parallel scale the single-cap profile. (5) Two different caps create a peak between their SRFs — the PDN's most important feature. (6) Reduce that peak by lowering ESL, bringing the two values closer, or raising ESR — use all three. (7) Controlled-ESR caps damp the parallel peak. (8) Mount inductance = traces + body + vias; reduce all three. (9) Mounted ESL < **4 nH** always achievable, < **2 nH** done well, < **0.5 nH** in good designs. (10) Below ~1 nH mounting inductance, transmission-line behavior appears: L falls and ESR rises with frequency, which helps Q.
