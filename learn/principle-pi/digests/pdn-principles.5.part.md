# L1 overview

- **Ch3 §3.11 (cont.) — Wire loop: fixture phase masquerading as R** — Final second-order model for the wire loop uses R = 0.005 Ω, L = 23 nH, Z0 = 50 Ω, TD = 42 ps (Figure 3.28). Measured and simulated impedances agree from DC to the ~600 MHz peak-and-drop caused by the SMA launch transmission lines. A pure RL model would predict impedance rising linearly forever. The extracted "resistance" rises with frequency even though the model's R element is constant: each fixture TL adds a negative phase shift to S21, which is virtually the impedance, rotating the ~90° inductive phase toward 0° and thereby growing Re{Z}. No skin-depth effect is needed to explain it. A shunt capacitor across the loop halves is only needed if the fixture model still leaves a residual at high frequency; up to ~300 MHz a plain RL sufficed.
- **Ch3 §3.12 — Measuring via inductance** — Two-layer board, through-connected 50 Ω microstrip between two SMAs; a 50 mil hole drilled mid-trace with a 50 mil wire soldered from signal trace to bottom return plane (Figure 3.29) shorts the line. Two-port S21 converted to Z (Figures 3.30–3.31) again shows "L falling and R rising with frequency." Best-fit second-order model: R = 0.0007 Ω, L = 0.28 nH, Z0 = 50 Ω, TD = 210 ps — reproduces data over the full 2 GHz bandwidth (Figure 3.32). Sanity checks from geometry: ρ = 0.7 × 10⁻⁶ Ω-in, Len = 0.064 in, r = 0.025 in give R_DC ≈ 0.02 mΩ and L_total ≈ 0.29 nH (partial self-inductance of a rod). Extracted R is ~0.7 mΩ, ~35× high: partly VNA noise floor, partly skin effect — at 10 MHz δ ≈ 0.8 mil, so the conducting annulus is 2πr × 0.0008 in = 0.00013 in² instead of 0.002 in², giving 15× more resistance, within 2× of the extracted value. The true √f trend is masked because the fixture artifact grows as f².
- **Ch3 §3.13 — Small MLCC on a board** — Intrinsic capacitance cannot be separated from mounting; only "capacitor plus its mounting inductance" is measurable. Fixture is a four-layer board with planes fed from edge SMAs into a central capacitor region, with movable pads (X2Y Attenuators, Figure 3.33). Measured S21/|Z| (Figure 3.34) matches a series RLC: R = 0.01 Ω, C = 175 nF, L = 0.41 nH, but only to ~400 MHz; beyond that the fixture must be added (Figure 3.35–3.36). Fixture = ~0.7 in of interconnect each side, modeled as a TL with length fixed and only Z0 fit — 3.5 Ω worked, plausible for a wide plane. Extracted C is ~25% below the 220 nF nameplate. R is ESR (dominates damping of parallel resonances); L is ESL, non-intrinsic and the single most important capacitor design term because it sets the minimum capacitor count. Via-in-pad mounting gave 0.2 nH; only 20 mil of surface trace between pad and via raised it to 0.9 nH (Figure 3.37).
- **Ch3 §3.14 — Measuring on-die capacitance** — Two-port method scales to the huge dynamic range of on-die C (81 nF class) because it is an insertion-loss measurement, not a reflection measurement. Three configurations: bare-die C4 bumps, one package ball pair, two package ball pairs (Figures 3.38, 3.43–3.44). VNA calibrated to microprobe tips; residual tip non-reproducibility ≈ 50 Ω TL with TD ≈ 1 ps, i.e. ~10 mils of placement/distortion. Sweep 300 kHz–3 GHz. Two extraction routes: (a) optimize a series-RLC model to measured S21, (b) interpret Re{Z} as R and Im{Z} as C or L algebraically at every frequency. Bare die: C = 81.5 nF, L = 2 pH, R = 70 mΩ; L hits the noise floor above 1 GHz. From one ball pair: C = 79.5 nF, R = 24 mΩ, L = 0.28 nH at SRF. From two ball pairs: L = 0.12 nH, so the 0.16 nH delta is the ball/via/spreading inductance of one path; each probe path contributes ~100 pH, fit with TD = 2 ps. Two cardinal artifacts: (1) fixture TD rotates imaginary into real; (2) spatial attenuation / transimpedance / insertion loss across separated ports makes Z look artificially low. Measured −50 dB port-to-port on the die. Real consequence: the on-die distributed R that would damp the ~100 MHz parallel resonance is shorted out by the much lower-resistance package planes, so 100 MHz gets less damping than the bump measurement implies; below 100 MHz ESR of the resonant loop is set by the package, above ~500 MHz by the on-die grid. Above 1 GHz only 3-D models of the die/package connection distribution are trustworthy.
- **Ch3 §3.15 — Bottom line (11 points)** — Impedance has two correct definitions (V/I, and reflection coefficient in the frequency domain). One-port VNA is accurate only above ~0.1 Ω; two-port is the Kelvin-style workaround and reaches very low Z at high frequency. To first order Z ≈ 25 Ω × S21 for a shunt low-Z DUT. The two artifacts to control are fixture TD phase shift (de-embed or model it in the topology) and spatial attenuation / insertion loss (makes Z and L look smaller than real; several products have published such false "low impedance" claims, at die, package, and PCB level alike). Same technique yields ESL/ESR/C for discrete capacitors and C for on-die capacitance; die-through-package interpretation needs 3-D simulation.

- `#L4352-L4390` — Low-frequency Z is identical (set by on-die C) from bumps or balls; high-frequency Z is a composite: package planes high-L/low-R in parallel with die low-L/high-R, position dependent. Full interpretation needs a 3-D field solver, but same-ball-pair probing is dominated by ball/via L and R while two-ball-pair probing emphasizes the multilayer planar composite; port selection and relative position strongly change the result. Figure 3.45 compares same-ball-pair data to a simple RLC.
- `#L4391-L4426` — Good low-frequency fit gives 79.5 nF for this rail (vs 80.5 nF for the different die above); near SRF, 24 mΩ and 0.28 nH fit well. Above ~100 MHz measured L falls with frequency, suggesting the simple RLC misses the low-R package planes shunting the high-R/low-L die grid; more current in the die path, or a shorter on-die path crossing fewer shunt resistors, both raise ESR with frequency. - `#L4427-L4464` — Extracted C agrees at low frequency; extracted L drops with frequency (die shunting the inductance or a shorter current path); low-frequency R variation is consistent with on-die insulation dielectric loss. Adding a 1 ps TL fixture model shows how much of Im{Z} could be rotated into Re{Z}; the rise starting at 10 MHz is not a fixture artifact, and L and C are barely fixture-sensitive; a probe tip scrub difference of ~2 ps between cal and DUT could explain the apparent R drop at 1 GHz.
- `#L4465-L4496` — Two-ball-pair probing removes ball and initial-via series L, some package spreading L and R, and probes the package cavity shunted by the die's distributed cap (Figure 3.47). Low-frequency Z is still the on-die C; SRF effective L is 0.28 nH for one ball pair vs 0.12 nH for two, the 0.16 nH delta being balls, vias, and package-plane spreading inductance. Extracted values in Figure 3.48.
- `#L4497-L4525` — Two-ball-pair L varies less with frequency and R is lower but still rises; consistent with the die's lower-L/higher-R grid shunting the package cavity, with extra attenuation from spreading L and R between the probes. Each probe path contributes ~100 pH (160 pH per path plus ~1 ps residual), fit with fixture TD raised to 2 ps; that TD rotates imaginary into real and explains the >1 GHz apparent resistance, so Re{Z} above 1 GHz cannot be read as real resistance, and the ~600 MHz glitches may be artifacts.
- `#L4526-L4564` — ESR of the resonance loop is package-dominated below 100 MHz (where on-die-C/package-L parallel resonances live) and on-die-grid-dominated above 500 MHz; hence less damping at 100 MHz than the bump measurement implies, because die circuits traverse series and shunt resistance to reach the supporting on-die cap. Above 1 GHz only 3-D models capturing the die/package connection distribution suffice. The same spatial-attenuation trap appears when ports span a package bump array (inductive plus resistive filtering) and at PCB level, where discrete capacitors between widely spaced ports attenuate port 1's signal — several products have claimed artificially low Z/L from exactly this.
- `#L4565-L4594` — PCB-level restatement of the insertion-loss artifact ("the PDN cannot deliver power at this artificially low impedance"), the two general two-port interpretation problems (uncalibrated probe extension rotating real↔imaginary, and spatial attenuation shrinking the apparent Z), and the Tip consolidating both.
- `#L4595-L4654` — §3.15 The bottom line: 11 numbered takeaways — two definitions of impedance; one-port limited above ~0.1 Ω; few ideal elements match real structures; two-port as four-lead Kelvin; Z ≈ 25 Ω × S21; de-embed or model fixture TD; insertion loss can shrink apparent Z; two-port yields C/ESR/ESL of discrete caps; on-die C by RLC fit or per-frequency algebra; die-through-package needs 3-D distributed simulation.
