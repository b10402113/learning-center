# L1 overview

- **Scope and definition of the PDN** — The power distribution network spans everything between the VRM and the on-die Vdd–Vss rails: interconnects (mostly inductive), intentional energy storage (capacitive), and loss mechanisms (damping). Power integrity = quality of power seen by on-die circuits; the book's focus is noise on the core Vdd rail caused by core switching current, i.e. "self-aggression," not I/O switching noise or ground bounce.
- **Why "power integrity" is a bad umbrella term** — The preface enumerates seven distinct problems often lumped under PI/SI: (1) Vcc–Vss noise from I/O switching/ground bounce, (2) VRM output noise from changing load impedance, (3) signal distortion through return-path discontinuities, (4) rail noise polluting board PDN interconnects, (5) PDN-to-Vdd cross talk, (6) PDN-to-I/O-rail cross talk, (7) PDN-to-signal cross talk. Each has a different root cause and different fixes; blindly generalizing one recommendation is dangerous. Board decoupling capacitors fix Vdd core noise but seldom fix cavity/return-plane bounce, and their parallel resonances can even increase cavity-to-signal cross talk.
- **Robust-by-design methodology (4 elements)** — (a) start from established best practices, (b) understand essential principles = applied Maxwell's equations, (c) identify common problems and root causes, (d) use analysis tools to explore the cost–performance–risk–schedule trade space. PDN failures are hard to reproduce (specific microcode × specific conditions), so quality cannot be "tested in"; a robust PDN must be designed in. "It depends" is answered only by putting in the numbers.
- **Central figure of merit: target impedance** — Design goal is an impedance profile that stays below the target impedance across frequency, at acceptable cost/risk/schedule. Target impedances for real PDNs run from 1 Ω down to below 1 mΩ, which motivates the specialty low-impedance measurement techniques of Chapter 3.
- **Chapter spine (10 chapters)** — 1 Engineering the PDN (impedance profile, target impedance) · 2 Essential principles of impedance (series/parallel RLC, SPICE as impedance analyzer, impedance matrix, transient response) · 3 Measuring low impedance (V/I, reflection/TDR, VNA limits, four-point Kelvin, two-port, fixture de-embedding, on-die C) · 4 Inductance (B field, quasi-static, internal/external, skin depth, loop/partial/mutual, closed-form approximations, 1 nH/inch-class rules of thumb, S-parameter extraction) · 5 MLCC integration (RLC models, parallel resonance between two capacitor values, PRF peak, ESR models, mounting inductance, vendor S-parameters) · 6 Planes and capacitors (parallel-plate C, fringe-field C, power puddles, spreading inductance, cavity modal resonances) · 7 Return-plane transitions (cavity noise, shorting vias, DC-blocking capacitors, damping, controlled-ESR) · 8 PDN ecology (on-die C + package lead L peak = "Bandini Mountain," OPD, FDTIM, bulk capacitor + VRM) · 9 Transient currents (clock-edge impulse, step, square wave at resonance, q-factor, rogue waves, clock/power gating) · 10 Spreadsheet-based PDN resonance calculator (PRC), three-peak PDN, simulation-to-measurement correlation.
- **Named concepts that recur** — Bandini Mountain (peak from on-die capacitance resonating with package lead inductance); spreading inductance as the most important plane property; claim that plane cavity resonances are "not important at all" for on-die power quality (Chapter 6); five general tactics for reducing peak impedance from capacitor combinations (Chapter 5); FDTIM = Frequency Domain Target Impedance Method for choosing capacitor values and minimum count (Chapter 8); three transient current waveforms each stimulating a different PDN feature, with any two of {impedance, current, voltage} giving the third (Chapter 9).
- **Book mechanics** — Prentice Hall "Simplified" series: minimal math formalism, equations used as shorthand for which terms matter, approximations to calibrate engineering judgment, figures with extended captions, boxed TIPS, a 10-point "The Bottom Line" per chapter, and the PDN Resonance Calculator spreadsheet hosted at informit.com/title/9780132735551 and www.beTheSignal.com. Authors: Larry D. Smith (Qualcomm; previously Altera, Sun, IBM) and Eric Bogatin (Teledyne LeCroy Signal Integrity Academy, CU Boulder), ~70 years combined experience. ISBN 978-0-13-273555-1, 2017.

Asks whether cavity noise from signals crossing planes and I/O-switching noise on Vcc/Vss ("switching noise"/"ground bounce", coupled through common package lead inductance) are PI or SI. Notes board decoupling helps Vdd core noise but seldom cavity noise, and can worsen cavity-to-signal cross talk via parallel resonance. States the focus: Vdd-rail "self-aggression" from core transient current.

Argues for naming the specific problem/root cause; many published, conference, or "favorite uncle" recommendations are wrong or contradictory because they were derived for one problem and over-generalized.
- `#L542-L592` — "MEETING THE CHALLENGE OF ROBUST PDN DESIGN": failures appear at the worst time and are hard to reproduce (specific microcode + conditions), so quality cannot be tested in. Some PDNs are robust with only a low-impedance VRM; others need specific capacitor values in specific positions plus restricted microcode. Every PDN is unique (performance, chip features, microcode, cost/risk/schedule), so copying someone else's rules fails. "It depends" is answered by defining the problem and putting in numbers. Four-element design process (best practices → essential principles/Maxwell → problems and root causes → tools for trade-space exploration) aimed at "getting it right the first time"; goal is acceptable performance at acceptable cost, risk, and schedule.
