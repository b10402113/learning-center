# L1 overview

- **Ch6 §6.1 — the key role of planes** — A cavity is any adjacent plane pair, regardless of DC voltage; it is a waveguide that traps fields, so field edges "echo off the edges." PDN noise in the planes is set by transient device current and plane impedance. Low frequency: lumped parallel-plate capacitance; mid frequency: loop/spreading inductance; high frequency: modal resonances from propagation speed and boundary conditions. Design rule: do everything to lower plane impedance — adjacent planes, thinnest practical dielectric, cavity near board surface (lower capacitor/package mounting inductance). Figure 6.1: 4 in × 6 in planes, 64 mil FR4, three impedance regions (capacitor / inductor / transmission-line-like).

- **Ch6 §6.2 — parallel-plate capacitance** — C = ε0·Dk·A/h, ε0 = 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m = 0.227 pF/inch, A = overlap, h = dielectric thickness. For FR4 (Dk = 4.2) this collapses to C/A ≈ 0.95/h(mils) nF/inch², i.e. ~1 nF/inch² at 1 mil falling inversely with thickness. Example: 3 mil → 0.3 nF/inch²; 10 in × 10 in → 30 nF. Assumes fringe fields are negligible, which fails at low aspect ratio.

- **Ch6 §6.3 — fringe field capacitance, stand-alone cavity** — 2D field solver gives total C from TD and Z0: C = TD/(Z0) form with c = 11.8 in/ns; microstrip over an infinite return is twice the line-to-image capacitance (two series capacitances). Ctotal = Cpp + 4 × Cff. Fringe is ~0.16 pF/inch of edge in air, independent of width above aspect ratio ~5 (w = 2–200 mils, h' = 5 mils, Figure 6.3) and independent of dielectric spacing at fixed aspect ratio 10 for h = 1–50 mils (Figure 6.4). For air, error of the parallel-plate model is 20% at a 10:1 aspect ratio, dropping to 7% with FR4 (Figure 6.5).

- **Ch6 §6.4 — fringe fields in power puddles** — When planes are islands in a board, dielectric extends past the cavity and increases fringe capacitance; the cross section looks like microstrip. With Dk = 4 the parallel-plate error is ~20% even at aspect ratio 10, and 1% requires aspect ratio 50 — e.g. 6 mil dielectric needs a 300 mil (0.3 in) square puddle (Figure 6.6). Parallel plate is the standard Dk-measurement method: C = −1/(2πf·imag(Z)) from S21, then Dk back-solved. Worked two-layer board 4.53 in × 6.3 in × 64 mil, aspect ratio 70, quasi-static limit 1/20 λ ≈ 50 MHz, first dip 500 MHz, departure from lumped behaviour at 100 MHz, Dk ≈ 5 below 1 MHz falling to 4.3 at 100 MHz (material dispersion, Figure 6.7).

- **Ch6 §6.5 — loop inductance of long, narrow cavities** — Lloop = Lsq × n, Lsq = µ0·h = 32 pH/mil, µ0 = 32 nH/inch; uniform current density assumed. Example 30 in × 0.3 in, 1 mil dielectric (aspect ratios 300:1 and 100:1) gives 3.23 nH, matching the 3D solver's 0.0203 Ω at 1 MHz via L[nH] = 159 × Z(1 MHz). Quasi-static limit ~10 MHz; one wavelength fits at 6/30 GHz = 200 MHz, and the lumped model is good to 1/20 λ. A via point contact fans out to uniform current within a few trace widths (Figures 6.8, 6.9).

- **Ch6 §6.6 — spreading inductance in wide cavities** — With small contacts the current spreads radially, so loop inductance per length falls with distance; this is spreading inductance, highest near the contact where current density is highest. Three equivalent derivations for a center-driven circular cavity: (1) via partial self-inductance from Ampère's law, (2) coax section with the via as center conductor and the cavity rim as return, (3) integration of the radial wavefront's instantaneous impedance. Result L = (µ0·h/2π)·ln(Rcavity/Rvia) — independent of Dk, log-dependent on geometry. Instantaneous impedance Z ∝ h/R, so dielectric thickness is the dominant lever. Example: 25 mil clearance in a 1 in, 1 mil cavity gives only ~118 pH; spreading is ≲1 square of sheet inductance (1 square when cavity/contact ≈ 500, e.g. 25 mil via and 12 in board).

- **Ch6 §6.7 — extracting spreading inductance from a 3D solver** — Open cavity (no shorting wall), return path is displacement current through the distributed plane capacitance, so spreading L sits in series with cavity C. Lumped LC is valid only below 1/20 λ laterally. Circular cavity D = 1 in, via 25 mil, h = 1 mil, Dk = 4: parallel plate is accurate to better than 0.1% at aspect ratio 1000, and the series LC model tracks the full-wave result up to a fraction of the ~7 GHz modal resonance (Figure 6.12). The first dip is the L-C interaction, not a modal resonance, and moves with probe/contact size. Square 5 in cavity, 50 mil contact, 1 mil: circular-equivalent estimate matches the solver's 27.8 pH (Figures 6.12–6.14).

- **Ch6 §6.8 — lumped series and parallel self-resonant frequency** — Spreading inductance is in series with cavity capacitance because current must pass the high-inductance contact region to reach the high-capacitance perimeter. C = ε0·Dk·πD²/4h, L = (µ0·h/2π)·ln(D/d), fres = 1/(2π√(LC)). 55% of the inductance lies within the first 250 mils while only 1% of the capacitance does; 50% of capacitance needs 1750 mils, by which 93% of inductance is already counted (Figure 6.13) — which is why the lumped series LC works. Shorting the center and probing the edge flips the same L and C into a parallel resonance at the identical frequency (Figure 6.15).

- **Ch6 §6.9 — features of the series LC resonance** — fres is independent of dielectric thickness: C ∝ 1/h and L ∝ h, so h cancels; 10 mil and 50 mil cavities resonate identically (Figure 6.16). fres rises slightly faster than 1/D: a 5× diameter reduction (5 in → 1 in) raises it 5.8× analytically and 5.9× in the solver, 0.213→1.24 GHz predicted vs 0.226→1.34 GHz simulated (Figure 6.17). Rule of thumb for D/d ≈ 500: fres ≈ 1 GHz / D(inches). FR4 special case uses a 0.72 coefficient at D = 5 in.

- **Ch6 §6.10 — spreading inductance vs source contact location** — Contact position changes current crowding, not capacitance. Center spreads into four quadrants; an edge contact uses two, giving ~2× the spreading inductance; a corner uses one, giving ~4×. Measured from the series-resonance shift (Figure 6.19). Low-frequency capacitive impedance is identical everywhere on the cavity, but above the series resonance the "impedance of the cavity" is not unique — it varies over the surface. Intrinsic modal resonances (from ~600 MHz here) are independent of probe location.

- **Ch6 §6.11 — spreading inductance between two contacts** — A single contact spreading into the whole cavity is the minimum series inductance any current sees; any other distribution only adds. Current flowing between two contacts (e.g. one capacitor and one power/ground via pair in a package) has much higher current density near both contacts, so the two-point spreading inductance exceeds the one-point value. Figure 6.20 shows the top-plane current density for that case (section continues past this chunk).
