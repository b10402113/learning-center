# L1 overview

- **6.1 The key role of planes** — A power/ground plane pair is a *cavity*: any adjacent pair of planes that traps fields and echoes them off its edges. Its impedance has three regimes — capacitive at low frequency, inductive in the middle, and modal/transmission-line-like at high frequency — so the design rule is simply to minimize plane impedance.
- **6.2 Parallel plate capacitance** — At low frequency the cavity is a parallel-plate capacitor, C = ε0·Dk·A/h; with FR4 (Dk ≈ 4.2) this reduces to about 1 nF/inch² per mil of dielectric, so 3 mils gives 0.3 nF/inch² and a 10-inch-square pair gives ~30 nF.
- **6.3 Fringe field capacitance** — The parallel-plate model ignores edge fields; a 2D field solver on an image-line model shows each edge adds about 0.16 pF/inch independent of spacing above an aspect ratio of ~5, giving a total-capacitance correction and a relative error that falls with aspect ratio (20% in air vs 7% in FR4 at 10:1).
- **6.4 Fringe fields in power puddles** — When planes are islands embedded in a dielectric slab, the fringe fields extend outside the cavity and the parallel-plate approximation can still be ~20% off even at aspect ratio 10; 1% needs roughly 50:1, so Dk extraction from a measured cavity works only when the structure is short (<⅟₂₀ λ) and high aspect ratio.
- **6.5 Loop inductance of long, narrow cavities** — The inductance figure of merit is *sheet inductance* Lsq = 32 pH/mil × h; the loop inductance of a rectangular cut is Lsq times the number of squares down its length, verified on a 30 in × 0.3 in × 1 mil strip where simulation and the uniform-current approximation both give 3.23 nH.
- **6.6 Spreading inductance in wide cavities** — When current launches from a small contact into a wide cavity it fans out radially, so the loop inductance seen is the *spreading inductance*. Three equivalent derivations (Ampere's law on the via, a coax section with the via as centre conductor, and integrating the radial wavefront) all give the same log-of-radius result.
- **6.7 Extracting spreading inductance** — A full-wave 3D solver's impedance matches a simple series-LC model — cavity capacitance in series with spreading inductance — up to a fraction of the first modal resonance; a 1-inch circular cavity agrees to ~7 GHz, and a 5-inch square cavity gives 27.8 pH, close to the circular estimate.
- **6.8 Series and parallel self-resonant frequency** — Probing the open cavity at its centre produces a series-LC *dip*; shorting the centre and probing the edge produces a parallel-LC *peak* at the same frequency. Both come from the same L·C product and are distinct from the higher-frequency modal resonances.
- **6.9 Features of the series LC resonance** — Because h enters both C (∝1/h) and L (∝h), the self-resonant frequency is essentially independent of dielectric thickness; it scales slightly faster than inversely with cavity diameter, yielding the rule-of-thumb fres ≈ 1 GHz / (cavity diameter in inches).
- **6.10 Contact location** — Spreading inductance depends on where current enters: a centre launch uses four quadrants, an edge launch two (≈2× inductance), and a corner one (≈4×). This shifts the series-resonance dip but not the intrinsic modal peaks, and above that dip "the cavity impedance" is not a unique surface-independent quantity.
- **6.11 Spreading inductance between two points** — For current flowing between two vias (capacitor to BGA), the point-to-point spreading inductance is roughly twice the single-point spreading inductance, ≈2 squares of sheet inductance, and depends only on the log of spacing and contact diameter — closeness helps, but softly.
- **6.12 Capacitor–cavity interactions** — Mounting a real capacitor (series RLC, e.g. 2 nH, 100 nF, 25 mΩ) across a cavity creates a *parallel* resonance between the capacitor's mounting inductance and the cavity capacitance, so the combined profile shows both the capacitor SRF and a new high-impedance peak.
- **6.13 When capacitor location matters** — If spreading inductance ≪ mounting inductance, the cavity is *transparent* and capacitor position is irrelevant (typical of thin dielectric and ~2 nH mounting). If spreading inductance dominates — typically h > 10 mils — position matters, though the log dependence means moving a capacitor from 2 in to 0.2 in only cuts ~1.22 nH to ~0.75 nH.
- **6.14 Saturating the spreading inductance** — Adding capacitors reduces parallel inductance as 1/n until the equivalent mounting inductance drops below the cavity spreading inductance; beyond that point the inductance saturates and more capacitors buy nothing. Distributing capacitors uniformly around the package and thinning the cavity are the main levers.
- **6.15 Modal resonances and transmission-line behaviour** — Above the LC dip the cavity behaves like a 1D transmission line: reflections from the open ends build standing waves when the round-trip phase is an integer number of cycles, i.e. a half-wave multiple fits between the boundaries, giving integer-spaced resonant frequencies.
- **6.16 Input impedance and modal resonances** — Input impedance of an open line peaks at the modal frequencies (S11 phase = integer cycles), so the impedance profile is a direct map of the modes. Peak locations are intrinsic; peak heights depend on characteristic impedance, terminations, and loss.
- **6.17 Modal resonances and attenuation** — Conductor and dielectric loss damp the reflected waves that cause resonances. Dielectric attenuation depends only on Dk/Df, not geometry; conductor attenuation is independent of line width but scales inversely with dielectric thickness, so thin dielectric both lowers Z0 and raises damping — the most effective way to suppress high-frequency peaks.
- **6.18 Cavity modes in two dimensions** — A plane cavity supports modes propagating along X, along Y, and diagonally, with resonant frequencies set by m and n half-waves per side and the dielectric constant. Mode frequencies are intrinsic, but whether a mode appears in a measurement or is excited depends on where the signal is injected.
- **6.19 Transfer impedance** — Self-impedance always carries the spreading-inductance artifact; the impedance matrix's off-diagonal *transfer impedance* is a cleaner probe. Measuring from opposite ends of a through via removes the via loop inductance and directly reveals the few-tens-of-pH cavity spreading inductance.
- **6.20 The bottom line** — Ten summary points: three impedance regions, capacitance for Dk metrology, LC-dip interpretation, modal dominance at high frequency, sub-0.1 Ω cavity impedance, current-density-dependent spreading inductance, log spacing dependence, capacitor/cavity parallel resonance, dimension-only modal frequencies, and thin dielectric as the best damping lever.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L62` — Chapter 6 opening and 6.1: planes connect VRM and capacitors to the loads; noise depends on transient current and plane impedance. Defines the *cavity* as any adjacent plane pair that traps fields like a waveguide, and previews the lumped-C / spreading-L / modal-resonance picture plus the guideline to minimize impedance with adjacent, thin-dielectric planes near the board surface.
- `#L63-L96` — The "free guideline" cost argument and the need to model planes. Figure 6.1 simulates a 4 in × 6 in, 64 mil FR4 cavity from a centre contact and shows the three impedance regions: capacitor at low frequency, inductor in the middle, and sharp modal/transmission-line transitions at high frequency.
- `#L97-L139` — 6.2 parallel-plate capacitance: C = ε0·Dk·A/h with ε0 = 0.227 pF/inch. For FR4 (Dk = 4.2) the rule becomes ~1 nF/inch² at 1 mil, so 3 mils → 0.3 nF/inch², and a 10-inch-square pair → ~30 nF; fringing fields are neglected.
- `#L140-L224` — 6.3 fringe-field capacitance: compare field-solver capacitance with parallel plate. Introduces the image-line model (a signal h′ above an infinite return plane equals half the capacitance to its image 2h away) and derives capacitance from characteristic impedance and time delay, C = TD/(Z0·√Dk)/c, then splits it into parallel-plate and per-edge fringe terms.
- `#L225-L312` — Polar SI9000 sweep of line width 2→200 mils shows fringe capacitance per edge saturates at ~0.16 pF/inch above aspect ratio ~5 and is independent of dielectric thickness at fixed aspect ratio (Figure 6.4). Total cavity capacitance = parallel plate + four edges; relative error drops with aspect ratio, 20% in air vs 7% in FR4 at 10:1 (Figure 6.5).
- `#L313-L383` — 6.4 power *puddles*: plane islands embedded in dielectric, where fringing extends into the surrounding slab and looks like microstrip. The 2D-solver error stays ~20% at aspect ratio 10 and needs ~50:1 for 1% — e.g. 300 mils on a side for a 6 mil dielectric. Notes this is the basis of laminate Dk measurement.
- `#L384-L457` — Extracting C from impedance (C = −1/(2πf·imag Z)) then Dk from C·h/(ε0·A). A 4.53 in × 6.3 in, 64 mil two-layer board is measured by two-port S21: quasi-static validity to ~50 MHz (⅟₂₀ λ at 6.3 in), aspect ratio 70 giving ~1% error, and Dk falling from ~5 below 1 MHz to ~4.3 at 100 MHz (material dispersion, Figure 6.7).
- `#L458-L546` — 6.5 loop inductance of long narrow cavities: Lloop = Lsq × number of squares with µ0 = 32 nH/inch = 32 pH/mil, so Lsq = 32 pH/mil × h. A 30 in × 0.3 in gap-1 mil strip is predicted at 3.23 nH and confirmed by a 3D solver extracting 0.0203 Ω at 1 MHz; lumped model valid to ~10 MHz.
- `#L547-L700` — 6.6 spreading inductance: in a wide cavity current fans out radially from a small contact, so inductance per length falls with radius and the total is the *spreading inductance*. Sets up shorted-wall vs open-cavity configurations and the three equivalent calculation routes — Ampere's law / via partial self-inductance, coax section with the via as centre conductor, and integrating the radial wavefront.
- `#L701-L800` — Non-uniform current means only a 3D solver is generally accurate; a quasistatic solver suffices at low frequency. For a central circular contact the radial wavefront sees an instantaneous impedance derived from per-length capacitance of an annulus (dC = ε0·Dk·2πR·dR/h), giving Z ∝ h/R and dL = Z·dTD.
- `#L801-L908` — Integrating dL from the via edge to the cavity rim gives Lspread = (µ0·h/2π)·ln(Rcavity/Rvia); a 1 mil FR4 cavity 1 inch from a 25 mil contact gives Z well below 1 Ω. Expressed in diameters and related to sheet inductance, the result is ~1 square for a 500:1 cavity-to-contact ratio and less for smaller boards.
- `#L909-L1008` — 6.7 validation: with no shorting wall, displacement current closes the loop and the cavity is modelled as spreading inductance in series with capacitance. A 1-inch, 1 mil, Dk = 4 cavity with 25 mil contact matches the full-wave solver to ~7 GHz (Figure 6.12); the first dip is the LC resonance (probe-dependent) while the peaks are intrinsic modes. A 5-inch square, 50 mil contact gives 27.8 pH from the solver vs the circular estimate.
- `#L1009-L1105` — 6.8 the LC dip and peak: spreading L is in series with cavity C because most L sits near the contact and most C near the rim. Derives fres from L and C for circular geometry; compares with the ¼-wave cavity resonance and shows the coefficient (1.11 for 1 inch, 0.72 for 5 inches) depends on the contact diameter.
- `#L1106-L1156` — Worked 5-inch, 10 mil, Dk = 4, 30 mil-contact cavity with computed C and L, overlaid on the 3D solver trace (Figure 6.14). Shorting the centre and probing two edges turns the same LC into a parallel resonance at the same frequency (Figure 6.15).
- `#L1157-L1227` — 6.9 why the LC model works: h cancels from the L·C product so fres is independent of dielectric thickness (10 vs 50 mils, Figure 6.16); fres rises slightly faster than 1/D (5 in→1 in gives 5.8–5.9×, Figures 6.17), yielding the shortcut fres ≈ 1 GHz / diameter(inches) for a ~500:1 contact ratio.
- `#L1228-L1309` — 6.10 contact position: centre, edge, and corner launches give one, two, and four quadrants of spreading current, so spreading inductance rises ~2× at an edge and ~4× at a corner, shifting the dip lower while the intrinsic modal peaks stay fixed. Low-frequency capacitance is probe-independent, but above the dip "cavity impedance" varies over the surface.
- `#L1310-L1404` — 6.11 point-to-point spreading inductance (capacitor to BGA vias) is larger than single-point because current densities peak at both contacts. A 10 in, 10 mil cavity with 0.25 in and 2.5 in spacing gives 2.55 mΩ and 4.0 mΩ at 1 MHz, i.e. ~0.4 and ~0.64 nH, both ≈2 squares of the 320 pH/sq sheet inductance.
- `#L1405-L1549` — Current spreads to a region ~2× the spacing before dying out, so Lpoint-point ≈ k·Lsq·ln(...) with k ≈ 2 fitted to solvers for 10 mil and 50 mil contacts (Figure 6.23). Ten-times spacing raises inductance less than 2×; thickness is first-order, spacing and contact diameter only logarithmic — hence multiple vias per pad matter.
- `#L1550-L1618` — 6.12 a mounted capacitor is a series RLC (SRF ≈ 1/(2π√(L·C)), ~10 MHz for 2 nH and 100 nF). In parallel with the cavity it adds a parallel resonance between its mounting inductance and the cavity capacitance (Figure 6.24); the mounted-cap dip near 10 MHz includes mounting plus spreading inductance, while the ~100 MHz dip is the port's own spreading inductance.
- `#L1619-L1720` — 6.13 the transparency test: case 1, Lmounting ≫ Lspreading (long traces, thin cavity) makes the cavity transparent and location irrelevant; for s = 1 in, d = 10 mils the log term is 5.3, giving ~54 pH/mil × h, so a 2 mil cavity adds only ~0.1 nH against a 2 nH mount (Figure 6.25).
- `#L1721-L1796` — Case 2, Lspreading ≫ Lmounting: a 20 mil cavity gives 1.22 nH at 2 in and 0.75 nH at 0.2 in — closer is better but only logarithmically (Figure 6.26). Derives the figure of merit (~4·32 pH/mil·h), shows >10 mils means a non-transparent cavity, and notes a 5-square, 2 mil power trace already adds 320 pH.
- `#L1797-L1872` — 6.14 saturation: n capacitors reduce inductance as 1/n until that falls below the cavity spreading inductance, after which adding parts does nothing. Uniform distribution around the package spreads current; the annular formula with a = 0.25 in, b = 1 in, 10 mils gives ~71 pH, and the three knobs are thinner cavity, larger pad radius a, and smaller b.
- `#L1873-L1994` — 6.15 modal resonances: an open-ended line reflects with no phase change; when the round-trip phase (Δθ = 2·TD·f) is an integer number of cycles, reflected waves add coherently and voltage builds (Figure 6.28). Resonant frequencies are intrinsic; only excitation and amplitude depend on the source and boundary conditions.
- `#L1995-L2045` — With equal terminations at both ends the one-way length is an integer number of half-wavelengths, so f = n·c/(2·Len·√Dk); a 6-inch FR4 line resonates at 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 GHz… (Figure 6.29). Unequal terminations add a half-cycle, moving resonances to odd quarter-wave multiples (¼, ¾, 5/4 …).
- `#L2046-L2122` — 6.16 input impedance: Zport = Z0·(1+S11)/(1−S11); an open 50 Ω line with S11 phase = integer cycles peaks at the modal frequencies (Figure 6.30). Peak frequencies are set by geometry, but peak heights depend on Z0, terminations, and loss; high impedance means small currents produce large voltages.
- `#L2123-L2202` — 6.17 loss: conductor and dielectric attenuation are frequency dependent, so multiple reflections weaken and peaks flatten, eventually collapsing to Z0 (Figure 6.31). Atotal = Aconductor + Adielectric; dielectric attenuation depends only on Dk and Df — all geometry cancels — and scales with frequency via dipole rotation.
- `#L2203-L2268` — Conductor attenuation with skin effect scales as RLen/Z0 and is independent of line width (both scale together) but inversely proportional to dielectric thickness. Thin dielectric thus both lowers Z0 and increases damping, dramatically shrinking high-frequency peaks (Figure 6.33); higher Dk also helps but less than thinner h.
- `#L2269-L2390` — 6.18 two-dimensional modes: propagation along X, Y, and diagonally gives fmn = (c/(2√Dk))·√((m/a)² + (n/b)²); a 6-inch FR4 square resonates at 0.98, 1.39, 1.96 GHz… (m,n) = (1,0),(1,1),(2,0) (Figure 6.34). Mode frequencies are intrinsic; only how strongly a probe excites them varies (Figure 6.35).
- `#L2391-L2439` — Centre injection excites only modes with even m and n: odd half-wave counts produce a null at the centre where reflected and incident waves cancel (Figure 6.36). Hence a centre-driven 6-inch cavity shows only the (2,0), (2,2), (4,0) peaks, while a corner probe sees all modes (Figure 6.37).
- `#L2440-L2536` — 6.19 transfer impedance: the impedance matrix V = Z·I defines self-impedances Zii (one-port) and transfer impedances Zij (voltage at j per current at i). Self-impedance is always coloured by the port's spreading inductance; Z21 is the impedance *shared* along the radial current path and is a cleaner measurement because probe residue is not in the transfer path.
- `#L2537-L2667` — Application: a 3-inch, 10 mil cavity with a centre port and three off-centre ports shows self-impedance rising toward the edge, while transfer impedance drops sharply with radius and is nearly purely capacitive near the rim (Figure 6.40). Measurements of a 1 mil buried cavity give ~80 pH one-port/two-port at the same via (via loop + spreading), and ~20 pH when probing opposite via ends — a direct read of spreading inductance close to the 32 pH sheet value (Figures 6.41–6.42).
