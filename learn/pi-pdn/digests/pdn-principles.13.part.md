# L1 overview

- **Bulk capacitor and VRM optimization** — The VRM output inductance and bulk capacitance form a parallel resonance whose peak is often the largest impedance in the PDN and a stability risk; ceramic MLCC bulk caps are underdamped because their ESR is too low, so choose total capacitance from the target impedance and VRM inductance, then pick capacitor count/type so combined ESR equals the target impedance (q ≈ 1). Higher capacitance then only adds margin.
- **The PDN ecosystem and fundamental peak limits** — Above ~1 MHz the VRM plus bulk caps look like a ~10 nH inductor that adds to package lead inductance and shifts the Bandini Mountain lower and higher. The die-pad peak can never go below the characteristic impedance Z0 = sqrt(Lpkg/Cdie); engineering below ~2×Z0 is hard, so the optimum board is not a dead short but a resistor equal to the target impedance across roughly 1–200 MHz.
- **MLCC value strategy (one value, three values, arbitrary sets)** — Adding any MLCC lowers the peak, but the best single value puts its SRF near the Bandini peak and minimizes mounting inductance. "10 µF" or "10 µF + 1 µF + 0.1 µF" recipes are arbitrary: with unknown ecology, more or mixed identical-value capacitors converge on the dead-short limit and can even raise the peak by removing ESR damping.
- **FDTIM, large on-die capacitance, controlled ESR, and on-package decoupling** — The Frequency Domain Target Impedance Method selects a discrete set of values (three per decade) whose parallel ESR minima sit at the target impedance, producing a flat profile that damps the peak without knowing the ecology. For low target impedances (5 mΩ class), controlled-ESR capacitors, and especially OPD controlled-ESR caps on the package, deliver flatter, fewer-component, lower-risk solutions.
- **Multiple chips on a shared rail and bottom line** — Other chips act as low-ESR, low-ESL decoupling capacitors: they slightly lower an unoptimized Bandini peak but can short out deliberately added damping and make a robust PDN worse. The bottom line restates peak-impedance thinking, the Bandini Mountain figures of merit, and the resistor-like board goal.

# L2 detail

- `#L15423-L15570` — Optimizing bulk capacitance against the VRM: the VRM L (20 nH) and bulk C parallel-resonate near 90 kHz with a peak 3× target; q-factor and critical-damping condition show a large MLCC would need absurd capacitance, so instead pick total C for the target impedance and choose electrolytic/tantalum or parallel count so total ESR ≈ target impedance, which nearly eliminates the peak.
- `#L15571-L15728` — Building the PDN ecosystem: VRM + bulk cap collapse to ~10 nH above 1 MHz and, with cavity spreading inductance and package leads, raise and shift the Bandini Mountain (example: 1 Ω at 45 MHz shorted becomes 9.6 Ω at 10 MHz); bulks' mounting inductance, not capacitance, sets this, so added MLCCs exist mainly to short that inductance and add damping.
- `#L15729-L15913` — Fundamental limits to peak impedance: the die-pad peak can never fall below Z0 = sqrt(Lpkg/Cdie), engineering below ~2×Z0 is impractical, and the optimum board impedance is a flat resistance equal to the target impedance over ~1–200 MHz; a higher series R lowers the peak while q > ~2 but raises low-frequency impedance, so R = Ztarget is the robust, low-cost rule.
- `#L15914-L16020` — One-value MLCC baseline: defines the reference ecology (50 nF die cap, 250 pH package, 2.4 nF cavity, 10 nH VRM, 100 µF bulk) and shows three impedance views; adding one MLCC always lowers the peak by paralleling its ESL, but creates a low-frequency peak (bulk-C/MLCC-C with both inductances) and a high-frequency peak (MLCC L ∥ package L with die C).
- `#L16021-L16201` — Optimizing the single MLCC value: any "add 10 µF" app-note is arbitrary; the best value puts the MLCC SRF near the Bandini peak (dip is slightly below SRF because package L adds), giving C_opt from the resonance condition (~160 nF in the example). More identical capacitors are not better—beyond matching package L they only lower ESR damping and can raise the peak.
- `#L16202-L16339` — Three-value MLCC myth: 10 µF/1 µF/0.1 µF versus three 10 µF differ only where ESR differs; with realistic ESR the peak reduction is marginal, and adding 100 arbitrary capacitors makes the board indistinguishable from a dead short at the Bandini peak, capping performance at the intrinsic limit at high cost.
- `#L16340-L16429` — Optimizing three values: manual SPICE search finds 1000 nF/60 nF/20 nF cutting the peak from 10 Ω to 0.25 Ω (q from 14 to ~3.5), beating both a dead short and three identical optimized caps, but only because the full ecology (die C, package L, ESL) is known—hence the motivation for FDTIM.
- `#L16430-L16521` — FDTIM principles and minimum count: (1) enough caps to bring board inductance below package lead inductance, (2) if peak < 200 MHz (high q) create a flat impedance near target to damp it, (3) if peak > 200 MHz (low q) values matter little; first compute Z0 and q, then N_min from Ztarget/(2π fmax ESL) and L_board < L_pkg, with every pH of mounting inductance costing capacitors.
- `#L16522-L16649` — FDTIM value selection: values come in 1/22/47 per decade from 22 µF down to ~1 nF (smallest set by SRF > fmax); choose per-value counts so each value's ESR/N reaches the target impedance; 16 capacitors then flatten 1–200 MHz and cut the peak from 3 Ω to 0.2 Ω, five times better than 16 × 10 µF.
- `#L16650-L16789` — Large on-die capacitance, small package inductance case: 400 nF die cap with 2.5 pH gives Z0 = 2.5 mΩ and a 10 mΩ shorted peak at 160 MHz; a 5 mΩ target demands ~0.5 nH mountings, 200 identical caps reach only ~20 mΩ, while 135 FDTIM-selected caps meet 5 mΩ, and 6 × 200 µF bulks plus 4 nH fix the 1 MHz VRM/bulk peak.
- `#L16790-L16936` — Controlled-ESR alternative: capacitors with 0.1–1 Ω ESR (1 µF, 10 µF available) give a broad flat impedance independent of value; a third sizing condition (parallel R ≤ target) applies, 13 × 1 Ω parts cut the 100 mΩ-case peak to 130 mΩ, and 100 × 0.5 Ω parts serve the 5 mΩ case—simpler than FDTIM but ~5× the capacitor cost.
- `#L16937-L17139` — On-package decoupling (OPD): fine package design rules allow 0.2 nH or less interdigitated/reverse-aspect-ratio caps, splitting the Bandini Mountain in two; OPD helps only when its parallel inductance is comparable to or below the package-to-board inductance, and an optimized value (SRF near the peak, e.g. 0.1 µF) beats 10 µF parts; 10 controlled-ESR OPDs replace 43 board MLCCs.
- `#L17140-L17248` — Multiple chips sharing a rail: other chips act as low-ESL, low-ESR 50–500 nF decoupling capacitors whose board-side SRF sits near the Bandini frequency; with unoptimized MLCCs they slightly lower the peak, but with an FDTIM-flattened board their low ESR shorts the damping resistance and raises the peak—better for a poor PDN, worse for a robust one.
- `#L17249-L17299` — Bottom line: the PDN ecology spans die pads to VRM; peaks (not low valleys) matter, chief among them the Bandini Mountain set by die C and package L; planes only supply low spreading inductance; a resistor-like board equal to the target impedance over ~1–50 MHz plus FDTIM (or known-ecology optimization) is the robust path.
