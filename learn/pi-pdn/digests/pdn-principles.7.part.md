# L1 overview

- **Why capacitors, and the series RLC model** — Capacitors fill the impedance gap between the VRM's upper frequency limit and the chip's clock. An ideal capacitor's impedance falls as 1/ωC (0.1 µF ≈ 1 Ω at 1 MHz), but a real MLCC is accurately modeled by a three-element series RLC circuit at all practical frequencies; the ESL turns the falling impedance back upward above SRF.
- **Paralleling and parallel resonance** — n identical caps in parallel give C×n, L/n, R/n with unchanged SRF and q-factor, so paralleling is a way to engineer lower inductance. Two caps with different SRFs create a parallel resonance peak between them — "the most important feature of any PDN" — at the PRF where their reactances cross.
- **The three peak-reduction knobs** — Peak impedance at PRF falls with (1) capacitor values closer together, ideally within a factor of two (about three values per decade), (2) lower mounting ESL, and (3) higher ESR. A simple formula relates Zpeak to L, R1/R2, and C of the higher-SRF circuit.
- **Engineering capacitance and its stability** — Capacitance comes from area, layer count, Dk, and dielectric thickness; board planes give only about 1/h nF per square inch (0.3 nF/in² at 3 mils), so planes are for inductance, not capacitance. Class 1 (NP0) is stable but low density; Class 2 (X5R, X7R) is the decoupling workhorse with ±15% over rated temperature, and applied voltage reduces capacitance more than temperature does — measure at the application voltage.
- **How much capacitance, and ESR modeling** — Bulk C must hold Z below target where the VRM gives up (10 kHz–1 MHz); C ≈ 1/(2π·f_VRM-max·Ztarget) from the frequency domain matches Q = I·Δt/ΔV from the time domain. ESR has two loss mechanisms (dielectric loss dominant at low frequency, current crowding/skin-effect-like √f growth at high frequency), varies up to 3× between nominally identical parts, and can be estimated from capacitance by empirical power laws (0603: ESR ≈ 0.32·C^-k, 0402: 0.20·C^-k) with C in nF.
- **Controlled-ESR capacitors** — Vendors sell caps with deliberately raised ESR (100 mΩ to 1.2 Ω, independent of C) by narrowing plate metallization or adding a series resistor. Their value is not a higher single-cap minimum impedance but damping of parallel resonance peaks, especially when combined with the VRM or with on-die capacitance; their low q-factor (2–4) is the design asset.

# L2 detail

- `#L6881-L6925` — Chapter 5 opening and 5.1: capacitors reduce PDN impedance between the VRM band and the clock; ideal Z_C = 1/ωC, rule of thumb 0.1 µF ≈ 1 Ω at 1 MHz; larger C gives lower impedance but real ESL reverses the benefit at high frequency, so an accurate physical-to-impedance capacitor model is the design lever.
- `#L6926-L6985` — 5.2: a real mounted 0603 MLCC matches an ideal series RLC model above ~1 MHz; defines equivalent C, ESL (mounted-configuration dependent), and ESR; the same RLC model fits X2Y-style caps (R = 0.01 Ω, C = 180 nF, L = 0.43 nH) and is valid for arrays, with an advanced model reserved for mounting inductance below 1 nH.
- `#L6986-L7054` — 5.3: n identical RLC circuits in parallel scale to C_n = n·C1, L_n = L1/n, R_n = R1/n with unchanged SRF = 1/(2π√(LC)) and unchanged q-factor; mounting inductance scales down with n while spreading inductance does not, so paralleling is the primary way to engineer low inductance.
- `#L7055-L7149` — 5.4: two RLC circuits with different SRFs in parallel produce a parallel resonance peak; low frequency sees C1 + C2, high frequency sees L1·L2/(L1 + L2), and the peak sits at the PRF where X1 = X2, giving PRF ≈ 1/(2π√((L1+L2)·(C1·C2/(C1+C2)))) — e.g. 2 nH with 10 nF and 1000 nF resonates near 1.2 MHz.
- `#L7150-L7315` — 5.5: Zpeak is simulated rather than solved exactly; it scales with L1, falls with larger R1 and R2, and falls as C of the higher-SRF cap grows; SPICE sweeps show the peak drops sharply as C1/C2 approaches 1 (biggest effect between 0.5× and 2×, i.e. three values per decade), rising ESR lowers the peak, and lowering L1 alone, L2 alone, or both reduces it (L2 alone only to a limit).
- `#L7316-L7378` — 5.6: parallel-plate capacitance C = 0.225·Dk·n·A/h (pF, area in in², h in mils); capacitance per unit area on a board is roughly 1/h nF/in², so a 3 mil plane pair gives 0.3 nF/in² and a 10 in × 10 in board only ~30 nF — planes exist for low inductance, not capacitance; real caps are within 20% of nominal.
- `#L7379-L7459` — 5.7: high-Dk ferroelectric dielectrics mean high fields and non-linear polarizability; Class 1 (NP0, Dk 20–40, < 30 ppm/°K) is stable but low density, Class 2 (X5R/X7R, Dk 200–14,000, barium titanate) is the decoupling choice with EIA labels giving range and tolerance (X5R: −55 to +85 °C, ±15%; X7R extends to +125 °C); voltage sensitivity exceeds temperature sensitivity and varies by vendor, so measure at the applied voltage.
- `#L7460-L7576` — 5.8: "how much capacitance" needs time-domain analysis with the VRM; bulk C must keep Z below target at f_VRM-max (10 kHz–1 MHz), giving C_bulk = 1/(2π·f_VRM-max·Ztarget) — 50 mΩ at 10 kHz needs ~318 µF; equivalent time-domain form C = I·Δt/ΔV with Δt = 1/(2π·f) yields the identical estimate (50 mΩ, 10 µs → ~32 µF); large VRM-vs-bulk parallel resonance can destabilize the VRM, and SMPS nonlinearity limits frequency-domain validity.
- `#L7577-L7728` — 5.9: ESR combines conductor loss in the plates and dielectric loss; the constant-R RLC model is a good first order, dielectric loss adds a parallel RC with G = 2πf·C·Df that raises real impedance at low frequency, while current crowding toward the bottom plates adds a √f term (R(f) = Rdc·√(1 + f/F0)) above SRF; fitting three parameters reproduces vendor curves, low ESR sits at 10–100 MHz, and nominally identical 100 µF 1210 parts vary ~3× (0.003–0.01 Ω), so either measure parts or use a constant R matched at SRF.
- `#L7729-L7809` — 5.10: ESR falls as plate count (and thus C) rises; mining AVX SpiCAP data yields ESR = 0.32·C^-k for 0603 X5R and ESR = 0.20·C^-k for 0402 X5R (C in nF), same exponent, coefficient set by body size — a directly implementable first-order SPICE estimate; q = (1/ESR)·√(L/C) then falls only slightly with larger C (≈4 at low C to ≈2 at high C for 2 nH ESL).
- `#L7810-L7884` — 5.11: controlled-ESR capacitors add 100 mΩ–1.2 Ω independent of C by narrowing plate metallization or inserting a printed resistor (e.g. Murata 0816 1 µF at 0.1/0.22/0.47/1 Ω vs 0.016 Ω standard); the benefit is a lower parallel-resonance peak when caps are combined, not a higher single-cap minimum, and the resulting low q-factor damps VRM and on-die/package resonances; such parts are limited to C above ~1 µF and less capacitance per body size than standard parts.
