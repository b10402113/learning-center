# L1 overview

- **Impedance as the PDN design metric (2.1–2.3)** — PDN voltage noise is the die current spectrum flowing through the interconnect impedance, so when the current spectrum is unknown the impedance profile becomes the surrogate acceptance criterion. Impedance is Z = V/I in both domains but far simpler in the frequency domain, where a capacitor is 1/(jωC) and an inductor is jωL, giving ±20 dB/decade slopes on log-log plots; any linear circuit's impedance can be simulated with a 1 A AC current source (QUCS or ADS), where output volts read directly as ohms.
- **Real components vs. ideal elements, and the two RLC topologies (2.4–2.6)** — Real capacitors are measured structures whose impedance is modeled to high bandwidth by adding parasitics; one 0603 MLCC matches a series RLC of R = 18 mΩ, C = 182 nF, L = 1.3 nH up to 200 MHz. The series RLC gives a dip (low-frequency impedance set only by C, high-frequency only by L, minimum = R at SRF = 1/(2π√LC)); the parallel RLC gives a peak (maximum = R at PRF).
- **Resonance figures of merit and damping (2.7–2.8)** — Every RLC circuit is summarized by four numbers: resonant frequency, characteristic impedance Z0 = √(L/C), q-factor = Z0/R, and Zmin or Zmax; peaks scale as Z0 × q-factor and dips as Z0/q-factor. Design target is q-factor ≈ 1 (R near Z0) with q = 1/2 critically damped; worked examples contrast a 1000 µF tantalum (low q, flat), a 1206 10 µF MLCC (very low ESR, sharp resonance), and a 0402 1 nF part.
- **The same PDN seen from die and from board (2.9–2.10)** — Identical package-lead-inductance plus on-die-capacitance appears as a parallel RLC peak from the die and a series RLC dip from the board, with R replaced by the sum of lead and on-die metallization resistance. In the time domain that peak shows up as ringing at the PRF, whose amplitude tracks the current spectrum energy at that frequency; BW (GHz) ≈ 0.35/RT (nsec) bounds what the current edge can excite.
- **The impedance matrix and the chapter bottom line (2.11–2.12)** — The n-port impedance matrix generalizes V/I: diagonal terms are self-impedances (other ports open), off-diagonal terms are transfer impedances measuring coupling, from 0 Ω (none) to the self-impedance (100% coupled). Each element is complex and frequency dependent and can be simulated by driving one port with 1 A while leaving the others open.

# L2 detail

- `#L1265-L1312` — Chapter 2 opening and §2.1: PDN voltage noise = die current spectrum × PDN impedance; board-level measurements understate on-die noise because acceptable results depend on which code runs, so with unknown worst-case current the impedance profile becomes the surrogate quality metric and design reduces to hitting a target impedance.
- `#L1313-L1421` — §2.2: Z = V/I holds in time and frequency domains but is simpler in frequency; time-domain capacitor impedance needs dV/dt, whereas frequency domain gives Z = 1/(jωC) and Z = jωL; reactance X is the imaginary part, falling 20 dB/decade for C and rising 20 dB/decade for L, so log-log plots show straight lines of slope −1 and +1.
- `#L1422-L1583` — §2.3: SPICE makes multi-element impedance calculation practical; build an impedance analyzer from a constant-current AC source, set it to 1 A at 0°, and the simulated voltage amplitude and phase are numerically the impedance magnitude and phase in ohms (a 1 GΩ shunt resistor gives simulators a DC path and caps the measurable impedance at 1 GΩ); also covers Kramers-Kronig: flat magnitude → 0° phase, −20 dB/decade → −90°, +20 dB/decade → +90°, and the warning that C and L values stay constant while impedance varies.
- `#L1584-L1687` — §2.4: ideal elements are simulated, real components are measured and carry parasitics; a real 0603 MLCC matches an ideal 182 nF capacitor only to ~3 MHz but a series RLC (R = 18 mΩ, C = 182 nF, L = 1.3 nH) tracks it to the 200 MHz measurement bandwidth; nearly all PDN structures reduce to series or parallel RLC combinations, with R in series or parallel with the reactive elements.
- `#L1688-L1777` — §2.5: series RLC impedance from summing element impedances, giving a dip; C sets only the low-frequency impedance (phase −90°), L sets only the high-frequency impedance (phase +90°), and at the crossing SRF = 1/(2π√(LC)) the impedance minimum equals R; unit-friendly SRF (MHz) = 159/√(L nH × C nF), and the reactance there is the RLC characteristic impedance Z0, unrelated to transmission-line Z0.
- `#L1778-L1824` — §2.6: parallel RLC gives a peak instead of a dip; L dominates (shorts) at DC, C dominates at high frequency, and the peak occurs at PRF = 1/(2π√(LC)) where the parallel L‖C impedance is maximum; the parallel resistor damps an otherwise infinite peak, and peaks in a measured impedance profile almost always come from parallel RLC circuits.
- `#L1825-L1985` — §2.7: resonance is described by three features (resonant frequency, min/max impedance, broadness), quantified by Z0 = √(L/C) and q-factor = Z0/R; Zmin = Z0/q for series, Zmax = Z0 × q for parallel (or Z0/q if R is in series with L or C), q = 1/2 is critically damped, high q means narrow sharp resonance and long ringing; to cut PDN peaks, raise the damping toward q ≈ 1 by increasing R, lowering L, or raising C.
- `#L1986-L2056` — §2.8: the four figures of merit every RLC circuit should be reduced to (SRF/PRF, Z0, q-factor, Zmin/Zmax) with the defining relations; worked examples compute a 1000 µF tantalum (L = 8 nH, R = 0.05 Ω, low q, broad flat profile), a 1206 10 µF X5R MLCC (L = 3 nH, R = 0.003 Ω, high q, sharp resonance), and a 0402 1 nF X7R part whose ~50× larger R offsets its higher Z0 to give a comparable q.
- `#L2057-L2195` — §2.9: whether the profile peaks or dips depends on where you measure — parallel LC gives a maximum, series LC a minimum, and the damping resistor may sit in series or parallel; the package-inductance plus on-die-capacitance PDN appears parallel (peak) from the die and series (dip) from the board, with the effective R being the series loop sum of lead and on-die metallization resistance; sample values (Cdie = 50 nF, Lpackage = 0.5 nH, Rleads = 0.01 Ω, Rmetal = 0.005 Ω) predict high q on both views, and how the probe is attached changes what is measured.
- `#L2196-L2304` — §2.10: frequency-domain impedance is only the steady-state response and misses rogue-wave transients; simulate transient noise by driving an RLC network (with VRM R/L and series-RLC capacitor models) from a current step; current bandwidth BW (GHz) ≈ 0.35/RT (nsec) sets the highest frequency the edge can excite, so a 10 ns edge reaches ~35 MHz and rings a 25 MHz PRF; ringing period is set by the circuit's PRF, not the current rise time, and longer edges carry less energy at the PRF and ring less.
- `#L2305-L2592` — §2.11: generalization of impedance to n ports via the impedance matrix V_j = Σ Z_jk I_k for linear, passive, time-invariant networks; diagonal elements are self-impedances (all other ports open), off-diagonal are mutual/transfer impedances = coupling, ranging from 0 Ω (no coupling) to the self-impedance (fully coupled); a transfer impedance is not an impedance between ports (an ohmmeter reading) but the voltage on one port per amp into another; two-port resistor examples (common return vs. shared series resistor) and SPICE extraction of all four terms by driving 1 A into one port with the other open, illustrated with two RLC circuits sharing 5 nH.
- `#L2593-L2637` — §2.12: ten-point bottom line — noise is current spectrum times impedance; impedance is V/I in both domains but simpler in frequency; never confuse real (measurable) with ideal (simulatable) elements; simple RLC models fit complex structures; master series and parallel RLC; compute the four figures of merit first; parallel-resonance peaks (≈ Z0 × q) matter most; transient ringing frequency equals the peak-impedance frequency; the impedance matrix extends impedance to multi-port, with diagonal self- and off-diagonal transfer terms.
