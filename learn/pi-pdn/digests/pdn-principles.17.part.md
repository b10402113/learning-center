# L1 overview

- **Switched-capacitor load and transient correlation (10.11–10.12)** — replaces the resistive load with a time-domain switched-capacitor subcircuit that draws charge impulses from the on-die capacitance, then correlates impulse, step, and resonance responses against PRC spreadsheet predictions; droop 45 mV predicted vs 47 mV simulated, resonance 922 mV vs 903 mV p-p.
- **Q-factor bookkeeping in two domains (10.13–10.14)** — isolates bump-loop R, ODC ESR, leakage, and load damping one at a time and confirms q = Zpeak/Z0 from spreadsheet, frequency domain, and time domain (all 2.5); then shows that rise time sets which peak gets stimulated, with the rule that a rise time longer than ~5 time constants (or the resonant period) suppresses a droop.
- **Three-peak PDN improvements (10.15–10.18)** — caps count and value distribution, a fictitious 10 mΩ R_damp on the SMPS, and on-package decoupling cut the Bandini peak from 91 mΩ to 51 mΩ; counterintuitively, dropping from 40 to 10 optimized board caps lowers the peak further (q 2.41 → 2.04) because ESR losses rise.
- **Transient assumptions and tradeoffs (10.19–10.20)** — relaxing the repeating transient from 8 A to 4 A doubles the resonance-band target impedance to 12.6 mΩ and cuts droop to 165 mV; a 17 ns ramp cuts it to 96 mV, but 96 mV still exceeds a 50 mV budget, leaving only the costly option of raising VRM voltage.
- **Reverse-engineering and correlation (10.21–10.24)** — reconstructs an Altera FPGA's PDN from five measurements (voltage, leakage, current at a few clocks, impulse response, resonant frequency): 313 nF ODC, 74 pH loop, Z0 = 15.4 mΩ, 30 nC impulse; measured vs simulated 105/104 mV, 146/150 mV, 577/584 mV p-p, with extra 0.6 mΩ of socket loss needed to match the resonance.

# L2 detail

- `#L21804-L21880` — Switched capacitor load model in ADS: top-level three-loop circuit, die separated as a `switch_cap_load` subcircuit, fictitious L1 plus L_tau/R_tau shaping an impulse that finishes within the clock period, PWL control of step vs resonance ramp rate, and a leakage current source modeled as proportional to V³ rather than a shunt resistor.
- `#L21881-L22059` — Impulse, step, and resonance responses of the three-peak PDN with 50%-duty 8 A load damping as the worst case: 4.5 nC impulse gives a 25 A, 0.5 ns die current that the bump loop filters to 2 A and produces 47 mV droop vs 45 mV predicted, ringing out in four cycles at q = 4.5; step gives 271 mV (ideal source) vs 292 mV PRC and 12 A vs 15 A peak for switched vs ideal load; resonance gives 903 mV p-p vs 922 mV predicted and 1.588 V p-p with an ideal source.
- `#L22060-L22289` — Individual q-factors isolated by zeroing series resistors and opening parallel ones one mechanism at a time: Z0 = 36 mΩ from both PRC and reactance crossing, combined peak 91 mΩ and q = 2.5 in all three methods, individual peaks of 200 mΩ (load at 8 A), then leakage, ODC ESR (2.5 mΩ from 250 ps × 100 nF), and bump-loop loss as least important; the 1 A vs 8 A load comparison shows p-p noise is largely independent of load current because impedance and drive move oppositely.
- `#L22290-L22479` — Rise time and peak stimulation: bandwidth ≈ 0.35/tr, so 1 ns excites all three peaks (droops at 16 ns, 180 ns, 2 µs), while tr > 7.9 ns stops stimulating the 44 MHz Bandini peak, tr > 177 ns the 1.98 MHz peak, and tr > 2.69 µs the 130 kHz peak; the 20 ns ramped switched-capacitor load (5× the first resonance time constant) erases the first droop entirely.
- `#L22480-L22627` — Board-level improvements: Bandini peak is 15× target and Z0 6× target; the 132 pH loop splits into 84 pH from mounted caps, 29 pH package, 19 pH BGA vias, so going from 10 to 20 caps cuts mounted inductance to 45 pH and 40 caps to 23 pH with diminishing returns past ~40; a distributed 1×10 µF to 16×100 nF set flattens impedance and kills the second peak but only moves Bandini from 91 to 74 mΩ.
- `#L22628-L22700` — Better SMPS model: the tall VRM peak in the linear model is unrealistic for a compensated supply, so a fictitious 10 mΩ R_damp shunts the bulk capacitor to the ideal source and a 10 nH series inductor sets the VRM droop time constant; valid only above the bulk-cap series resonance and for simulations shorter than a few microseconds, leaving Bandini (74 mΩ, Z0 = 26 mΩ, q = 2.85) as the dominant peak.
- `#L22701-L22900` — On-package decoupling: top-side, embedded, and bottom-side locations all consume package resources; mounted loop inductance matters most, ESR second (often the dominant Bandini loss), capacitance least (≈5× ODC suffices since the series combination governs); two 220 nF caps with 150 pH shift the peak from 62 to 75 MHz and drop it from 74 to 53 mΩ (Z0 = 22 mΩ, q = 2.41), and cutting board caps to 10 optimized values gives 65 MHz, 51 mΩ, q = 2.04 because more current routes through higher-ESR paths.
- `#L22901-L23005` — Before/after transient response: step droop at 15 ns improves 218 → 186 mV (less than 40-board-cap case because Z0 was traded for damping), the second droop near 150 ns disappears into a flat ~50 mV offset that is step current through flat impedance rather than IR drop, and the VRM band is overdamped with only the 31 mV DC IR drop (10 A × 3.1 mΩ, 312 mW) remaining; resonance p-p falls 480 → 285 mV but is still 5× tolerance.
- `#L23006-L23097` — Re-examining transient current assumptions: if only half the 8 A transient can repeat at resonance, the target impedance rises from 6.3 to 12.6 mΩ and droop falls to 165 mV; transient current is really frequency-dependent because pipelines fill and empty gradually, and a 17 ns ramp (5 time constants) cuts droop to 96 mV, with residual content from cap ESR, clock-edge noise, and near-100 ns reactance.
- `#L23098-L23138` — Risk, performance, and cost tradeoffs: droop went from 497 mV (50% of Vdd) to 96 mV but still exceeds the 50 mV budget, acceptable in consumer markets but not aviation, automotive, or medical; the last resort is raising VRM voltage 50 mV, costing ~10% dynamic power (V²) and ~15% leakage (V³) plus thermal and reliability impact.
- `#L23139-L23374` — Reverse-engineering an Altera FPGA PDN from measurements: Kelvin/four-point 50 Ω sense lines centered in a checkerboard of balls and vias, Vdd−Vss by scope subtraction or coax transmission-line subtraction with equal-length cables, and probe resistance causing ~4% divider error; measured 3 A leakage, 11 A at 266 MHz, 19 A at 533 MHz, 105 mV clock-edge noise, 33 MHz resonance, 30 mV step droop at 100 ns yielding 27 nF switched and 313 nF total ODC, 74 pH loop, Z0 = 15.4 mΩ.
- `#L23375-L23502` — Simulation-to-measurement correlation: 30 nC / 27 nF switched load with 200 ps time constant draws a 100 A, >1,000 A/ns impulse whose area sets the droop; impulse 105 vs 104 mV, frequency peak 33.88 MHz at 25.9 mΩ with Z0 crossing 15.4 mΩ, q ≈ 1.68 vs 1.75 hand-calc, step 146 vs 150 mV over 25 clock edges, and resonance 577 vs 584 mV p-p after adding 0.6 mΩ of ball/socket loss to 4.35 mΩ because the first simulation over-predicted ringing.
- `#L23503-L23565` — Consolidated summary table of inputs, extracted parameters, and simulated results: target impedances of 6.9 and 3.4 mΩ sit far below both Z0 and the 25.9 mΩ peak, so the FPGA was pushed far past its intended operating region; the earlier published q = 3.9 / 63 mΩ is revised down because leakage and load damping had been omitted, and the five required measurements make the method repeatable.
- `#L23566-L23610` — Bottom line: PDN design is peak management, every peak is described by resonant frequency, Z0, and q, and three current waveforms (clock-edge impulse, step, resonant square wave) capture the voltage response; the spreadsheet's approximations track simulation closely, board caps should "do no harm," OPD is cost-effective, and measured FPGA data confirms the whole approach.
