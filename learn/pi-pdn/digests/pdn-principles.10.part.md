# L1 overview

- **Capacitor–cavity interaction and spreading inductance** — A mounted capacitor forms a parallel resonance with cavity capacitance at f = 1/(2π√(Lmount·Ccavity)), creating an impedance peak between the two self-resonances. Total effective inductance is Lmount + Lspread, and the ratio Lspread/Lmount decides whether capacitor location matters at all (transparent vs. non-transparent cavity).
- **Cavity transparency rule of thumb** — Lspread ≈ 32 pH/mil × h × ln(s/d)-scale; with ~2 nH mounting inductance, Lspread must stay under 0.4 nH, which happens only when the dielectric is thinner than ~10 mils. Above 10 mils the cavity is not transparent and position matters; power traces instead of planes reintroduce spreading inductance even on thin dielectric.
- **Saturating spreading inductance** — Parallel capacitor ESL falls as 1/n until it drops below the shared spreading inductance from the capacitor ring to the package power/ground region; beyond that point added capacitors buy nothing. Three knobs reduce the saturated value: thinner cavity, larger BGA pad radius a, smaller capacitor radius b, plus uniform distribution around the package.
- **Modal resonances as transmission-line physics** — A cavity behaves as a wide, low-Z transmission line whose open boundaries reflect waves; resonances occur when a half-wave multiple fits the length (same boundary conditions) or a quarter-wave odd multiple (different boundaries). Input impedance peaks mark those modal frequencies, which are intrinsic to length, Dk, and geometry, not to the source.
- **Attenuation and 2-D modes** — Conductor loss (independent of line width, inverse in dielectric thickness) and dielectric loss (pure material property, ∝ frequency) damp the impedance peaks; thin dielectric reduces Z0 and raises damping, the single most effective way to kill high-frequency cavity peaks. In two dimensions modes carry indices (m, n), and the probe location decides which modes are excited — a center probe excites only even-indexed modes.
- **Transfer impedance and design bottom line** — Self-impedance always carries the local spreading inductance artifact; transfer impedance Z21 (voltage at port 2 per current at port 1) measures the impedance "shared" by the radial spreading wave, staying nearly purely capacitive far from the drive point. Probing opposite ends of a via pair cancels via loop inductance and directly yields the few-tens-of-pH cavity spreading inductance.

# L2 detail

- `#L10519-L10587` — Section 6.12: a real capacitor is a series RLC whose SRF with 2 nH mounting inductance and 100 nF sits near 10 MHz; mounting it across a cavity puts its impedance in parallel with the cavity capacitance, adding capacitance at low frequency and creating a new parallel resonance peak where the capacitor's inductance equals the cavity's capacitive impedance.
- `#L10588-L10765` — Section 6.13: total inductance seen at an observation point is Lmount + Lspread, with Lspread ≈ (μh/2π)·ln(s/d) in pH for spacing s and contact diameter d; case 1 (Lmount ≫ Lspread, thin dielectric) makes the cavity transparent so location is irrelevant, case 2 (Lspread ≫ Lmount, thick dielectric) makes position matter, and the log dependence means moving a capacitor from 2 in to 0.2 in only cuts Lspread from 1.22 nH to 0.75 nH; the 10 mil transparency threshold and the power-trace exception (~320 pH for 5 squares on 2 mil dielectric) follow.
- `#L10766-L10841` — Section 6.14: with a transparent cavity n capacitors behave as n parallel lumped elements and ESL falls as 1/n until the parallel ESL drops below the spreading inductance from the capacitor ring to the package's central power/ground region, at which point spreading inductance saturates (~71 pH for 10 mil cavity, a = 0.25 in, b = 1 in); mitigation is thinner cavity, larger a, smaller b, and uniform capacitor distribution around the package.
- `#L10842-L11014` — Section 6.15: an open-ended uniform transmission line with time delay TD reflects waves with round-trip phase Δθ = 2·TD·f; constructive interference when Δθ is an integer number of cycles gives resonances at f = n/(2·TD) for equal boundary conditions (half-wave multiples, 0.5 GHz spacing for a 6 in FR4 line) and at odd quarter-wave multiples when the two ends differ.
- `#L11015-L11091` — Section 6.16: input impedance Z = Zport·(1+S11)/(1−S11) peaks at the modal frequencies because reflected and incident waves add in phase; peak height depends on Z0, termination, and loss, but peak frequencies do not, so a cavity impedance sweep maps its modal resonances directly.
- `#L11092-L11237` — Section 6.17: with heavy round-trip attenuation the input impedance flattens toward Z0; total attenuation is Aconductor + Adielectric, where dielectric loss depends only on Dk and Df (geometry cancels) and conductor loss above skin-depth onset is independent of line width but inversely proportional to dielectric thickness, so thinner dielectric lowers Z0 and raises damping, suppressing modal peaks far more than raising Dk does.
- `#L11238-L11408` — Section 6.18: cavity modes in two dimensions satisfy half-wave fitting along X (m), along Y (n), and diagonally, giving f_mn = (c/(2√Dk))·√((m/Lx)² + (n/Ly)²); the mode frequencies are intrinsic and independent of probe position, but excitation efficiency is not — a center probe cannot excite odd modes because reflected and incident waves cancel at the null, so only (2,0), (2,2), and (4,0) appear.
- `#L11409-L11636` — Section 6.19: the impedance matrix V = Z·I separates self-impedance (diagonal, one-port, contaminated by local spreading inductance and probe residuals) from transfer impedance Z21 (off-diagonal, voltage at port 2 per current at port 1, the impedance "shared" by the outward radial wave); transfer impedance of a 3 in, 10 mil cavity falls toward pure capacitance as the probe moves outward, and a two-port measurement across opposite ends of the same via pair removes via loop inductance to reveal ~20 pH of spreading inductance directly.
- `#L11637-L11667` — Section 6.20: ten-point summary — three impedance regions (capacitive, spreading-inductance dip, modal peaks), low-frequency capacitance as a Dk measurement, dip as an LC self-resonance with L = Lspread, cavity Z0 typically below 0.1 Ω, spreading inductance set by current constriction and log spacing, capacitor-induced parallel resonance, mode frequencies fixed by dimensions and Dk, and thin dielectric as the best modal-peak damper.
