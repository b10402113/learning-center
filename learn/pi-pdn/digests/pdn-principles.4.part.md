# L1 overview

- **Two-port measurement of a 1-inch copper loop (§3.10)** — repeats the §3.6 loop as a two-port DUT with both SMAs' signal pins on one end and returns on the other; S11 carries little usable data for a low-impedance DUT while S21 does, and impedance is 25 Ω × S21 below 1 Ω (breaks down above 10 Ω). Extracted L is 5 nH lower than the one-port result because the one-port path included the SMA fixture's series inductance; two-port R is about 8 mΩ at 10 MHz.
- **Fixture artifacts and "hacking" a model (§3.11)** — the right way to read measured data is to build an equivalent circuit, simulate it, and compare; agreement proves consistency, never correctness, so keep stacking consistency checks. Adding lossless transmission lines (Z0, TD) for the SMA launches explains the single-RL model's apparent frequency-dependent R: the fixture's negative phase shift rotates the near-90° inductive phase toward zero, dumping imaginary impedance into the real part. Fitted values: R = 0.005 Ω, L = 23 nH, Z0 = 50 Ω, TD = 42 ps, matching the 600 MHz peak and roll-off. Start with the simplest model and add complexity only as needed.
- **Via inductance (§3.12)** — a 50 mil wire via shorting a 50-Ω microstrip, measured two-port, yields R = 0.0007 Ω, L = 0.28 nH, Z0 = 50 Ω, TD = 210 ps and a full 2 GHz match. The apparent frequency-dependent R and L are fixture phase-shift artifacts, not skin depth: hand calculation gives 0.02 mΩ DC and 0.29 nH (partial self-inductance of a rod) versus 0.28 nH extracted, and the 10 MHz skin-depth annulus (0.8 mil) explains only ~15×, within 2× of the 0.7 mΩ extracted.
- **Mounted MLCC on an evaluation board (§3.13)** — because a capacitor cannot be measured apart from its mounting, the fixture board defines ESL; an X2Y part fits R = 0.01 Ω, C = 175 nF (spec 220 nF, ~25% low), L = 0.41 nH up to 400 MHz, then needs 0.7 inch, 3.5 Ω transmission-line fixtures. ESR damps parallel resonances; ESL is not intrinsic and sets the minimum capacitor count. Moving from near-via-in-pad to 20 mil surface traces raises ESL from 0.2 nH to 0.9 nH — always make fixtures short and close to 50 Ω.
- **On-die capacitance (§3.14)** — two-port extraction from C4 bumps through package balls to the PCB, covering 300 kHz–3 GHz. Die bumps give 81.5 nF (81 nF by point-by-point algebra) and only 2 pH, R ≈ 70 mΩ; a single package ball pair gives 79.5 nF, 0.28 nH, 24 mΩ, while two ball pairs yield 0.12 nH, the 160 pH delta being balls, vias, and package spreading inductance. Two traps recur: uncalibrated probe extension (~1–2 ps) rotating phase, and spatial attenuation (transimpedance) making Z and L look artificially small — the measurement is an insertion loss, not a deliverable impedance.
- **Bottom line (§3.15)** — eleven numbered takeaways tying together both impedance definitions, the 0.1 Ω one-port accuracy floor, the 25 Ω × S21 rule, fixture de-embedding, insertion-loss artifacts, and the capacitor and on-die measurement workflows; followed by six references.
