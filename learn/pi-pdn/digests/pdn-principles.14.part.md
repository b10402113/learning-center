# L1 overview

- **Chapter 9.1–9.3: transient current, target impedance, and estimating dI** — argues that a PDN spec cannot be written without knowing the die's worst-case transient current; a flat (resistive) impedance profile turns Z into the sole noise driver, giving Ztarget = ΔV/ΔI; when the vendor will not supply dI, 50% of the maximum rail current is the working estimate, and a lower-than-needed Ztarget is "buying insurance."
- **Chapter 9.4–9.5: the clock-edge current and the PDN loop** — builds the die current model from a CMOS inverter driving a lumped capacitive load; when that load is referenced only to Vss the PDN sees one pulse per clock edge, and when referenced to both rails (the usual case) rail-to-rail loop current flows on both rising and falling transitions, so logic draws once per cycle while the clock tree draws twice, producing impulses at 2× the clock frequency.
- **Chapter 9.6–9.7: measurement and the real mechanism of noise** — an Arduino-class embedded controller instrumented with quiet HIGH/LOW pins and 450 Ω + 50 Ω scope feeds shows ~20 mV Vdd collapse and ~35 mV Vss bounce per clock edge; the root cause is charge depletion of the on-die capacitance, and the ODC droop (v/L) — not di/dt — is the forcing function that pulls replenishing current through the package inductance.
- **Chapter 9.8–9.9: impedance-peak equations and the three canonical waveforms** — the die-pad view is a parallel RLC and the board/VNA view is a series RLC at the same fres, characterized by Z0 = sqrt(L/C), Q = Z0/R, and peak ≈ Q·Z0 (accurate only for high Q); the design loop is RLC→properties, measurement→RLC, and properties→RLC. The three test waveforms are impulse (one clock edge, or an anti-impulse), step (abrupt dynamic current turn-on), and resonance (repeated steps at fres), used both to predict noise and to reverse-engineer the PDN model.

# L2 detail

- `#L17337-L17446` — Section 9.1: why constant current would reduce PDN design to IR drop alone, and why the transient current waveform, its spectral content and timing, together with the impedance, set the die-pad noise; introduces the three canonical stimuli (single clock pulse/impulse, step, resonant) and the rule that knowing any two of current waveform, impedance profile, and voltage response yields the third; notes the LTI assumption used to mix time-domain current with frequency-domain Z.
- `#L17447-L17499` — Section 9.2: for a flat, resistor-like impedance profile ΔV = ΔI·Z, so a noise tolerance ΔV sets the target impedance Ztarget = ΔV/ΔI (worked example: 1 V rail, 1 A transient, 5% = 50 mV gives 50 mΩ); defines transient current as Imax − Imin between idle and heavy-logic operation, with Imin from static plus leakage current.
- `#L17500-L17550` — Section 9.3: how to bound dI when the vendor supplies only maximum rail current — the two extremes (idle-to-full = 100% of Imax, constant code = ~0%) bracket the answer, typical FPGA/large-gate-count parts swing about 50%, and mobile parts can go 1%→100% in microseconds; conclusion: use 50% of Imax as the default estimate, treat the resulting Ztarget as uncertain, and buy margin deliberately.
- `#L17551-L17725` — Section 9.4: replaces the constant-current die model with a gate-level one built on a CMOS inverter (p-FET/n-FET push-pull) driving a lumped capacitive load; with capacitance referenced to Vss only, PDN current flows just on the rising edge as a once-per-clock pulse train; defines the clock-edge current and charge Qclk_edge = C·Vdd (area under the pulse), the dynamic current as that charge per period, doubling of average current (not pulse shape) when clock frequency doubles, and on-die capacitance smoothing the spikes into an averaged waveform by the time current reaches the package.
- `#L17726-L17848` — Section 9.5: the usual case where load capacitance is referenced to both Vdd and Vss (roughly symmetric); on a rising edge displacement current splits half to Vss and half back to Vdd, so only half the switching charge loops through the PDN, and on the falling edge the Vdd-referenced capacitor drives loop current too — hence rail-to-rail PDN current on both transitions, p/n-channel currents at twice the loop current, clock tree at 2× clock frequency, combinational logic once per cycle on the rising edge.
- `#L17849-L17953` — Section 9.6: measurement method — peg one pin HIGH (Vdd sense) and one LOW (Vss sense), route both through 450 Ω into 50 Ω coax/scope (10 mA draw), toggle a third pin; on a 16 MHz controller the on-die Vdd collapses ~20 mV and Vss rises ~35 mV on each edge at 2× clock frequency, larger when I/O latches fire; asymmetry attributed to differing combinational activity or return-path SSN; closes with dynamic current = averaged clock-edge current over one cycle.
- `#L17954-L18213` — Section 9.7: the causality discussion — capacitor dv/dt = i/C and inductor di/dt = v/L, with "cause vs effect" depending on the situation; in CMOS PDNs charge depletion of the ODC causes the droop (ΔV = ΔQ/C), and that droop across package inductance is the forcing function that draws replenishment current (di/dt = v/L), so lower L brings current faster and reduces noise; SPICE-style example with a single clock-edge impulse shows a 31 mV ODC drop, doubling di/dt when L goes from 50.7 pH to 25.3 pH, +25% fres and lower Q; contrasts with I/O ground bounce, where di/dt and mutual loop inductance are the aggressor.
- `#L18214-L18327` — Section 9.8: the series-vs-parallel RLC case study — board/VNA view is series (resonant dip to R), die-pad view is parallel with a low-impedance board short (resonant peak); same fres = 1/(2π√LC), characteristic impedance Z0 = √(L/C) (explicitly not a transmission-line Z0), Q = Z0/R, and peak ≈ Q·Z0 valid only for high Q; the three usage modes (RLC→properties, measured fres/Q/dip→RLC, desired properties→RLC); worked design: 50 nF ODC, 100 MHz and 100 mΩ peak → L = 50.7 pH, Z0 = 32 mΩ, Q = 3.15, loop R = 10.1 mΩ.
- `#L18328-L18411` — Section 9.9: the three characterizing current waveforms — impulse (single clock-edge charge, with anti-impulse as a missing pulse), step (abrupt jump from minimum to maximum dynamic current, e.g. clock start/stop or pipeline restart after a cache miss, with clock-edge-fast rise time), and resonance (repeated steps at the PDN resonant frequency, low probability but devastating); they serve both to predict voltage noise from known RLC and to reverse-engineer RLC from measured response; case study uses 1.55 A dynamic current so Ztarget (32 mΩ for ±5% at 1 V) lands exactly on Z0, and a constant-current-source SPICE stimulus is the simplest valid drive.
