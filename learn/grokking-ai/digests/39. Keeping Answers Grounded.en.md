# L1 overview

- **The right chunk is not enough**: retrieval works perfectly and the chunk says "orders more than 30 minutes late get a credit of 20% of the order total; credits cannot be paid out to a card", yet the model's third sentence says the money goes back to the card in 3–5 business days — the opposite of the chunk. **Grounding** is keeping every answer true to its evidence; **RAG reduces hallucination, it does not end it**.
- **Three failure modes even with the right chunk**: **contradict** the evidence, **embellish** it (promise free delivery the chunk never states), or **blend** it with memorized facts (many companies do refund to cards in 3–5 days — the model repeated that pattern). All three are hallucination: stating something the evidence does not support.
- **Definition**: **groundedness** is whether every claim is supported by the evidence supplied. Grounding means the model uses the context, admits gaps, and the system **proves claim by claim** that it did both. Grounding is **not** correctness — a grounded answer built on old evidence is still old — and no model can be bought that never adds claims, so grounding belongs to the **system**.
- **Four stacked layers**, each catching what the one before lets through, over **retrieval quality as the floor**: (1) **instructions and framing** ("answer only from the context", "say when the context lacks the answer", label each chunk with source and date — cheap, reduces invented claims, but the refund case had these and still failed); (2) **citations** as a code-checkable contract — the model returns claim–source pairs and a post check fails claims with no source or a chunk ID not in the context (nearly free, but asks only whether the source exists, not whether it supports the claim); (3) **entailment checks** — for each claim, is it **entailed** by its cited chunk (the chunk, taken as true, forces the claim true)? An **NLI model** (small, fast, cheap; labels supported/contradicted/neither) or an **LLM judge** with a rubric (handles harder wording, costs more). Check **per claim**, not per answer, so two correct sentences cannot hide the wrong one — and only the failed sentence needs fixing.
- **Ladder and stakes**: lightest is **strip or hedge** the claim (remove it or note the documents don't confirm it), then **retry once** with tightened instructions, heaviest is **refuse and escalate** to a human. Choose by **stakes** — high-stakes claims (money amounts, allergy information) must pass or not ship; low-stakes (how to track an order) can be stripped or hedged. Cost sets timing: a check may add 100–300 ms and a fraction of a cent, so high-stakes paths get a **synchronous** check (user waits, no streaming) while low-stakes get a **sampled asynchronous** check (5%, failures join the golden set). Checking everything synchronously slows every reply; checking nothing lets a wrong refund rule reach a customer.
- **Designed refusal (layer 4)** is part of the product, not an error message. Triggers: **empty retrieval**, a **failed check** that persists after retry, or an **out-of-scope question** (e.g. taxes). A weak refusal ("I cannot answer that") leaves the customer stuck; a strong one says it could not confirm the refund policy for this case and offers a next step (a support agent). Refusal fails in two directions — answering wrongly (should have refused) and refusing wrongly (could have answered, so a human handles a simple case) — and pushing one down pushes the other up; a system that refuses everything has no wrong answers and no value, so the golden set must include **unanswerable questions where refusal is correct**.
- **Metric**: **faithfulness** is the share of claims the context supports, measured on the golden set before a change ships and on production traffic via sampled async checks. Track it beside **retrieval recall**: low recall = retrieval failing; low faithfulness with good recall = writing failing. The deepest failure is a wrong answer where a refusal was available — a customer recovers from a refusal, but a confident wrong answer about money or allergies can cost someone's health.
