# L1 overview

- Recognition move: "the harmful content risk funnel at platform scale with fraud's adversary attached" — cheap checks on all 200M daily uploads, expensive checks only on the risky part, against an opponent who changes tactics in hours while retraining takes days or weeks.
- Policy comes first because a model can only learn what policy defines: each category (e.g. graphic violence) gets its own threshold set by trust and safety with legal, per region; CSAM gets a separate lane matched by perceptual hash and always blocked, with trained staff deciding new cases, never a score.
- Measure in views, not uploads: North Star is prevalence of violating views (reviewers label a random sample of views, each number carrying a confidence interval; 0.05% harassment ≈ 0.04–0.07%), plus time to action measured in views accrued; guardrails are false removal rate and appeal overturn rate (both needed because many wrongly removed users never appeal).
- Three label feeds: reviewer decisions (best), confirmed false positives from restored appeals, and user reports (weakest — weighted by reporter reputation; a brigade can raise priority but never remove).
- Four-stage funnel: stage 1 perceptual hashes and hard rules, stage 2 fast classifiers on all 200M items (flags ~1% ≈ 2M/day ≈ 23/s), stage 3 heavy multimodal models reading pixels, caption, OCR and ASR (~0.1% ≈ 200,000/day), stage 4 human review; each extra 0.1% flagged adds 200,000 heavy calls, so the flag rate sets the GPU bill (≈2 GPU-s per heavy call → ~46 GPUs) and the review band sets headcount (~250 decisions/shift → ~800 shifts for 200,000).
- The action ladder turns scores into actions — allow, warn, age gate, reduce, remove — with thresholds owned by trust and safety, not the model team; reach-aware rescoring re-scores each item at view tiers (e.g. 10,000 views), the review queue orders by uncertainty × reach, and fail open/closed is chosen per category and surface.
- Three-speed defense: incident lane (minutes — new hashes, rules, kill switch), review loop (hours — reviewer labels and confirmed removals join the hash list), retraining (weeks — must pass a backtest then shadow scoring); reviewer hours and mental health (exposure budgets) are the hard limit, not GPUs.
