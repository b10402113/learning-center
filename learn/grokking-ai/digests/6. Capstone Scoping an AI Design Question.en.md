# L1 overview

- The vague prompt ("leadership wants to use AI, what would you build?" at a company with ~25 million monthly users and ~500,000 orders/day) deliberately omits three things every design needs: a **feature**, a **metric**, and a **family**. **Scoping** fills those gaps in the first ~8 of 45 minutes, and the interviewer grades two signals: **range** (do you see multiple kinds of AI?) and **decision** (can you pick one and defend it?).
- Five moves. Move 1: show the **menu** in ~30 seconds, grouped by family — predictive (ETA prediction, restaurant recommendations, promotion abuse detection), generative (support assistant, review summaries, AI dish descriptions), agentic (an ordering assistant that builds the cart and pays).
- Move 2: decide on **impact** plus **tractability**, judged by three questions — do labels already exist, is the knowledge available, and what does a mistake cost? ETA wins predictive because 500,000 deliveries/day yield 500,000 new labels/day; the support assistant wins generative because support has a known price per ticket and internal knowledge; the ordering agent is weakest because autonomous payment trust is unsolved.
- Moves 3–4: name the family and **design centers** — for the chosen support assistant that is generative with retrieval (**RAG**), pushed slightly agentic by small refunds; centers are retrieval/context assembly (the **prompt**), cost control via model routing, and guardrails around the refund action. Then cut scope out loud: English text chat, Tier 1 tickets, refunds capped at an assumed $10; voice, other languages, and large refunds are out.
- Move 5 puts numbers on it: 8% of orders become support contacts → 40,000 conversations/day (~1 every 2 seconds); 60% Tier 1 → 24,000/day; ~10 model calls per conversation → ~5 calls/second. Targets: 50% Tier 1 resolution rate (≈12,000/day), customer satisfaction as guardrail, ~1 s time to first token, ~5 s time to finish, ~13 cents per conversation against a $4 human baseline — saving ~$48,000/day for ~$5,000 of model cost, about a tenth of the human work removed.
- Seniority ladder: mid-level says "build a chatbot and measure how happy users are" (a direction, not a target); senior makes all five moves unprompted; staff questions the need (a live order status screen might remove contacts with no model) and sees the dependency — the answer to "where is my order" is only as good as the ETA.
