# L1 overview

- Three first-minute tools: the **form** (how a question is given), the **lane** (what your role must go deep on), and four **areas** to cover every time.
- Five forms: (1) known ML product — hour goes to data, features, training, evaluation; (2) gen AI product — goes to retrieval, safety, prompt; (3) AI added to an existing system — goes to scoping; (4) agent — goes to control and approvals; (5) critique or scale — order problems most harmful first. A vague "design whatever AI feature you'd like" is form 3 in disguise and tests scoping.
- Forms are not families: a family is what the model mainly outputs (number/label vs content vs actions); forms 1, 2, 4 match the three families, forms 3 and 5 can hold any family.
- Lane by role: a senior SWE loop keeps the model a box (architecture, data flow, serving, failure handling, cost); an ML engineer loop opens it (models, features, training, metrics); AI/platform goes deep on GPU utilization and batching; a manager on build-vs-buy and phased rollout.
- The four areas: requirements with concrete numbers, the complete loop back into training/evaluation, cost and latency realism, and failure honesty. Worked targets: resolve ≥50% of routine questions, first words within ~1 second, ≤13 cents per conversation.
- Cost realism anchors: a key-value read ~$0.000001 and ~2 ms; one hosted LLM chat turn ~1 cent and ~2 s — 10,000× the cost and 1,000× the latency, so cache and route ~80% of traffic to a small model. Avoid model math, model names, and framework trivia.
