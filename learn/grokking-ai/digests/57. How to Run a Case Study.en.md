# L1 overview

- The "clock": seven-step framework phases get fixed minutes — framing and success L0–L8, data and model 5 min each, serving path 12 min, keeping it working plus costs 13 min, last 2 min slack; every phase ends with a spoken exit criterion, and an empty rubric row reads as missing knowledge, not missing time.
- The recognition move, said by minute 2: name the known system plus what changes ("this is the RAG pipeline, facing customers instead of employees"), saving ~15 minutes for the last two steps; if nothing fits, the family test (what does the model mainly output?) picks a skeleton — score→funnel, content→pipeline, actions→loop with fixed limits.
- Three mechanics protect the clock: announce the plan in minute one, timebox phases out loud ("about five minutes on data and labels"), and park rabbit holes (side model, exotic failure, library debate — each worth 10 min) named in one sentence and listed on the board; never park a direct question.
- Interruption moves: the redirect (skip to architecture, restate targets), the stress test (answer from the fallback ladder — cache, second provider, help articles, human — and end with the monitoring hook/alarm), the hallucination question (groundedness check, one retry, then "cannot confirm" plus sources), and the depth probe (two levels — mechanism then trade-off — then offer; a third unasked level wastes minutes).
- On disagreement, restate the deciding variable (knowledge gap vs behavior gap → retrieval over fine-tuning) rather than repeating your choice; an interviewer's silence is usually note-taking, so say the exit criterion and continue.
- Practice and grade: full sample run lands ~40,000 conversations/day at ~12.6 cents ≈ $5,000/day; grade a recording (never memory) against a ~5-dimension rubric where "passing" is a working serving path and "strong hire" is latency/cost/failure on every arrow raised unprompted; with 25 minutes keep all seven steps and cut depth, not steps.
