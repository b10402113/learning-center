# L1 overview

- Start with scale, not a model: 50 million videos, a ~200 ms latency budget, one user opening the app at 8pm after three cooking videos, 20 videos on screen. One accurate model at one microsecond per video would take 50 seconds for the full catalog, so a model cheap enough to score everything is too simple to rank well.
- The answer is a **retrieve-then-rank funnel**: candidate generation narrows 50 million to ~500 with cheap sources, ranking scores those 500 with a slower model and keeps 50, reranking applies rules to pick the 20 on screen. A video that candidate generation drops never comes back — ranking only sorts what it receives.
- The business truth is watch time per session and the **daily return rate** (share of users back the next day), but that arrives slowly, so train on **engagement** (a proxy: a click followed by a meaningful watch) that arrives within minutes. Record the goal and its proxy so the interview can discuss the gap.
- Each stage gets its own offline metric from saved logs: candidate generation on **recall at k** (share of engaged videos found in the top k candidates), ranking on **AUC** (0.5 is guessing, 1.0 is perfect) plus precision near the top, all judged on held-out data. Offline wins only earn an **A/B test**, not a launch.
- Impression logs record user, video, and slot, and become labels: a click plus a 30-second watch is positive; shown-and-skipped is negative. Two flaws: labels cover only shown videos (the model learns from its own past choices), and they carry **position bias** (slot 1 gets more clicks than slot 10), so the model repeats yesterday's order. Left uncorrected this is a feedback loop that narrows the feed.
- Candidate generation runs three sources in parallel because each finds videos the others miss: a **two-tower model** (user tower + video tower; user vectors sit near likely-enjoyed videos, video vectors are user-independent so they are precomputed into an **ANN** index that quickly finds neighbors but sometimes misses the closest), **co-occurrence** (videos often watched by the same people), and **regional trends plus new uploads** (same for everyone). Merging removes duplicates and already-watched videos.
- First ranker: a **gradient-boosted tree** model predicting engagement. **Reranking** is a rules layer kept outside the model — diversity (at most two videos from one creator per page) and a freshness rule saving slots for recent uploads — so the product team changes a rule without retraining.
- Serving timeline: ~10 ms read user state, ~15 ms user tower, ~35 ms three sources plus merge to ~500, ~80 ms rank all 500 in one batched call and keep 50, ~85 ms rules pick 20 — well inside 200 ms. An overnight precomputed list would miss tonight's cooking videos and anything uploaded after the job ran, so retrieve per request.
- **Fail soft**: a broken nightly index build keeps serving the last good index; a ranker timeout sorts candidates by recent popularity; a full outage serves a saved regional trending list. Tag every reduced response in the logs so it does not mislead the next training run.
- Cadences differ by part: session state seconds (interests change with each click), ANN index nightly, ranker daily. **Cold start**: new users see trending videos for their region and language; a new video has zero views so co-occurrence cannot find it — content features (title, thumbnail) let the video tower make its vector before the first view, plus an **exploration boost** for a small share of impressions.
- Position-bias fixes: make the slot number a training feature (so the model explains part of each click by the slot) and serve every candidate the same slot value, e.g. zero; plus **propensity weighting** (a propensity is the estimated chance a user looks at a slot; clicks in rarely seen slots get more weight).
- The **proxy trap**: optimizing only for clicks rewards a misleading thumbnail that wins the click and loses the viewer, so the target is click plus meaningful watch, with not-interested rates as guardrails, and the A/B test decides on the daily return rate. To diagnose, log each video's source and rank so you can tell whether a good video was never retrieved or retrieved and ranked too low; test a fourth source in **shadow mode** first.
- Capacity math: 128 numbers per video vector × 4 bytes × 50 million ≈ 25.6 GB of raw vectors before index links; 100 million users × 3 opens/day = 300 million requests, about 3,500/s average and 7,000/s peak; 500 candidates per request ≈ 1.75 million scores/s; about 6 billion impression rows logged per day — plan that storage before promising daily retraining.
