# L1 overview

- The **classic skeleton** (requirements, API, a service and a database, then scaling) breaks in two places: a model's quality is a number rather than a fact, and the real outcome arrives after the response so the answer must end in a loop. Fix: the seven-step framework, an order of thinking, not a script — and a checklist when asked to critique a finished design.
- Step 1 has two moves: name the family from the output, then name a **known system** you have studied (ride-hailing ETA, but kitchen prep time changes every minute); then state the scope explicitly and ask to proceed — a wrong scope costs little at minute three and most of the interview at minute 25.
- Step 2 puts numbers on three axes: **quality bar** (average miss under 5 minutes), a **guardrail metric** that must not worsen (share of very late orders), **latency** (~100 ms at checkout), and scale/cost (~500,000 orders a day).
- Step 3 maps the data via **labels** and **label delay** (about an hour for delivery; chargebacks weeks later) and **freshness** (kitchen load must refresh within seconds; fraud needs the last few seconds even though its label is weeks late). The support assistant often has no labels, so design signals instead.
- Step 4 starts from a **baseline** (restaurant's historical hourly average; a hosted LLM with clear instructions; one model with two or three tools) and upgrades only with a stated condition tested on **held-out** orders. Prompting comes before fine-tuning; missing facts are a retrieval problem, not a fine-tuning trigger.
- Step 5 (largest share, ~12 minutes) answers three questions: what can be precomputed, which requests can take a cheap path via a **router**, and what happens on timeout or outage (every arrow into a model needs a timeout and a fallback). Step 6: log inputs and outputs, capture the real outcome, retrain, and release in three stages — offline test, shadow deployment then A-B test, then staged rollout with rollback and drift monitoring.
- Step 7 closes with two ledgers: the **failure ledger** (top ~3 breakages each with a response, including feedback loops that amplify bias like clickbait) and the **cost ledger** (unit cost × volume; 2M of 10M daily requests routed to an LLM at ~1 cent is ~$20,000/day, 5× more without the router). Budget: end step 1 near minute 3, keep the last two minutes free; with only 10 minutes build around steps 1, 5, and 7.
