# L1 overview

- The **family test** asks one question — what does the model output? A number or label is predictive (family 1), content is generative (family 2), an action is agentic (family 3). The model type does not decide the family: an LLM that outputs one ticket category is still family 1.
- Family 1 graded on four details: **labels** (delivery time within the hour; fraud chargeback weeks later), the **funnel** (a cheap stage cuts millions of items to a few hundred, a richer model ranks them), **evaluation** (AUC offline where 0.5 is chance and 1.0 perfect; CTR live, then an A-B test), and the **feedback loop**. Classic failure: reaching for a chatbot/LLM to rank a feed, which ignores your proprietary click data and costs too much at ~500 candidates per request.
- Family 2 starts from a **foundation model** and needs three things around it: RAG to feed your own documents, cost control (billing per token ≈ three quarters of an English word; streaming does not lower cost), and mistake catching (**hallucination** checks, policy compliance). Family 1 often gets quality signals free; family 2 must design evaluation — **golden set** plus an **LLM judge** with human sampling. Classic failure: training a foundation model from scratch.
- Family 3 is an agent loop: model picks a step, a **tool** runs it, model reads the result and picks again. Analogy: a capable intern who also has your passwords. Controls live in code, not the prompt — the **tool interface** and permissions (task-shaped tools beat a general database tool), a **budget** (hard step limit) whose exhaustion hands the task to a person, and **approval gates**. Two failure modes: too much freedom (one trick avoids every system) and too little (every low-risk step needs approval, reviewers rubber-stamp).
- Maturity differs: family 1 is settled (funnels, A-B tests), family 2 is emerging (RAG, LLM judges), family 3 is youngest so interviewers expect caution. Mixed products need three moves: name each family, name each **seam** where one family's output becomes another's input, and confirm where to go deep.
- Worked example: a 50-minute-late ticket — routing classifier at 0.94 confidence (family 1, seam 1), policy-grounded reply draft (family 2, seam 2), agent-proposed credit checked in code against ownership and a limit (family 3).
