# L1 overview

- Recognition move: three lanes with different time limits sharing one data spine — a real-time transcription pipeline (captions within 1–2 s), an async generation pipeline (summary within minutes), and RAG over the archive (answers can wait weeks) — all reading and writing one meeting record of attendees, shares, and consent state, so no lane calls another.
- Scope and hard gate: live captions, post-call summaries with action items, and questions about past meetings are in; live translation and coaching are parked by name; consent is a hard gate (every participant sees a recording notice, no consent, no recording), and the ACL of who may see the meeting derives from attendees plus explicit shares.
- The signature embarrassment is named in minute three: an action item with the wrong owner (Chen agreed to send the contract, summary says Ben will), which shapes every later check.
- ASR basics: WER (word error rate — a seven-word sentence with three errors ≈ 43%) ignores who spoke, so speaker attribution accuracy is tracked separately and reported by slice (e.g. noisy rooms), because a wrong speaker becomes a wrong owner; room microphones need diarization, the likely source of most wrong owners.
- Evaluation differs by lane: summaries have no single right answer, so offline a judge (LLM scoring against a rubric) is anchored by a human-graded sample, and online edit rate stands in for quality with reassignment rate for action items; archive answers use retrieval recall and faithfulness, which point to different faults.
- Pipeline shape: the post-call path is a workflow (code runs fixed steps in a fixed order), not an agent — map-reduce summarization over ~10-minute chunks keeps decisions and open questions as separate fields so a minute-5 question settled at minute 90 gets connected; an extraction pass makes each action item a record with task, owner, and source moment (so a hallucinated commitment becomes visible and an unclear moment yields "owner unconfirmed", never a guess; the owner must be an attendee).
- Traffic and serving: many meetings end on the hour or half-hour so the load is comb-shaped (~200/s at 10:00, ~17× the weekday average); a queue turns spikes into a backlog, a worker pool grows a few minutes before each hour, and the promise is "within 10 minutes, never wrong" — never switch to a weaker model to catch up.
- Other mechanics: hybrid retrieval (keyword + embedding) limited to meetings the user can see; behavioral labels (edit rate, reassignment rate) come free; edited summaries fine-tune where customers allow; rollout is offline metrics → shadow → canary with stops on either rate; drift shows first as one organization's share of low-confidence words; deletion uses tombstone-then-propagate and email holds only a link (an invite to a 500-person list for a 6-person meeting would leak to 494 people); cost is unit cost × volume — transcription ≈ $1,000/week of ~$1.07M total, so price audio minutes, not just tokens.

# L2 detail

- `#L1-L147` — Design an AI meeting assistant: three lanes (real-time captions, async summary, archive RAG) on one meeting record, consent and ACL gates, the wrong-owner signature embarrassment, WER plus speaker attribution by slice, judge-and-human-anchor offline evaluation with edit/reassignment rate online, queued map-reduce workflow with source-moment action items, comb-shaped load, and shadow/canary rollout with tombstone deletion.
