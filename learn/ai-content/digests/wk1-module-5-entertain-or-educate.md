# Digest — Module 5 - Entertain or Educate

## Overview (L1)

- Splits all content into a simple two-way classification: it either entertains or it educates.
- Claims every viral or high-usage piece of content gives the viewer some form of "value," and that value classifies as either entertainment or education.
- Argues that educational content pays larger dividends than entertainment for building a business.
- Uses the "MrBeast test" to show consumers pay a large premium for education (a skill) but very little for entertainment.
- Concludes that if the end intent is monetizing content, education is the better path, and previews the next question: what topic to educate on.

## Sections (L2)

### entertain-or-educate

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/6-Module 5 - Entertain or Educate/6-Module_5_-_Entertain_or_Educate.en.vtt#entertain-or-educate]]`
- Summary: Proposes the core split of all content into two categories — entertainment and education — with subcategories under each.
- Key claims: every single piece of viral or high-use content exists because it gives some form of value to the viewer; that value is a function of whether it is for entertainment or for education; entertainment and education each may have subcategories, but the core split is entertainment vs. education.
- Learner-relevant: Provides the first lens for defining a content identity — every piece you make will lean entertain or educate.

### value-and-the-mrbeast-test

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/6-Module 5 - Entertain or Educate/6-Module_5_-_Entertain_or_Educate.en.vtt#value-and-the-mrbeast-test]]`
- Summary: Explains that consumers place a low premium on entertainment and a high premium on education, illustrated with MrBeast.
- Key claims: audiences would not pay much (if anything) to watch MrBeast's entertainment videos; Netflix costs only a few dollars for unlimited entertainment; if MrBeast sold toolkits, custom software, or consulting about how he creates content, many people would pay a lot — some upwards of $1,000 or more — because education gives the buyer a skill in return.
- Learner-relevant: Reframes how much money and trust are available on the education side versus the entertainment side.

### education-builds-trust-and-scales

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/6-Module 5 - Entertain or Educate/6-Module_5_-_Entertain_or_Educate.en.vtt#education-builds-trust-and-scales]]`
- Summary: Makes the case that education builds more trust and scales better for a business, while allowing entertainment as a legitimate personal choice.
- Key claims: content that educates builds more trust and gives more value to viewers; if the end intent is to build a business and monetize content, educational content is the one that scales better; creating entertainment content is fine if it is what you are passionate about, but it monetizes less well.
- Learner-relevant: A decision rule — if the goal is a content business, choose the education path.

### choosing-your-topic

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/6-Module 5 - Entertain or Educate/6-Module_5_-_Entertain_or_Educate.en.vtt#choosing-your-topic]]`
