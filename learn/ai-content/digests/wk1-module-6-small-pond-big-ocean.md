# Digest — Module 6 - Small Pond, Big Ocean

## Overview (L1)

- Answers "what content will you actually post," aiming to leave the learner with a few concrete topic ideas.
- Introduces the small pond vs. big ocean concept: beginners naturally pick broad topics that are dominated by established players.
- Shows why a newcomer loses when competing head-on with a huge, established channel in a broad space like AI.
- Prescribes becoming a big fish in a small pond by narrowing your focus at the start.
- Introduces Naval Ravikant's "bear on a unicycle" to justify combining AI with a personal niche.
- Notes you can expand outward into the bigger ocean once you have gained a following.

## Sections (L2)

### small-pond-vs-big-ocean

- Summary: Frames the module around playing in a small pond versus the big ocean, using AI as the running example.

- Learner-relevant: Gives the strategic frame for choosing how narrow your content topic should be.

### the-big-ocean-problem

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/7-Module 6 - Small Pond, Big Ocean/7-Module_6_-_Small_Pond,_Big_Ocean.en.vtt#the-big-ocean-problem]]`
- Summary: Uses Dan Martell as the example of an established creator who can succeed with general beginner-AI content, and contrasts it with a tiny account.
- Key claims: a "basics of using Claude" video from a nobody likely won't fly because many other tutorials already exist; Dan Martell is well established in business/tech, has published books, has ~3 million YouTube subscribers, and can make more general content; if you have only 100–200 followers and recreate his general AI video, you likely won't get viewership — you're a small fish in the big ocean; viewers would rather listen to someone already established.
- Learner-relevant: Explains why copying a big player's broad topic fails for new accounts — it is about positioning, not skill.

### be-a-big-fish-in-a-small-pond

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/7-Module 6 - Small Pond, Big Ocean/7-Module_6_-_Small_Pond,_Big_Ocean.en.vtt#be-a-big-fish-in-a-small-pond]]`
- Summary: Recommends narrowing your focus to become a big fish in a small pond.
- Key claims: instead of competing on "everyone talking about AI," narrow down at least in the beginning; narrowing means less competition; you can always expand outward later once you get a followership.
- Learner-relevant: The actionable strategy for a first-time creator to gain traction.

### bear-on-a-unicycle

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/7-Module 6 - Small Pond, Big Ocean/7-Module_6_-_Small_Pond,_Big_Ocean.en.vtt#bear-on-a-unicycle]]`
- Summary: Recounts the "bear on a unicycle" idea (attributed to Naval Ravikant on a podcast) as the reason combining topics works.
- Key claims: a bear that is semi-interesting and a unicycle that is semi-interesting, put together, become outstanding; people get more interested when two or more things are combined into one cohesive whole.
- Learner-relevant: The conceptual justification for combining a broad topic with your niche instead of competing in the general space.

### combine-ai-with-your-niche

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/7-Module 6 - Small Pond, Big Ocean/7-Module_6_-_Small_Pond,_Big_Ocean.en.vtt#combine-ai-with-your-niche]]`
- Summary: Gives concrete examples of creators who combine AI with an area of expertise to carve out a niche.
- Key claims: combine AI with a niche you have expertise in; Mike Sherrard (real estate agent) makes content around Claude + real estate; Mark Coles (fitness coaching business) talks about AI in the context of fitness coaching; Dr Mukesh Aggarwal talks about it in the context of his work as a doctor; these examples may not have the highest subscriber/view counts, but you don't need a big follower count to drive business.
- Learner-relevant: Shows what a viable "small pond" looks like in practice.

### expanding-outward-over-time

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/2-Wk1 - Your Content Identity/7-Module 6 - Small Pond, Big Ocean/7-Module_6_-_Small_Pond,_Big_Ocean.en.vtt#expanding-outward-over-time]]`
- Summary: Shows the small-pond strategy is a starting point, not a ceiling, with the author's and others' histories.
- Key claims: you can branch out and become a bigger creator once you gain experience; the author's older videos skewed toward branding/advertising content due to a background managing brands at Unilever, HelloFresh, Virgin, and others, and only became more general a few years in; Alex started on gym management (a narrow subset of the business ocean) and later expanded to broader business after ~8 years; the general guidance is to focus first on a smaller pond and become the big fish there before expanding.
- Learner-relevant: Reassures the learner that narrowing now does not lock the topic forever.
