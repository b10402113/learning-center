# Digest — Module 18 - Trust and Truth

## Overview (L1)

- Building a good reputation as a creator matters, especially when content drives business — an audience that doesn't trust you erodes the associated business.
- The guiding principle: under-promise and over-deliver, because the hook/title/thumbnail is a promise the content must meet.
- Clickbait isn't automatically bad, but it balloons expectations and leaves a gap; that gap erodes reputation, and platforms actively optimize against it.
- Platform incentives align with avoiding clickbait: YouTube's goal is watch time, and clickbait gets clicks but early exits. YouTube names misleading clickbait in its spam policy and its clickbait test is lots of clicks but people leaving early.
- Volume allows experimenting early, but reputation erodes slowly; long-term, showing your face builds trust faster, though faceless is completely fine to start.

### under-promise-over-deliver

- Summary: The core trust principle: whenever you can, under-promise and over-deliver; the hook/packaging is the promise, and meeting it (at minimum) is the bar.
- Key claims: content creators often hype promises then under-deliver; ideally over-deliver on the promise; "quality" here means usefulness matching the promise, not production value.
- Learner-relevant: the single rule that keeps trust intact while creating.

### hook-is-a-promise
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/6-Module 18 - Trust and Truth/6-Module_18_-_Trust_and_Truth.en.vtt#hook-is-a-promise]]`
- Summary: The hook applies to all packaging a viewer sees first — first three seconds, title, thumbnail — and is a promise that clicking will deliver at least the expected quality.
- Key claims: if you wrote the hook first or last, recheck it still matches the finished content; it's ~50% hook and 50% what you actually say, so if both are true you're good to ship.
- Learner-relevant: a pre-post check to keep packaging and content aligned.

### clickbait-and-reputation
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/6-Module 18 - Trust and Truth/6-Module_18_-_Trust_and_Truth.en.vtt#clickbait-and-reputation]]`
- Summary: Clickbait, used correctly, isn't automatically bad; the problem is it balloons the consumer's expectation, leaving a gap that erodes a factual reputation.
- Key claims: beginners should avoid all-caps click-baity titles; as the space matures, clickbait titles become a signal of creators you instantly wouldn't trust.
- Learner-relevant: why to resist empty-promise packaging even when it works short-term.

### platform-incentives-watch-time
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/6-Module 18 - Trust and Truth/6-Module_18_-_Trust_and_Truth.en.vtt#platform-incentives-watch-time]]`
- Summary: Interests are aligned on avoiding clickbait: YouTube wants watch time, and clickbait leads to clicks but early exits, so YouTube, Instagram and TikTok teams optimize against clickbait.
- Key claims: YouTube names misleading clickbait in its spam policy; its clickbait test is lots of clicks but people leaving early; platforms want to be trusted rather than filled with spam.
- Learner-relevant: the platform mechanics behind the trust principle.

### volume-and-reputation-erosion
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/6-Module 18 - Trust and Truth/6-Module_18_-_Trust_and_Truth.en.vtt#volume-and-reputation-erosion]]`
- Summary: If you post high volume, sometimes a script won't be up to date or checked; being aware lets you improve next time. Small creators posting shorts can experiment with click-baity hooks.
- Key claims: a real example — a post about Claude/Anthropic "paying $7,000 a month to learn AI" went viral (~900,000 views) but by the time viewers found it the round had closed; even though gained followers came, they attribute that experience to the creator. Long-term, adjust.
- Learner-relevant: acknowledges beginner experimentation while warning about slow reputation erosion.

### own-words-and-restraint
