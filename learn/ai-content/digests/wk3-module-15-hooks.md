# Digest — Module 15 - Hooks

## Overview (L1)

- The hook is the part of a video that decides whether someone stays or scrolls, and the module calls it "bar none the most important part of your script".
- Frames video performance as roughly 50% "packaging" (hook, title, thumbnail, first seconds) and 50% the actual content/body.
- Defines the hook differently by format: for shorts the first ~3 seconds; for long-form the title, thumbnail, and first 15–30 seconds. The course recommends starting with shorts.
- Teaches nine reusable hook shapes, each shown with a real post from AI and one from outside AI: contrarian, investigator, teacher, experimenter, fortuneteller, magician, curiosity gap, versus, and list.
- Adds a bonus visual hook (the hook need not be spoken) and a habit of bookmarking good hooks into a personal hook library, ultimately packaged as a downloadable /hooks skill.

## Sections (L2)

### why-the-hook-matters
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#why-the-hook-matters]]`
- Summary: In the first few seconds people decide whether to finish or scroll; attention is what viewers "pay", so the hook must make the right person care right now.
- Key claims: the hook's one job is to make the right person know why they should care right now; good content can underperform if the packaging/first seconds are weak.
- Learner-relevant: motivates why hooks are taught first in the creation week.

### packaging-and-content-fifty-fifty
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#packaging-and-content-fifty-fifty]]`
- Summary: For a ~60 second video, the last ~57 seconds are the content/body and the first ~3 seconds are the hook; performance is about half content and half packaging (which includes the hook).
- Key claims: content is the "meat"; packaging including the hook accounts for roughly half the performance and is over-indexed for getting served to the right people.
- Learner-relevant: quantifies how much effort the hook deserves.

### shorts-vs-long-form
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#shorts-vs-long-form]]`
- Summary: For shorts (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts) the hook is the first three seconds because everything auto-plays; for long-form (mainly YouTube) the hook is the title, thumbnail, and first 15–30 seconds.
- Key claims: the course covers both but recommends starting with shorts to build volume and posting habit; the patterns transfer to long-form.
- Learner-relevant: tells the learner which format and which hook definition to start with.

### the-contrarian
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-contrarian]]`
- Summary: Say the opposite of what your audience has been told. AI example: "stop prompting Claude" (Nate Herk), arguing a better way (spec-first) exists; non-AI example: "five diet trends that need to die" (The Fitness Chef).
- Key claims: works when a niche has well-known but outdated advice; the opposite take grabs attention.
- Learner-relevant: a shape for challenging common advice in the learner's niche.

### the-investigator
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-investigator]]`
- Summary: Dig into something and reveal a hidden thing most people miss. AI example: someone "leaked the full system prompt from Claude" (Nick Saraev, over 275,000 characters); non-AI example: "here's why I would never buy ground beef at Aldi... and it's not for the reason you think" (Bobby Parrish).
- Key claims: works well combined with fresh news in your niche; withholding the real reason sustains interest until the reveal.
- Learner-relevant: pairs investigation with current news to attract a niche audience.

### the-teacher
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-teacher]]`
- Summary: Promise a fix for a problem the audience already knows they have. AI example: "I'm going to show you how you can build your very own AI Jarvis in under three minutes" (Kevin Fremon); non-AI example: a deadlift tutorial for building the back (Jeremy Ethier).
- Key claims: a teacher hook must actually deliver the promise by the end or it erodes the creator's reputation; this shape may not suit everyone.
- Learner-relevant: tutorial-style content aimed at people already curious about a known problem.

### the-experimenter
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-experimenter]]`
- Summary: Test something yourself and report back what you found. AI example: trying a "Claude skill" that turns a side project into a launch video (Tunde Alao's Fact or Hype format); non-AI example: Guga Foods experimenting with food.
- Key claims: takes longer to prepare but can pay dividends, especially when capturing something you were doing anyway; verdict-style ("is this actually useful?") builds trust.
- Learner-relevant: turns routine tinkering into content with honest verdicts.

### the-fortuneteller
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-fortuneteller]]`
- Summary: Say what's coming next — point at a future the audience can picture and help them get ready. AI example: "SaaS is now dead... the next trillion-dollar company will be..." (Brand Nat); non-AI example: "if you have $100,000 invested by age 25..." (Humphrey Yang).
- Key claims: everyone is interested in their own/industry future; this shape can be blended with a contrarian opening; hook shapes can be mixed.
- Learner-relevant: prediction content for the learner's industry.

### the-magician
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-magician]]`
- Summary: Stop the scroll with something to look at — a result so impressive it looks impossible. AI example: showing OpenClaw controlling Blender on a Mac Mini on its own (Riley Brown); non-AI example: a first frame of phones with a knife, "is it cake or is it fake?" (Zach King).
- Key claims: a screen that appears to run on its own, or an unreal-looking first frame, is itself the hook.
- Learner-relevant: visual/result-first hooks for technical and non-technical niches.

### the-curiosity-gap
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-curiosity-gap]]`
- Summary: Open a gap in what they know and leave it open. AI example: "Don't tell your boss about this Excel trick" (Kevin Stratvert); general example: "This is the craziest fact I know" (Mark Rober).
- Key claims: probably the most flexible hook, especially for educational content; the "craziest fact/ tip I know" template is reusable across niches and a good general fallback.
- Learner-relevant: a default shape when no other hook fits.

### the-versus
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-versus]]`
- Summary: Put two things head to head and make the viewer pick. AI example: comparing ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude by their "superpowers" (Jeff Su); non-AI example: letting a five-year-old choose between $1 and $5 pasta (Max the Meat Guy).
- Key claims: side-by-side comparison creates an easy decision the audience wants to make.
- Learner-relevant: comparison content for tools/options in a niche.

### the-list
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#the-list]]`
- Summary: Promise a set number of things (a listicle). AI example: "three Claude Code plugins that stop Claude from wasting tokens" (Greg Isenberg); non-AI example: "three things I wish I knew before passive income" (Ali Abdaal).
- Key claims: a countable payoff creates an expectation of high ROI; for shorts keep the count modest (roughly under 10) so it still feels short and consumable.
- Learner-relevant: easy-to-understand value promise for a short.

### visual-hook
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#visual-hook]]`
- Summary: A bonus shape where the hook is not spoken but visual. The creator points at his screen with an orange on a chopstick, keeping production quality low to produce massive volume while creating a recognizable first frame.
- Key claims: a visual hook doubles as branding (orange ties to RoboNuggets); keep the visual hook related to the brand to drive familiarity, especially for faceless content.
- Learner-relevant: how to make a distinctive, repeatable first frame and brand cue.

### hook-library
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/3-Module_15_-_Hooks.en.vtt#hook-library]]`
- Summary: The hooks are only patterns; build your own library by bookmarking posts whose hooks stop your scroll, and follow accounts (e.g. Postprotocol) that break down viral formats.
- Key claims: as a producer, scrolling a niche-optimized account surfaces the latest trends and hooks; the algorithm will surface accounts that post good hook formats worth copying.
- Learner-relevant: a repeatable habit for sourcing new hooks.

### hooks-skill-and-prompts
- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/4-Wk3 - Content Creation/3-Module 15 - Hooks/index.html#hooks-skill-and-prompts]]`
- Summary: The written page ships a downloadable /hooks skill (all nine shapes, the bonus visual hook, and the lesson examples) and gives four copy-paste agent prompts plus the recommended action.
- Key claims: /hooks suggests 3 hooks in 3 different shapes per script; RUBRIC Scripts later uses /hooks to give 3 hooks for every script; the four prompts cover suggesting hooks for an idea, finding the right hook for a script, saving a liked hook to the library, and (recommended action) saying every hook out loud and cutting any word you stumble on; a hook is a promise the rest of the content must keep.
- Learner-relevant: the practical tool and prompts that operationalize the nine shapes.
