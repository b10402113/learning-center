# Digest — Module 25 - Master the Channels

## Overview (L1)

- Reframes "content" into just **three big kinds**: shorts (vertical reels/TikToks/YouTube Shorts), longs (mostly YouTube, landscape), and text (newsletters, X, LinkedIn posts).
- Gives the creator's force-ranked starting order for shorts: TikTok first (most sophisticated algorithm, fastest feedback even at zero followers), then Instagram (more social, better for leads/DMs/sponsors), then YouTube Shorts (best used as a supplement to long-form).
- Argues YouTube long-form builds the most trust, illustrated by a rough estimate that **one long video can equal about 60 shorts** (10 min vs 10 sec average watch time).
- Explains why not to start with text: X scrollers mostly lack buyer intent; newsletters have no discovery algorithm — most newsletter readers arrive via someone's videos first (the lesson author's own RoboNuggets newsletter attempt).
- Recommends starting with shorts and shipping ~5 this week; at this stage ship what you like as long as it is useful and truthful.
- Notes shorts/longs/text is shared creator/industry vocabulary, which helps when talking with other creators, sponsors, and partners.

### three-kinds-of-content

### shorts-tiktok-instagram-youtube

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Module 25 - Master the Channels/3-Module_25_-_Master_the_Channels.en.vtt#shorts-tiktok-instagram-youtube]]`
- Summary: Ranks the three primary short-form channels and explains each.
- Key claims: the three big shorts channels are TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts; posting vertical video to X or Threads is free and allowed but usually doesn't get traction; TikTok has the most sophisticated algorithm and gives the fastest feedback even with zero followers, making it the best first step; Instagram is harder to break through on for new accounts but is more social — creators and brands DM there, so it's better for leads and sponsors; YouTube Shorts is third because YouTube is fundamentally a long-form platform, so Shorts work best as a supplement to long-form; since posting is free, cross-post the same content to all three with only minor caption tweaks.
- Learner-relevant: tells the learner which channel to start with and why, plus the case for cross-posting all three.

### longs-youtube-builds-trust

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Module 25 - Master the Channels/3-Module_25_-_Master_the_Channels.en.vtt#longs-youtube-builds-trust]]`
- Summary: Explains why long-form YouTube is the strongest trust-builder but costs the most time.
- Key claims: long-form is undisputedly just YouTube; YouTube builds trust the most because viewers sit and learn from you over time; downside is that one long video takes a long time, during which you could have shipped multiple shorts and gotten more reps and feedback.
- Learner-relevant: frames the trade-off between trust (long-form) and reps/speed (shorts).

### one-long-equals-60-shorts

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Module 25 - Master the Channels/3-Module_25_-_Master_the_Channels.en.vtt#one-long-equals-60-shorts]]`
- Summary: Quantifies the reach/trust gap between long-form and shorts.
- Key claims: if a viewer watches a long video for ~10 minutes and each short averages ~10 seconds, it takes about 60 shorts for one viewer to spend the same time with you; the real number can be lower or higher; the point is it takes many shorts to equal the trust built by one long-form video.
- Learner-relevant: gives an intuition for why, done right, long-form is powerful; worded as a rough demo estimate.

### text-and-why-not-first

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Module 25 - Master the Channels/3-Module_25_-_Master_the_Channels.en.vtt#text-and-why-not-first]]`
- Summary: Explains why text-based content is the trickiest and not recommended as a starting point.
- Key claims: on X, scrollers mostly don't have buyer intent, far less than on Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube, because video/images are more powerful than text; newsletters have no algorithm to help new people find you — people usually find newsletters through someone's Instagram or YouTube content first; the author learned this when trying to start RoboNuggets as a newsletter.
- Learner-relevant: warns against investing in text/newsletters early if the goal is business leads.
