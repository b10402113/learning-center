# Digest — Module 11 - Idea Sources

## Overview (L1)

- This lesson introduces where good content ideas come from, opening with the reminder that a lot of the best ideas come from your own experience, and that agents can help you find even more.
- Its through-line is the **well of knowledge**: every niche has a place where the latest news lands first, and you should find yours in order to display expertise.
- Teaches that real-time sources like X, GitHub and official docs beat TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, which are "second-order" knowledge sources with a delay.
- Introduces the `/latest` skill, which checks the last 7 days across social media, Reddit and the web for fresh ideas (adjustable to 14 or 28 days).
- Provides install prompts and extra prompts for news alerts and finding your niche's sources.
- Learner outcome: identify the well of knowledge for your niche, run `/latest`, pick one idea, point your phone at the screen, and post it today.
- A `resources/` folder is attached (the `latest` skill: README, LICENSE, `skills/last30days`, `skills/latest`, and `robonuggets-latest.zip`); its code is digested separately and was not read for this digest.

### the-well-of-knowledge

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Wk2 - Content Ideation/5-Module 11 - Idea Sources/5-Module_11_-_Idea_Sources.en.vtt#the-well-of-knowledge]]`
- Summary: Defines the well of knowledge and why it matters for educational content.
- Key claims: The well of knowledge is where the latest information in your industry lands first; to get followership you must display expertise, and being updated with the most recent knowledge is one of the best ways; talking about outdated news (e.g. the 2023 GPT app store) loses confidence in you as an expert; the well of knowledge differs by industry (a forum, a group, a community).
- Learner-relevant: Gives the learner a concrete research target: find and track the first-landing source for their niche.

### real-time-sources-x-github-docs

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Wk2 - Content Ideation/5-Module 11 - Idea Sources/5-Module_11_-_Idea_Sources.en.vtt#real-time-sources-x-github-docs]]`
- Summary: Explains why X, GitHub and official documentation are earlier sources than short-form and video platforms.
- Key claims: For AI, X is a great source because it's all text, so news drops as a post first; bookmarking useful posts turns consumption time into an ideas-research phase, and Claude can go through your bookmarks to suggest ideas; GitHub is where code, plugins and software are published, and Star History is a trending list of most-starred repos checked about every two weeks; official Claude and OpenAI docs are good knowledge sources, and a topical video built from Claude's own Opus 5.5 prompting guide reached ~400,000 views because it was recent and high quality; TikTok/Instagram/YouTube are second-order knowledge because video takes longer to produce, so knowledge is delayed versus the source.
- Learner-relevant: Tells the learner where to look first and why, and models converting passive consumption into research.

### the-latest-skill

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Wk2 - Content Ideation/5-Module 11 - Idea Sources/index.html#the-latest-skill]]`
- Summary: Presents `/latest` as the module's installable skill and how it works.
- Key claims: `/latest` checks the last 7 days across social media, Reddit and the web for fresh ideas; typing `/latest` returns up to 10 ranked content ideas in 2 to 3 minutes, each with an angle and a source link, on a searchable/filterable page; the window is adjustable to 14 or 28 days when results aren't useful; install by sending the agent the provided zip prompt, then run `/latest` or name your niche (e.g. `/latest home gym equipment`).
- Learner-relevant: Provides the lesson's core tool and its tuning knobs for the learner's own niche.

### other-useful-prompts

- Locator: `[[sources/ai-content/20261015/3-Wk2 - Content Ideation/5-Module 11 - Idea Sources/index.html#other-useful-prompts]]`
- Summary: Supplies two additional ready-made prompts plus usage tips.
- Key claims: A "get alerts" prompt sets up a scheduled niche news alert (list sources, check twice a day at 8am and 5pm, 3-5 items each with one-line summary, why it matters, source link, and one postable idea, skipping repeats); a "find your sources" prompt researches the niche's well of knowledge across official sources, first posters, communities and tools/data, and marks 5 to check weekly; tips say if you get too few results go broader or use a 14/28-day window or add peer accounts to `/outliers`, go broad-to-narrow (AI, then local AI), and teach it your taste by adding liked ideas to the cart and pasting "Copy for my agent" back.
- Learner-relevant: Lowers the setup barrier with copy-paste prompts and troubleshooting for thin results.
