# L1 overview

- **Why evals now** — LLM products turn deterministic input→output testing into measuring a distribution; classic CI/CD assertions ("output == expected") break, and the questions become "what should happen, what must not happen, did it address the ask, what risk remains, can we ship." Skipped evals produce real incidents (deleted production DB, chatbot-invented refund policy).
- **Benchmarks ≠ product evals** — public model benchmarks (MMLU-style, leaderboard races) compare models, not your workflow; the instructor's CRM use-case benchmark compares models on accuracy, cost, speed, trust & safety *per use case* and runs in an automated pipeline.
- **Definition and loop** — eval = knowing whether the AI meets user expectations for task completion, safety, trust, and value. Treat eval as a loop of build → test (offline, batch, go/no-go) → monitor/observability (online, drift, hallucination), not a one-time gate; feeds recursive self-improvement.
- **Three-leg eval system** — (1) task / jobs-to-be-done, (2) dataset / test scenarios (golden set, production traces, adversarial red-team), (3) scorer (code, LLM-as-judge, human). Missing one leg leaves the eval incomplete.
- **Six quality dimensions + two metric buckets** — quality, behavior, safety, reliability, cost, latency; metrics split into AI metrics (hallucination rate, accuracy, faithfulness) and business/user metrics (CSAT, resolution/deflection rate, escalation rate). Thresholds are contextual: 50% acceptable for an internal Q&A agent, 90%+ for legal/finance with citations and deterministic checks.
- **Eval-driven development** — write the eval spec/rubric (outcome, experience, trajectory, governance, what the solution may and may not do) *before* building; it doubles as PRD and as GTM/sales evidence. Roles are shared: PM owns outcome and failure modes, AI engineer pulls traces and de-identifies data, architect/designer/data analyst contribute.
- **Error analysis and failure taxonomy** — sample ~40–50 sessions, read traces (not logs) end to end, annotate pass/fail plus notes, cluster into named, binary, specific, countable failure codes, then rank by frequency × severity × blast radius. Repo tooling (TraceWeaver trace viewer) and Excel-based annotation are both taught; thumbs-down feedback with reason categories is a high-value eval signal.
- **Running example: Pronto** — fictional 30-minute grocery store customer-support agent with order status, refunds, substitutions, warranty, escalation; rules include photo within 24h for perishables, 30-day non-perishable returns, and the **$50 rule** (any refund over $50 requires human approval). Week-1 project: instrument 30 traces, annotate all, build 5+ failure codes, report 3 findings + 1 anomaly.
