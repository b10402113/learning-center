# L1 overview

- **Week 1 agenda and outcomes** — Five blocks (why AI products need evals, fundamentals, error analysis, live build, Project 1) across 120 minutes; ten learner outcomes from "explain eval in plain English" to "write an eval-driven spec" and "build eval-driven prototypes fast."
- **Why the old QA playbook fails** — Traditional software has one right output; AI products have a output distribution. The replacement assumptions are: measure success rate over many runs, pass/fail on each of **six quality dimensions**, living judges and datasets, and error analysis from day one.
- **Six dimensions of AI product quality** — Model quality (faithfulness, factual accuracy, completeness, coherence) vs product behavior (right question, task completed, right tool); safety (prompt injection, PII, toxicity, unfairness); reliability (consistency across runs, graceful failure, no silent errors); cost (tokens × price, tool calls, retries, retrieval); latency (TTFT, total turn time, queue wait, tool latency).
- **Three building blocks** — Every eval is a task, a dataset, and a scorer. Datasets come from golden sets, production traffic, and red-team cases; scorers are code checks, LLM judges, or human review.
- **Tools in the course** — LangSmith (tracing/datasets/feedback weeks 1,4,5), DeepEval (LLM judges week 2), Promptfoo (red-team week 3), RAGAS (RAG metrics week 4), GitHub Actions (CI eval gate week 6); the method transfers to any stack.
- **Error analysis before automation** — Read traces, not logs: logs show timings and a soft warning, traces show which policy chunk was retrieved, what the model saw, and why the wrong refund happened. Three steps: source traces → annotate → cluster into a failure taxonomy.
- **Failure codes and prioritization** — Good codes are named, binary (pass/fail, no scale), specific (two people agree), and countable (frequency feeds triage). Priority = frequency × severity, with the coding-agent example showing 3 of 8 / 5 of 8 style counts (stale terminology, unverified status reporting, weak preview coverage).
- **Pronto running example and Project 1** — Fictional "Groceries in 30 minutes" store with explicit policy: perishables refunded only with a photo within 24h, 30-day non-perishable returns, delivery fee refunded only if >60 min late or Pronto-cancelled, **any refund over $50 needs human approval**, substitutions only with checkout opt-in, 1-year warranty on Pronto-branded appliances only, escalate legal/safety/health/other-customer-data requests; tools `get_order_status`, `issue_refund`, `lookup_policy`, `escalate_to_human`. Project: 30 inputs, annotate all, 5+ binary codes with frequency + quality dimension, top 3 findings and 1 saved anomaly.
