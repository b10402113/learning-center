# Digest — 企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节

## Overview (L1)

- Core competitiveness (核心竞争力) — Answers the programmer's most painful question in the AI era with a new measuring stick and a three-layer progression.
- What is no longer a moat — Tool fluency, years of experience, tech-stack depth, and big-company background have all become entry tickets defining your floor, not your ceiling (智力平权 / "intelligence parity").
- Data and leaders — Three credible datasets reveal an adoption-trust gap, real slowdowns, and squeezed junior roles; four respected practitioners show five common traits.
- Vibe coding vs agentic engineering — A crucial distinction between accepting AI output and encoding engineering judgment into AI's workflow.
- Three-layer competitiveness — Workflow design + deep engineering judgment + compound-asset accumulation.
- Open source and community — Open source is the best carrier of compound assets and its dividend is expanding; the Oryx community runs four real projects, each with a long-term growth entry.

## Sections (L2)

### no-longer-moat
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#no-longer-moat]]`
- Summary: Four things that used to be moats are now entry tickets: tool fluency (AI iterates faster than people learn), years of experience (a 3-year AI-fluent engineer can match an 8-year output), tech-stack depth (AI crosses stacks in days), and big-company background (judgment transfers faster).
- Key claims: none of these disappeared, but each now defines your lower bound; what remains valuable is the engineering intuition formed by real hard-won experience.
- Learner-relevant: Resets the learner's self-assessment; directs investment toward durable capabilities.

### data-and-leaders
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#data-and-leaders]]`
- Summary: Three data points (Stack Overflow 2025: 84% usage but trust fell from 40% to 29%, 66% spend more time fixing "almost right" code; METR 2025: developers thought 20% faster but were 19% slower; Stanford 2025: 22–25 developer employment down ~20% from peak) and what four leaders actually do (Karpathy, DHH, Simon Willison, Boris Cherny).
- Key claims: the industry is in a confused transition of "everyone on board, half-skeptical, unclear effect"; the leaders' five common traits are using it by hand, adjusting continuously, not selling answers, guarding engineering understanding, and wariness of hype.
- Learner-relevant: Evidence-based grounding that dissolves both hype and panic.

### vibe-vs-agentic
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#vibe-vs-agentic]]`
- Summary: Contrasts vibe coding (describe X, accept output, don't inspect) with agentic engineering (encode engineering judgment into AI's workflow using CLAUDE.md + Skills + Hooks + Plan Mode).
- Key claims: vibe coding suits personal projects/prototypes/low-risk internal tools but not production systems; vibe-coded PRs often miss edge cases, error paths, and exception logic; teams that lean heavily on AI see PR review time rise 91%; your ceiling is your ceiling of system understanding — AI doesn't understand the system for you.
- Learner-relevant: Defines the engineering posture the course trains and its hard prerequisite of judgment.

### three-layer-competitiveness
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#three-layer-competitiveness]]`
- Summary: A three-layer progression — (1) workflow design ability, (2) deep engineering judgment, (3) compound-asset accumulation ability — and three standards a true moat must satisfy: AI-hard-to-replicate, uniquely yours, continuously growing.
- Key claims: layers are progressive, not parallel; missing a lower layer makes the upper layer empty talk; only assets meeting all three standards are real moats.
- Learner-relevant: The central mental model for the learner's long-term positioning.

### layer1-workflow
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#layer1-workflow]]`
- Summary: Workflow design ability — designing a human+AI collaboration workflow that carries something from fuzzy requirement to production.
- Key claims: AI cannot design its own workflow; each person's workflow carries a personal fingerprint; the stronger the tools, the more usage differences dominate output; workflows are highly transferable across stacks (understand → constrain → verify → compound).
- Learner-relevant: The first and most immediately actionable layer; explains why SDD tools and skill repos keep appearing.

### layer2-judgment
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#layer2-judgment]]`
- Summary: Deep engineering judgment — making the right technical call at key nodes (choose A or B? ship to production? surface bug or underlying problem?).
- Key claims: judgment depends on concrete context and real hard-won experience AI cannot fully access; AI makes "doing" faster so "doing the right thing" becomes the bottleneck; judgment carries personal style and grows project by project (what matters is how many calls you've made and your hit rate).
- Learner-relevant: Explains why the course prizes judgment over operation.

### layer3-compound-assets
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#layer3-compound-assets]]`
- Summary: Compound-asset accumulation — local assets (CLAUDE.md, skill library, slash commands, hooks, scaffolding), semi-public assets (internal articles, pitfall notes, decision records), public assets (blogs, open-source contributions, PR records), and identity assets (GitHub profile, tech IP, community influence).
- Key claims: completing a project ≠ accumulating an asset; AI greatly accelerates accumulation but won't produce assets for you; the most valuable is public + identity combined = open-source contribution; the only standard is "next time the starting point is higher."
- Learner-relevant: The most overlooked but highest-return layer; a concrete list to build.

### open-source
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#open-source]]`
- Summary: Open source is the best carrier of compound assets, with four benefit levels (capability, identity, network, compounding) and four reasons its dividend is expanding in the AI era.
- Key claims: GitHub is a public résumé whose commits are verifiable; the real value is identity/network/compounding which a company can't give; AI lowers the barrier, raises the "含金量" (only rigorously reviewed code counts), speeds up compounding, and makes doers faster to identify.
- Learner-relevant: Motivates the advanced-layer open-source direction.

### four-suggestions
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#four-suggestions]]`
- Summary: Four practical suggestions: run the full workflow on every project without shortcuts; ask "why" at every key decision and record it; after each project ask what was compounded; proactively pick an open-source project.
- Key claims: don't start from writing code but from clarify + write spec, and don't stop at "code done"; judgments without real hard-won experience are knowledge, not judgment; the biggest barrier is always the first step.
- Learner-relevant: Turns the three-layer model into daily behaviors.

### business-vs-ai
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#business-vs-ai]]`
- Summary: Business development and AI mastery are both required, not a choice: business understanding is the root of judgment, AI mastery is the lever that amplifies it.
- Key claims: your vibe-coding ceiling is your business-understanding ceiling; without deep business experience it's hard to write a valuable spec; AI doesn't know your company's real constraints and historical baggage.
- Learner-relevant: Guards against the false trade-off of abandoning business depth for tooling.

### daily-five
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#daily-five]]`
- Summary: Five daily things: spend half an hour using AI in unimportant projects; find someone half a step ahead and ask how they do it; don't wait until you see clearly to start; guard your understanding of engineering and don't cede it to AI; accept that the anxiety period is normal.
- Key claims: all content is worth less than using it once by hand — consistency beats occasional; the alternative to us isn't AI, it's people who use AI better.
- Learner-relevant: Concrete, low-friction starting actions.

### oryx-community
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/企业级 AI 编程先导课程 - 第二节.pdf#oryx-community]]`
- Summary: Why the Oryx community exists: courses end and people disperse, but programmers grow by long-term practice with others; it runs four real projects, and your real competitor is the person next to you.

- Learner-relevant: Provides the long-term environment and social scaffolding for the course.
