# Digest — 03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成

## Overview (L1)

- Theme: how capability forms when struggle is erased — If AI flattens the "struggle" through which older programmers grew, how do the young build ability and how do veterans keep their feel and judgment? Integrates 2026 research (including Anthropic's RCT) and cognitive science.
- Why a separate essay — The prior "AI-era Software Engineering" assumes engineers can make higher-dimensional judgments; but if the formation mechanism breaks, that whole hope is built on air. Older engineers grew ability in an environment forcing line-by-line coding and bug-wrestling; AI makes struggle optional, and under time/KPI pressure most skip it.
- Anthropic RCT — 52 (mostly junior) engineers unfamiliar with the Trio async library: AI group scored 50% vs. handwritten 67% (d=0.738, p=0.01) while the AI speed-up (~2 min) was not significant; the widest gap was debugging — the exact capability needed to supervise AI code. But the deeper finding: usage pattern decides outcome; six interaction modes, three destroy learning (AI delegation, progressive dependence, iterative AI debugging) and three preserve it (generate-then-comprehend, hybrid code-explanation, conceptual questioning). "Generate-then-comprehend" and "AI delegation" are operationally identical except the former checks its understanding afterward.
- Struggle itself is learning — The handwritten group hit more errors and, by independently solving them, built debugging ability; errors and being stuck are where learning happens; AI's danger is not writing wrong code but being so smooth it erases being stuck.
- Theory base — Desirable difficulties (Bjork): harder learning yields stronger long-term memory/transfer; cognitive offloading (Microsoft 2025) and neural "cognitive debt" (Kosmyna 2025); but the topic is not closed — methodological critiques note longitudinal evidence is absent and "cognitive offloading" is overused; Anthropic admits its limits (n=52, immediate test only).
- Macro: apprenticeship collapse — Traditional chain junior → error → struggle-debug → docs → ask senior → deep understanding → repeated thousands of times → senior is broken; new path gives surface familiarity without deep knowledge; a 5–7 year time bomb means 2024–2026 AI-taught juniors become 2029–2032 seniors when the whole-industry gap hits; junior roles down ~40% vs. 2022 peak; Ericsson: expertise needs struggle, sacrifice, and painful self-assessment.
- Young programmers — Make AI a teacher not a ghostwriter; deliberately retain the right to get stuck; migrate capability toward what AI can't take over (debugging broken systems, API design, DB internals, regulated-domain expertise); confront the paradox that the "boring foundational work" bridge is being dismantled.
- Veteran programmers — Kent Beck as a live sample: never delegate understanding; proactively slow down (let Gemini slow so he can learn); guard design (the genie "eats the seed corn"; AI "lacks taste"); decouple understanding/judgment from the act of writing and guard it separately.
- Organizations — Rebuild the apprenticeship: Revelry's "must explain before merge" training wheels; protect mentorship by locking it in the calendar; AI should augment not replace mentorship; a dangerous divergence between big firms and startups.
- Closing judgment — AI erased struggle, which is the raw material of capability formation; struggle moved from "imposed by environment" to "must be deliberately designed". The mechanism didn't vanish — it went from automatic to deliberate; slow is genuinely fast here.

### anthropic-rct

- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成.html#anthropic-rct]]`
- Summary: Details the Anthropic RCT (Shen & Tamkin, 2026) with the 52-engineer Trio experiment, the 50% vs. 67% result, and the six interaction modes.
- Key claims: AI group scored ~2 grade levels lower (d=0.738, p=0.01) with no significant speed gain; the largest deficit is debugging; three learning-destroying modes (AI delegation, progressive dependence, iterative AI debugging) score <40%, three learning-preserving modes (generate-then-comprehend, hybrid code-explanation, conceptual questioning) score >65%; "generate-then-comprehend" differs from "AI delegation" only in checking understanding afterward.
- Learner relevant: The hardest empirical evidence on how AI usage patterns shape skill retention.

### struggle-is-learning

- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成.html#struggle-is-learning]]`
- Summary: The counterintuitive finding that the handwritten group's extra errors — especially Trio-related — were exactly how they built debugging ability; errors and stuckness are where learning happens.
- Key claims: AI's scariness is being so smooth it erases being stuck, and with it the growth hidden inside; mainstream tools already ship Learning/Explanatory modes to preserve understanding; "cognitive effort, even painfully being stuck, is likely a necessary condition for mastery".
- Learner-relevant: Reframes errors and being stuck as the mechanism of growth, not obstacles.

### theory-base

- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成.html#theory-base]]`
- Summary: Grounds the issue in cognitive science: desirable difficulties (Bjork), cognitive offloading and Microsoft's critical-thinking finding, neural "cognitive debt" (Kosmyna), and a candid counter-side.
- Key claims: Making learning harder can yield stronger long-term memory and transfer; daily AI users self-report lower critical-thinking demand; cognitive debt exists at both engineering (ununderstood code) and neural (habitual offloading) levels; but longitudinal evidence is absent and the effect's persistence as engineers gain skill is an open question; treat "struggle erasure harms ability" as strongly supported but not yet proven law.
- Learner-relevant: The theoretical foundation plus the honest caveats.

### apprenticeship-collapse

- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成.html#apprenticeship-collapse]]`
- Summary: Zooms out to the collapse of the apprenticeship model: the traditional growth chain is broken, creating a 5–7 year time bomb and an industry-wide senior gap.
- Key claims: Traditional 3–4 years to mid, 5–7 to senior means 2024–2026 AI-taught juniors reach seniority in 2029–2032 when the gap bites; the gap can't be solved by poaching since the whole pipeline has the same problem; junior roles down ~40% vs. 2022 with lower pay, higher experience bars, and LLM-detection take-homes; Ericsson: expertise needs struggle, sacrifice, and honest, often painful self-assessment; AI's selling point (removing pain) directly opposes expertise formation.
- Learner-relevant: The macro consequence that makes this a structural, not personal, problem.

### young-programmers

- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成.html#young-programmers]]`
- Summary: Actionable principles for young programmers: treat AI as teacher not ghostwriter, deliberately retain being-stuck, migrate toward what AI can't take over, and confront the paradox.
- Key claims: After AI generates code, ask why, what if X, why this API design (the "cognitive mirror"); leave time to be stuck before asking; focus on debugging broken systems, API design, DB internals, system startup, and regulated-domain expertise (HIPAA/PCI-DSS) which AI can't certify or be legally responsible for; the old bridge of repetitive foundational work is being dismantled, so one must design a new path deliberately.
- Learner-relevant: Concrete habits for the learner to build capability despite AI.

### veteran-programmers

- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成.html#veteran-programmers]]`
- Summary: Using Kent Beck as a live sample, lays out how veterans keep feel: never delegate understanding, proactively slow down, guard design, and decouple judgment from writing.
- Key claims: Beck watches the genie's intermediate results and intervenes, checks for cheating (disabling/deleting tests); augmented coding's value standard matches handwriting — clean, working code, just not hand-typed; he deliberately slows AI so he can learn from what it just built; the genie "eats the seed corn" via bad design and AI "lacks taste"; the veteran's "feel" migrates from typing muscle memory to a nose for complexity and design.
- Learner-relevant: The veteran's playbook for keeping judgment while using AI heavily.

### organizations

- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/03-15 15. 扩展篇：AI 时代，程序员的能力还怎么养成.html#organizations]]`
- Summary: Argues apprenticeship collapse is structural and needs organizational rebuilding, citing Revelry's "training wheels" and the need to protect mentorship.

- Learner-relevant: The organizational counterpart to individual effort.

### closing-judgment
