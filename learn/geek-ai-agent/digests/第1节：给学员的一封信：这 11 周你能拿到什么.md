# Digest — 第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么

## Overview (L1)

- Opening letter — Why the course is called "enterprise-grade" and what the learner can actually take away from 11 weeks.
- Why "enterprise-grade" — The positioning is to become an engineer who can do Engineering, not merely a Vibe Coding user: complex business requirements, complete engineering delivery, real team environment.
- The evolution of AI coding — Four successive stations (Vibe Coding → Context Engineering → Harness Engineering → Loop Engineering), each an extension, not a replacement.
- Two categories — Vibe Coding vs Engineering are not the same; doing Engineering people can use Vibe Coding, but Vibe-Coding-only people can't do Engineering.
- Conclusion and deliverables — Become a better Engineering practitioner; three concrete takeaways, materials provided, and four learning principles.

## Sections (L2)

### why-enterprise
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么.pdf#why-enterprise]]`
- Summary: "Enterprise-grade" is a positioning, not a gimmick: we want to be engineers who can do Engineering rather than only Vibe Coding.
- Key claims: the work involves complex business needs, complete engineering delivery, and real team environments — designing/transforming systems, performance and risk, team alignment, requirements analysis, rapid launches and online incident response, code quality, multi-system integration, continuous iteration; AI is a tool, you are the decision-maker.
- Learner-relevant: Sets the identity and standard the whole course aims for.

### purpose
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么.pdf#purpose]]`
- Summary: Two driving questions (What can you get after the course? What can I, a decade-plus hands-on programmer, genuinely teach?) plus two underlying ones (Will this profession persist? If I were starting AI coding today, how would I learn?).
- Key claims: the course is designed from a pragmatic "what's in your hands, what can you do, what can go on your résumé" perspective.
- Learner-relevant: Frames outcome orientation rather than content orientation.

### evolution
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么.pdf#evolution]]`
- Summary: AI coding is evolving along a line of four stations: Vibe Coding (prompting and collaborating to ship without deep programming), Context Engineering (managing context so AI works within the right information boundary), Harness Engineering (test suites and constraint frameworks to verify AI output), Loop Engineering (managing AI's ReAct loop, control granularity, checkpoints, rollback).
- Key claims: most people's impression is stuck at station one — the biggest misunderstanding; the course focuses on Harness + Loop Engineering; each station extends rather than replaces the previous, so engineering-capable people can drive every station.
- Learner-relevant: The evolutionary frame for positioning current skills and where to aim.

### two-categories
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么.pdf#two-categories]]`
- Summary: Vibe Coding (no need to understand programming; simple single-product needs; no long-term maintenance; "works is enough"; AI is the lever, you are the designer; no need for implementation details) vs Engineering (complex business: design, decomposition, implementation, risk assessment, multi-system collaboration; full lifecycle; performance, team, launch; needs solid programming foundations; AI is a tool, you are the decision-maker).
- Key claims: the relationship is one-directional and irreversible — Engineering people use Vibe Coding, but Vibe-Coding-only people cannot do Engineering.
- Learner-relevant: Prevents the common conflation and clarifies the learning target.

### conclusion
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么.pdf#conclusion]]`
- Summary: The conclusion is to become a better Engineering practitioner, backed by two arguments: this field is always a mastery/practice game (AI coding isn't inherently complex — whoever spends more time wins), and time × practice = capability (fundamentals can't be skipped; fundamentals × AI = 10x leverage).
- Key claims: those who say "AI will replace programmers" are usually Engineering experts themselves, which is telling; computer fundamentals, network, OS, storage, caching, and databases still matter.
- Learner-relevant: Reframes effort toward deliberate practice over fundamentals.

### deliverables
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么.pdf#deliverables]]`
- Summary: Three concrete takeaways: (1) truly master AI coding and demystify it — knowing its essence, theory, and tools, with a real from-zero experience in a large project; (2) have something for the résumé — OryxOS is a technically substantive, in-demand direction, and a good open-source participation record matters; (3) develop good engineer habits — most programmers don't lack will but don't know what to do, using OryxOS as an anchor.
- Key claims: the takeaways are tangible, not vague "growth."
- Learner-relevant: Defines success criteria for the 11 weeks.

### materials

### how-to-learn
- Locator: `[[sources/geek-ai-agent/20260922/第1节：给学员的一封信：这 11 周你能拿到什么.pdf#how-to-learn]]`
- Summary: Four principles: preview and learn early (read cognition/extension/base lessons and live streams, build systematic cognition); find your purpose and confirm your investment (decide Vibe Coding vs Engineering); communicate continuously (recorded lessons can't cover everything — talk offline); practice and invest long term (projects are large; especially find those genuinely interested in OryxOS to build long term).
- Key claims: what to learn is the course's business; how to learn is your own; growth takes time.
- Learner-relevant: Concrete study-habit guidance.
