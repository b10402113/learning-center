# L1 overview

- Connects the two root causes to a deeper wrong mental model: treating the agent like a genie whose wish is rewritten whenever the output misses.
- Offers the correct model: the human is the architect and the agent is the engine; you set constraints, make real decisions, and define what good output is.
- Describes the AI as fast, tireless, and prolific but without judgment, likening the relationship to a senior directing a junior.
- Quantifies direction: an undirected instruction yields about 20 silent decisions and a 300-line diff, while a scoped task yields a reviewable 40-line diff.
- Frames the agent as a multiplier that amplifies vague direction into a mess and clear direction into working software.
- Argues developers who merely hope are replaceable, while those who turn fuzzy problems into clear executable direction become more valuable.
- Ends with the habit of catching yourself hoping and asking what a senior engineer would do.
