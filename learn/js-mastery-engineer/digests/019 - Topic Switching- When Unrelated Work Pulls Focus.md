# L1 overview

- Besides volume and position, unrelated material competing for attention can pull even a half-empty context off track.
- In a controlled test, a model answering correctly 100% of the time dropped to around 75% once eight unrelated topics were folded into one growing conversation.
- Research names this "contextual distraction": irrelevant earlier material measurably pulls attention away from the current question, even when it is topically related.
- A single session drifting across a bug fix, design discussion, unrelated feature, and back again is worse than several short focused sessions.
- Day-to-day symptoms are repeating rejected suggestions, forgetting settled decisions, and contradicting earlier answers.
- The model is not less capable late in a session; the ratio of competing to relevant material has changed.
- The next lesson moves to choosing between clearing and compacting.
