# Digest — UX/UI Design Mastery Course (part 5: UX Design Process)

## Overview (L1)

- 1. Design Thinking Process — Introduces design thinking as a people-first problem-solving mindset: understand users' needs and frustrations, then design solutions that work for them rather than for the designer. Frames the process as five iterative stages (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test) that you can loop back through, and stresses cross-functional collaboration. Grounds the idea in Google's interdisciplinary teams and Airbnb's near-bankruptcy turnaround, and closes on failure as a stepping stone.
- 2. Stages of the Design Thinking Process — Walks each of the five stages in order with techniques and tips. Empathize (interviews, surveys, user/market/competitive research; avoid confirmation bias; distinguish stated vs. real needs — Henry Ford's "faster horses"); define (synthesize research into personas and a problem statement); ideate (quantity over premature judgment, brainstorming, worst-idea exercise, brainwriting, "how might we" questions); prototype (quick mid-to-high fidelity, not pixel-perfect, iterating via Figma); test (user interviews, focus groups, usability testing with a prepared script, no leading the user).

## Sections (L2)

### 1. Design Thinking Process

- Locator: `[[sources/ui-ux-design/20261007/ui1/5. UX Design Process/1. Design Thinking Process.en.srt#design-thinking-process]]`
- Summary: Defines design thinking as a user-centered mindset rather than a rigid process: it is about what works for users, not what the designer assumes works. It names the five stages (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test), illustrates them with a cake-baking analogy, and emphasizes flexibility — you may circle back when testing exposes problems. Collaboration across disciplines is presented as a crucial component.
- Key claims: design thinking is about understanding and solving user problems, not creating what designers think is cool; the five stages are empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test, and they are flexible and iterative; diverse cross-functional teams (engineers, designers, business strategists, psychologists) produce richer, more robust solutions; failure is a stepping stone to success, and the mindset requires empathy, creativity, and resilience.
- Learner-relevant: Establishes the mental model for the whole UX process — user-centered, iterative, and collaborative — anchored by Google and Airbnb examples.

### 2. Stages of the Design Thinking Process

- Locator: `[[sources/ui-ux-design/20261007/ui1/5. UX Design Process/2. Stages of the Design Thinking Process.en.srt#stages-of-the-design-thinking-process]]`
- Summary: A stage-by-stage guide to running the design thinking process, pairing each stage with practical techniques. Empathize gathers deep user insight via interviews, surveys, and user/market/competitive research; define synthesizes findings into personas and a problem statement; ideate generates volume and variety of ideas; prototype builds quick testable designs; test validates with real users before development.
- Key claims: empathize is the foundation of user-centric design and should avoid confirmation bias, gathering many perspectives and distinguishing what people say they want from what they actually need (Ford's "faster horses"); define turns research into user personas and a clear problem statement that focuses solution-finding; ideate is a judgment-free "free speech zone" using brainstorming, sketching, worst-idea, brainwriting, and "how might we" questions, always keeping user needs in mind; prototypes should be detailed enough to test but not pixel-perfect (e.g. in Figma), iterating between prototype and test; testing should let users explore unaided, use open-ended questions, and follow a pre-written usability test script.
- Learner-relevant: Gives an actionable playbook for each stage plus concrete methods (personas, problem statements, ideation exercises, prototyping, usability testing) that feed directly into later UX/UI work.
