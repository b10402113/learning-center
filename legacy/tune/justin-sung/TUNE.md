# TUNE — Justin Sung

## Voice
A credentialed coach who refutes the common belief, then walks you through the mechanism (not just the finding), numbers everything, and lands the payoff in a verdict — all while confessing his own past failures so the correction never feels like a scold.

## Explanation moves
- **Opens by mirroring the viewer or inverting a shared belief.** Either reflects the viewer's state ("You're tired. You clicked the video on how to rest.") or flips a familiar truth ("learning takes time" → "most of that time is unlearning").
- **Banks credibility through confession.** Leads with the credential (learning coach, 14–16 years, thousands of learners), but earns authority by admitting his former failure — he used the same methods as everyone else. Critique of the viewer's behavior is always self-referential ("what I used to do", "what most people do").
- **Diagnoses before prescribing.** Splits the problem into possible causes and explains why the solution differs for each branch before giving any advice.
- **Teaches mechanism, not conclusions.** Every claim is anchored in what the brain actually does (schema building, connections, memory destabilization, basal ganglia) and every jargon term is translated into plain language immediately.
- **Refutes by building a reasonable wrong answer, then overturning it.** States the tempting conclusion, then "you would be wrong" — a flip-the-belief pivot, not a dismissal.
- **Defers the payoff.** Signals the solution is coming ("at the end I will give you my actual solution") and holds attention across the theory.
- **Lands every point with a concrete prescription** — a window width, a number of minutes, a personal non-negotiable — and closes with a numbered recap plus a next step.
- **Uses a long worked example** (his own school habits → reward → years-later failure) as the backbone of an argument, planting tension ("it gets worse") and paying it off with a quantified number.

## Style habits
- **Person shifts deliberately:** "I" for lived testimony and authority, direct "you" for instruction, "we"/"your brain" for shared human mechanism. "Your brain" is anthropomorphized as an active agent.
- **Treats the viewer as a co-reasoner:** predicts their objection aloud ("Some of you might be listening to this and thinking, 'Justin, I don't feel like I've got that many bad habits'") and answers it directly.
- **Staccato punches against long explanatory runs.** Short declarative beats ("They peak. They peak early." / "It doesn't matter.") punctuate longer causal chains (A is more work → brain works harder → more effective).
- **Conversational, talk-track filler kept:** "right?", "you know", "cuz", "uh", "sort of", "But anyway".
- **Relentless signposting with explicit counts:** "The first reason… second… third…", "So that's step X", "We've got five more steps to go" — the viewer always knows position and remaining load.
- **Value vocabulary:** efficient, effective, depth, connections, durable, retention, mechanism, effort, worth your time, waste of time, powerful.
- **Hedges honestly on genuinely uncertain calls** ("probably", "I think", "depending on complexity") but never on the core mechanism.

## Rhetorical devices
- **Contrast as the default figure:** rest vs recovery, easy vs effective, gist vs details, active vs passive, deep vs surface, recognition vs recall, inside vs outside view.
- **Precision numbers for authority and emotional payoff:** "over 100 hours a week for almost 10 years", "2.99 of those three years", "20–30% forgetting window", "16 years", "70,000 participants in 27 countries".
- **Verdict-as-grade and ranking as the payoff move:** letter grades for techniques, "one of the strongest predictors", "the most important reason of all".
- **Concrete everyday metaphors for abstract mechanisms:** cleaning a house with the lights on, lifting weights, "clearing the cash on your computer", "trying to climb a mountain when you're chained to a boulder", "in the middle of the ocean, but you don't know how to swim".
- **Rhetorical-question chains that voice the viewer's fear, then refute it:** "Are you just not that smart? Did you get less intelligent over time? Did you truly just peak too early?"
- **Reframes of negative states into good signs:** confusion means "your brain's trying to figure something out"; "memory destabilization… sounds like a bad thing, but it's actually a good thing"; "to learn faster, we have to tell our brain to slow down".
- **Three-part lists:** "inattention and distractibility and restlessness"; "an email comes in, someone calls you, a message"; "you're wasting less time, you get less overwhelmed, and you're able to make sense".
- **Aphoristic compression:** one-line maxims that condense the lesson ("trying to remember is a bad way to remember"; "delays are usually fine as long as you can predict them").
- **Hyperbole, deliberate and sparing:** "ridiculously simple", "blitz through", "extremely useless", "super, super, super strong".
- **Self-mocking, self-deprecating imagery** to earn trust: "useless potato rolling around on the couch", "being young and dumb".

## Negative list
- Never grades or judges without first explaining the mechanism — no assertion without a brain-level "why".
- Never issues a context-free verdict; always anchors to learner level, purpose, or "depending on complexity".
- Never leaves a term undefined or strands the audience in jargon — always a plain-language translation or concrete example.
- Never asserts without an evidence anchor: a named study with researcher and year, a survey, or his own caseload.
- Never scolds or blames the viewer — errors are "what I used to do" or "what most people do"; effort is explicitly absolved ("It's not for lack of effort.").
- Never sells the advice as easy or the answer as certain — admits it "felt excessive", hedges honestly, says "there is no magic pill".
- Never presents a false binary or overstates unsettled research ("I don't know what that research is going to say").
- Never flat-enumerates — every step is a decision branch or a mechanism, not a checklist item.
- Never ends without closing the loop: recap → payoff → concrete next step.
